# Position card: Adam Bly

## 1. Role at summit and bio

**Role:** Participant in Session 3, "Knowledge Infrastructure and Tools: Commons, Enclosure, and Sovereignty" (with Adrian Johns, Benjamin Bratton, Jason Priem and David Weinberger). He is the only S3 participant running a venture-backed company that uses AI to turn the scholarly record into a proprietary-plus-public knowledge graph. That makes him both a builder the concept note would welcome and a live test case for its enclosure worries.

**Bio:** Founder and CEO of **System**, a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation founded in 2017–18 whose purpose is "to relate everything, to help the world see and solve anything, as a system." It builds the **System Graph**, an AI-extracted graph whose atomic unit is "a scientifically-established finding." The company now leans heavily toward healthcare ("System Health", clinical decision support). Before System he was VP of data at Spotify (2015–17), after Spotify acquired his firm Seed Scientific. Earlier he founded *Seed* magazine (2001–2012), ScienceBlogs and Visualizing.org. He was a Harvard Kennedy School STS visiting fellow and a WEF Young Global Leader, and he edited *Science Is Culture*. System's advisors and board include Amy Brand (advisor), Katherine Maher (board), Danny Hillis (advisor) and Tom Glocer (board).

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Generative AI threatens the "epistemological foundations of society." His fix is scientific method plus provenance.** "the public (and our regulatory agencies) needs an audit-trail back to underlying data sources, methodologies, and prompts." (`adam-bly/by/2023--ai-muddying-the-truth-globe.md`, 2023, with Amy Brand).
- **Chatbots violate scientific standards because they have no grounded model of the world.** A chatbot is "trained to sound authoritative but has no actual observed, experienced, or measured model of the world to align with" (Globe, 2023).
- **Build "AI done right": extract findings from trusted literature and cite them.** "We could train AI systems on source material that adheres to society's highest standards of trust, such as peer-reviewed scientific literature, corrected for retractions." (Globe, 2023). System has done this since 2022: "System now continuously auto-extracts statistical relationships from peer-reviewed scientific papers" using GPT-3 (`adam-bly/by/2022--scaling-system-with-gpt-3.md`, 2022).
- **Society, not corporations, decides AI's path. Evidence-based decision-making should be a governance principle.** "that should be society's assessment to make — not a handful of corporations" and "Evidence-based decision making should immediately become a principle of nascent international AI governance efforts" (Globe, 2023). He also wants the Office of Technology Assessment revived (`adam-bly/by/2023--this-is-the-help-congress-needs-to-regulate-ai.md`, excerpt; `…/2018--congress-needs-science-tech-advice.md`).
- **Today's AI stack reflects Big Tech's conditions, not science's or society's.** Current AI is "optimized for closed fast feedback loops where nearly everything can be A/B tested, generally unconcerned by black boxes," but "AI should look and work the way society wants and needs it to look and work" (`adam-bly/by/2019--thought-experiment-ai-infrastructure.md`, 2019). On opacity: "We are designing a world that we may not be able to interpret, let alone govern." (`adam-bly/by/2017--why-im-starting-a-new-ai-company.md`, 2017).
- **Trust in institutions, science included, has cracked. He admits his own earlier trust reflected privilege.** He writes that the "irresponsibly rapid rollout of generative AI by big tech is eroding the epistemological foundations of society." Also: "Science, too, is broken." (citing the record 10,000+ retractions in 2023). And: "I've benefited from these institutions because I look like the men who built them." (`adam-bly/by/2024--all-the-institutions-crumbling.md`, 2024).
- **Knowledge should be organized as one system, by statistical relationships rather than semantic silos.** He wants "A world where considering the whole, accessing context, is just as easy as accessing content." And: "An architecture based on systems not on silos, only possible today because of AI." (`adam-bly/by/2026--system-purpose-relate-everything.md`, 2026). He calls this a "generations-long project" meant to power "every search, every LLM" (`adam-bly/av/2025--visualizing-systems-24-hour-envisioning-intelligences-event.md`, 2025, ASR).
- **The charter promises open knowledge, but release terms are hybrid.** The PBC charter promises to "Maintain a free, open, and collaborative knowledge base of the world's systems" and to "Share the statistical metadata… in an open and interoperable format, and actively support open data and open science," funded by enterprise licenses (`adam-bly/by/2026--system-charter.md`). The 2026 **System Public Graph** is an annual snapshot "derived only from open, publicly available sources," under **CC-BY-NC 4.0**, built "with a patented approach" (`adam-bly/by/2026--system-public-graph-dataset.md`). In 2025 he described it as "contributing it into the commons… public knowledge infrastructure," alongside "enterprise-grade versions of the graph" (Envisioning Intelligences, 2025). In 2022, System content was CC BY-SA "Like Wikipedia" (`adam-bly/by/2022--announcing-public-beta-of-system.md`). The license has drifted from share-alike to non-commercial.
- **Earlier on, he held radical open-science and anti-publisher views.** "Let us just take it out of the hands of publishers." "Peer review should not be done for commercial gain," though he said it is "absolutely critical" and "must be protected" (`adam-bly/by/2012--science-re-imagined-nasig.md`, 2012 NASIG keynote). He predicted that "closed" would become the marked exception.
- **He is pro-interpretability and pro-globalism, and anti-fragmentation.** "Our values will be ardently pro-globalism and pro-interpretability… Our culture will be pro-open source." (2017).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Endorses:**
- AI accelerating a pre-existing crisis, and the erosion of trust in science. He names the replication and retraction crisis himself (2024).
- The "intelligence" critique, partly: chatbots "sound authoritative" without a world model (Globe 2023).
- Concentration of power: "not a handful of corporations" (2023).
- "Invitation to build" and "AI… could also help us fix them." This is exactly System's pitch (2022 GPT-3 post; 2025 talk).
- The deterministic-check observation. He anticipated it: Big Tech AI grew up in "closed fast feedback loops where nearly everything can be A/B tested," unlike health and science (2019). He'd cite this as prior art.
- Scholars as trusted source. His grounding layer depends on "verified sources like peer-reviewed studies and expert-curated databases" (2026 Public Graph).

**Pushes back or nuances:**
- **Enclosure and extraction.** The note warns that the "surveillance logic… now eyes the scholarly record as raw material." System itself mines the literature with AI, patents the method, licenses enterprise versions and releases a *non-commercial* public snapshot. He'd frame this as a PBC commons model funded by enterprise revenue ("Prioritize using the profits… to run System.com", charter). He would resist a manifesto that treats all AI extraction of the record as enclosure.
- **"Statistical model" framing.** He'd accept it for chatbots but argue that *grounded*, provenance-preserving AI extraction is a different category.
- **Publishers as "ultimate annotators."** His 2012 stance put peer review "out of the hands of publishers." The corpus has nothing recent on publishers' roles, and his advisory tie to Amy Brand (MIT Press) suggests he has softened.
- **Sovereignty.** He is instinctively globalist and multilateral (2017, 2024). He'd be wary of national or data-sovereignty framings that fragment knowledge.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Harvest claims (Session 3):**
1. Knowledge infrastructure for the AI era should organize *findings with provenance* (study, population, effect size, DOI), not documents. Every claim should carry an audit trail back to evidence.
2. Scholarly-grounded AI must be trained or grounded on the vetted, retraction-corrected literature and must cite its sources. That makes the integrity of the scholarly record (retractions, metadata) critical infrastructure.
3. The commons can be sustained by PBC or hybrid models: a free public snapshot plus paid enterprise tiers, bound by charter commitments and annual public-benefit reports.
4. AI governance needs institutional science advice, meaning a revived OTA, and evidence-based decision-making as a principle.

**Session 5 interventions:**
1. An open provenance and "finding-level" metadata standard, so machine-readable claims link to DOIs and populations and carry retraction status (building on System's Retraction Watch integration, `adam-bly/system-blog-index.tsv` 2023-10-08).
2. Public funding for open knowledge-graph infrastructure, following the Wellcome program-related investment in System (`system-blog-index.tsv` 2025-12-16; not Bly-bylined).
3. Revive Congressional technology assessment (OTA) as part of the governance roadmap.
