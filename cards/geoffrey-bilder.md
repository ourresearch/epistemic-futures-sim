**Bio:** Bilder spent eighteen years at Crossref (2007 to about 2025) as Director of Strategic Initiatives and later Technology and Research. He led CrossCheck/Similarity Check, Crossmark, the Open Funder Registry and ORCID's early technical build. With Neylon and Lin he co-wrote the **Principles of Open Scholarly Infrastructure (POSI, 2015)**. He left Crossref, joined MIT/The MIT Press around October 2025 (title unknown), and lives in Nîmes. He is a technologist, not an academic, and still writes code and tests AI coding tools himself. His long-running themes are trust signals, provenance metadata, persistent identifiers, and stopping infrastructure from being enclosed ("fauxpen"). Since 2024 he has written a steady stream of sardonic, skeptical posts on "AI".

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Say "machine learning", not "AI". The term itself assumes the conclusion.** "the very term “artificial intelligence” frames and shapes how people think about the field by assuming answers to some of the most controversial philosophical and practical debates" (`geoffrey-bilder/by/2024--on-ai.md`, 2024; written for the Crossref board in Nov 2023).
- **LLMs are statistical models that echo their authors' intentions and have no intentions of their own.** "conflating “AI” and “LLMs” anthropomorphizes what is actually an enormous statistical model" … "they are parroting the intentionality of the people who wrote the content" (`2024--on-ai.md`, 2024).
- **AI detection by content analysis won't work, and it mixes up two separate problems: credit and reliability.** "any approach to detecting ML that relies *primarily on analyzing the content itself* will not likely work- at least not for long" (`2024--on-ai.md`, 2024). He wants to separate "Accurately crediting the creators … regardless of whether humans or machines generate them" from "distinguishing between reliable and unreliable research outputs."
- **The root cause is using publication as a proxy for productivity. AI may finally force evaluation to be decoupled from it.** "the intractability of the problem may be the final straw that pushes research institutions to decouple career evaluation from publication" (`2024--on-ai.md`, 2024).
- **Context over content: trust will come from the provenance network (metadata), not the text.** "Advances in our ability to identify reliable research outputs will be driven by context analysis, not content analysis." "LLMs don’t have affiliations, co-authors, awards, preprints, funders, grants, or registered clinical trials." (`2024--on-ai.md`, 2024). Earlier version of the same idea: "the worst way on earth to gain trust is to say, "Trust me."" (`geoffrey-bilder/av/2018--geoffrey-bilder-crossref-metadata-as-a-signal-of-trust.md`, 2018).
- **The scholarly record is the curated corpus that AI will need once the web becomes a "data swamp".** "our community has a massive collection of curated content and context. We call it “the scholarly record.”" He adds that "LLMs rapidly degrades if they are trained on ML-generated content" (`2024--on-ai.md`, 2024). This is the source of the concept note's model-collapse and "annotators" ideas.
- **The realistic AI threat is quiet contamination, not AGI.** "the most realistic threat from AI — not that we will all soon be replaced by AGI, but that more and more of our systems are going to become infested with subtly buggy data and code" … "we’re not going to be able to use AI tools to help us do it" (`2025--bots-behaving-badly.md`, 2025).
- **AI productivity is partly a gambling illusion.** "Every completion or prompt is a pull of the lever." (`2025--epistemic-slot-machines.md`, 2025). See also the drunk pair-programmer satire in `2025--the-developer-experience-in-early-2025.md` (2025).
- **Claims that AI will accelerate discovery should be tested before we make knowledge work unviable.** He rejects Amodei's "messianic sermon" and proposes a pre-1930 LLM test: "let’s do the experiment **before** we make knowledge-production and creative work inviable career paths" (`2025--ai-like-its-1930.md`, 2025).
- **Open infrastructure needs a "Ulysses pact" against enclosure. Labels like "nonprofit", "shared" or "mission-driven" prove nothing.** "a “Ulysses Pact” that aims to make it harder for organizations to “enshitify” essential scholarly infrastructure" (`2025--comments-on-proposed-posi-2-revisions.md`, 2025). "Open by any other name is not open. Open is the key. Open is what provides people with exit." (`av/2024--geoffrey-bilder-the-principles-of-fauxpen-scholarly-infrastr.md`, 2024).
- **Research-integrity tooling is the new enclosure battleground.** "we're building suites of research integrity tools that are closed, that are expensive, that only a subset of publishers can access" (`av/2024--…fauxpen…md`, 2024).
- **Infrastructure labor is invisible and "cost-free" is a lie. Too much critical infrastructure sits in the US.** "It highlights otherwise invisible labor" (`2025--comments-on-proposed-posi-2-revisions.md`, 2025). "I’ve been concerned about the concentration of critical open scholarly infrastructure in the US for over a decade. I have ideas." (`2025--tomorrows-scholcomm-headlines-predictions-from-2023.md`, 2025).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Opening remarks and Session 5 harvest claims:**
1. We need a precise vocabulary. Stop saying "AI knows" or "hallucinates", and separate "machine learning" from LLMs.
2. Trust comes from context, not content. The provenance graph (PIDs, funders, affiliations, citations, retractions) is the hard-to-fake signal that replaces fluency. Detection tools are a dead end.
3. Integrity failures come from treating publication as the evaluation currency. AI makes that system untenable, and institutions must decouple the two.
4. Open means exit. Any intervention that builds infrastructure must carry POSI-style guarantees (open data, open source, living will, forkability), not just "nonprofit" or "shared" labels.

**Session 5 interventions he'd propose:**
- **POSI for the AI era.** Extend the Ulysses-pact model to knowledge infrastructure that AI depends on, including research-integrity tools. Back it with **community scrutiny of self-audits** (his own "1,000 days" posts).
- **A shared insurance pool or endowment ("Diamond OA infrastructure")** so small open infrastructures can survive, plus a funder-backed "mission-completion bounty fund" (`2025--comments…`: "Pay them to.").
- **Open provenance and research-integrity tooling built on the Research Nexus** as a public good, equitably accessible, instead of AI detectors.
- Possibly a **non-US redundancy plan** for critical scholarly infrastructure (`2025--tomorrows-scholcomm-headlines…`).
