# Position card: Nick Vincent (Simon Fraser University)

## 1. Role at summit and relevant bio

**Role:** One of three 7-minute talks in "What might a well-designed AI-mediated knowledge marketplace look like?" (11:45–12:30), with Tim O'Reilly and Alex Springer, followed by discussion.

**Bio:** Assistant Professor of Computing Science at SFU, where he directs the People- and Data-centric Computing group. He is Research Director at Metagov and is affiliated with the Public AI Network. His Northwestern PhD, supervised by Brent Hecht, on **"data leverage"** won the 2024 SIGCHI Outstanding Dissertation Award. His research has followed one line since 2018. It started with measuring Wikipedia's and user-generated content's value to search engines. It continued through **data strikes**, **conscious data contribution**, and **data dividends** (he helped lead the 2021 Berggruen white paper that anticipated Senator Sanders's 2026 AI-dividend proposal). His current work covers **collective bargaining for information**, **Public AI**, and **attestation and provenance** markets. He writes the *Data Leverage* newsletter and digital garden. On the pro-market versus pro-commons question, his answer is "both, with explicit rules": markets for *attested* data brokered by collectives, commons where communities choose them ("Don't market-ize everything!"), and public infrastructure to make the subsidies durable.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **What is happening to the knowledge commons is a "precarious quasi-enclosure,"** not simple theft or a tragedy of the commons. "The overall flow of value still kind of looks like AI is acting as a public good, but we are actually in a very precarious situation." AI labs offer "subsidized club goods" that "can (1) have their subsidy reduced, (2) be cut off entirely, or (3) redirected any time." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--the-paradox-of-reuse-in-2026-a-case.md`, 2026, co-written with David Pham)
- **The paradox of reuse:** "reuse without attribution will actually hurt platforms where information curation happens and therefore hurt AI itself eventually." (same file, 2026)
- **Information producers, researchers included, must bargain collectively.** "Producers of information goods (such as journalists, researchers, and creative professionals) need to be able to collectively bargain with AI product builders." Collective bargaining for information (CBI) "is the shortest (and perhaps only) path" to a sustainable AI ecosystem. Otherwise we risk an "ecological collapse" of the commons and a "capital singularity." (`nick-vincent/by/2025--collective-bargaining-in-the-information-economy-can-address.md`, NeurIPS 2025)
- **An AI dividend funded by a "Presumptive Commons-Rent Tax."** It is "a tax on unexplained or mysterious capability." Firms can reduce it to zero by documenting licensed or reciprocal data provenance, and auditors verify the claims. Disbursement should stay coarse: "tax dependence on collective data and disburse it coarsely while we figure out better valuation and interpretability methods." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--ai-dividends-without-taxing-compute-or-state-ownership.md`, 2026)
- **Clear "Data Rules" benefit creators and most AI companies alike**, so data policy "should not be reduced to 'Tech vs. Everyone Else.'" Keep bottom-up routes into the commons and use-restricted open sharing, such as CC Preference Signals: "Don't market-ize everything!" (`nick-vincent/by/2025--almost-everybody----including-both-data-creators-and.md`, 2025)
- **The AI evaluation crisis forces labs to rebuild provenance, and expert communities can seize it.** "Good evals are just good data." The open question is "whether future AI data work becomes centralized gig labor or a more plural ecosystem of data guilds, trusts, unions, and professional communities." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--the-ai-evaluation-crisis-is-an-opportunity-to.md`, 2026)
- **Link attestations along the whole supply chain.** Training data, evaluations, retrieval at inference time, outputs, and downstream AI-assisted artifacts should all be linkable. Guilds selling attested data "provide a resisting force against overreliance and slop." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--attestation-across-the-ai-supply-chain.md`, 2026)
- **Cheap polish breaks curation.** "AI progress is decoupling presentation quality from underlying quality," which damages peer review's proxy signals. He argues "we need to adjust credit allocation" toward terse, verifiable artifacts that expose raw data and APIs, and floats publishing full AI-interaction transcripts. (`nick-vincent/by/2026--ai-is-driving-the-cost-of-polish-down.md`, 2026)
- **Using AI means interacting with people upstream.** "Generative AI instead ranks grains: sub-bundle chunks, recombined into something new." We should keep "the causal chain in view" and "re-bundle in a coarse fashion." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--using-ai-kind-of-involves-interacting-with-other.md`, 2026)
- **Data labelling deserves a "new bargain."** It is "one of the grand challenges for computing and AI" to produce such data "with greater reward and concrete power for those doing the generation." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--data-labeling-work-should-be-dignified-not-dismissed.md`, 2026)
- **Data leverage is getting easier to use, and evaluation data is its strongest form.** "Data strikes and bargaining around scarce evaluation data in particular can create very large amounts of leverage." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--a-short-guide-to-data-strikes-and-conscious.md`, 2026)
- **Open source is not enough; we need Public AI.** "Open source AI must be complemented by public AI: infrastructure and institutions that ensure models are accessible, sustainable, and governed in the public interest." (`nick-vincent/by/2025--if-open-source-is-to-win-it-must.md`, 2025)
- **Scholarly publishing already shows the pattern.** "Professors provide the essential labor of writing and conducting peer review, but publishers capture most of the resulting economic value." (`nick-vincent/by/2026--are-we-horses-rethinking-data-as-labor.md`, 2026, with Tae Wan Kim)

## 4. Predicted contributions

**What his 7-minute talk would argue:**
1. **The marketplace has to price and route *attested* human judgment, not raw tokens.** That means markets for attested evaluation and training data, sold by guilds and trusts of experts (including researchers, reviewers and editors), with provenance linked from input to output.
2. **Collective bargaining is the missing counterweight.** Individual creators have no leverage against an information market whose price tends to zero. Joint ventures, data trusts, and antitrust safe harbours give it to them. Evaluation data is the pressure point.
3. **Pair the market with a commons backstop.** A presumptive commons-rent tax or dividend, reciprocity payments to Wikipedia- and OpenAlex-like commons, Public AI infrastructure, and opt-in routes to open publication. The aim is to move from quasi-enclosure to the "High E, High C" quadrant.
4. **Re-bundle attribution coarsely.** We can't yet trace "grains" to individual people, so credit should flow to communities and collections.

**Session 5 interventions he would push:**
- **Launch or seed a scholarly data guild or trust** (publishers, societies, reviewers) that negotiates the terms for AI training, retrieval and evaluation use of the scholarly record, with a standard contract template (`2025--collective-bargaining-in-the-information-economy-can-address.md`; `2025--almost-everybody----including-both-data-creators-and.md`).
- **An interoperable attestation registry** linking datasets, evaluations, retrieval events and AI-assisted papers, starting with evaluation data produced by expert reviewers (`2026--attestation-across-the-ai-supply-chain.md`).
- **Change credit and curation norms:** reward terse, verifiable artifacts (raw data, code, APIs) and consider requiring AI-interaction transcripts for AI-touched submissions (`2026--ai-is-driving-the-cost-of-polish-down.md`).
