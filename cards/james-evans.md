## 2. Core recent positions

1. **AI today narrows science collectively while it rewards individuals.** "In all fields, AI-augmented research focuses on a narrower scope of scientific topics and reduces the scientific engagement of follow-on research, leading to more overlapping research works that slows the expansion of knowledge." (`james-evans/by/2026--artificial-intelligence-tools-expand-scientists-impact-but-contract-sciences.md`, 2026). Spoken version: "it's not opening up new science, it's, it's closing down science." (`james-evans/av/2026--the-impact-of-ai-in-the-scientific-community.md`, 2026, ASR)
2. **Current LLMs converge on a "hivemind" and cannot negate. Human grounding is still required.** "For all the hype, today's AI for science remains a collaborator whose imagination, outputs, and judgment benefit from human grounding." (`james-evans/by/2026--contemporary-ai-lacks-the-imagination-to-diverge-or-negate.md`, 2026)
3. **The irreducibly human part is building the evaluative criteria themselves.** "The work of framing the question, recognising what kind of result would matter, contesting the categories themselves, and so of building and rebuilding the epistemic infrastructure on which useful knowledge depends, remains, for now, usefully human." (same file, 2026). Note the words "for now".
4. **Automated evaluation is circular.** "Automated evaluation in its current form risks generating an optimistic literature about itself. Judges and judged are drawn from the same distribution" (same file, 2026)
5. **Negative knowledge is missing from training data and from the record.** "Until pretraining ingests these absences through deliberate elicitation of expert null intuitions, registered reports, replication archives, and laboratory notebooks of dead ends, the asymmetry will persist." (same file, 2026). The paper also finds that after ChatGPT, null claims in abstracts were "sharply suppressed", undoing "a decade of progress".
6. **Peer review is overwhelmed, and verification needs a new science that uses AI as well as humans.** "This new era is gonna require much more from scientific verification than scientific peer review has ever produced for it in the past" (`james-evans/av/2026--the-impact-of-ai-in-the-scientific-community.md`, 2026, ASR). In the reverse-alignment essay: models "draft manuscripts at a pace that renders traditional peer review functionally obsolete, burying important breakthroughs beneath an avalanche of incremental recombination and outright slop." (`james-evans/by/2022--what-humanity-needs-to-flourish-in-the-next-decade.md`, 2026; the file is mis-dated 2022, the content is July 2026)
7. **Don't design AI to maximize trust. Design it to provoke evaluation.** "We need to build AI that opens the space of voices rather than performs this authoritative move of producing the single balanced response." He wants to "maximize, um, evaluative trustworthiness" (`james-evans/av/2026--the-impact-of-ai-in-the-scientific-community.md`, 2026, ASR). He also wants AI as "a stationary bike for the mind", not an e-bike (`james-evans/by/2022--what-humanity-needs-to-flourish...md`, 2026).

9. **LLMs are cultural and social technologies, and that is an expansive framing, not a deflationary one.** "This is not a diminutive framing of AI, but an expansive one." (`james-evans/social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-03-18)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Would endorse:**
- *"Writing is now cheaper than reading" and "human judgment... becomes the scarce, decisive ingredient."* His coalition essay says almost the same thing ("execution without verification": "the scarce input becomes the human bandwidth to validate outputs"), and his idea-rating study supplies the evidence.
- *Hype needs checking against evidence.* The idea-rating paper opens: "Bold claims that artificial intelligence will accelerate scientific discovery have raced ahead of evidence from working scientists."
- *Cognitive atrophy.* His offloading experiments found that "balanced" answers lead people to perform worse but feel better (Science podcast, 2026). He supports the "stationary bike" design principle.
- *Model collapse and homogenization.* His simulations of 200,000 agent conversations "all collapsed... lexically they become more diverse. Semantically, they collapse" (`james-evans/av/2026--james-evans-incentivizing-the-improbable-from-imitation-to-i.md`, 2026, ASR). This is the concept note's "photocopy of a photocopy" in his own data.
- *Old dysfunctions predate AI.* His 2008 finding on electronic publication narrowing citation, and his work on aging gatekeepers, fit "what was already broken".

**Would push back:**
- *"Keeping humans meaningfully in the loop" as a defence of existing human gatekeeping.* In his data, senior reviewers are part of the problem: "older scholars who are given naturally more credence... are also much more likely to criticize new and potentially disruptive work" (Science podcast, 2026). He wants diverse, younger and flatter judgment, not simply more human judgment.
- *Keeping AI out of evaluation.* He thinks AI is needed in verification: "even though reviews that are completely automated are suspect... if we seriously wanna evaluate the top to the bottom of the science... we have to go there" (Science podcast, 2026).
- *"A vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people whose writing it was trained on."* He would half-accept this through the cultural-technologies frame. But he co-signs claims that reasoning models "simulate complex, multi-agent-like interactions" and he writes about agents' "phenomenology" (Silicon Interior). He would resist a vocabulary that forbids verbs like "reason".
- *Openness as a pure good.* In his account, shared open databases can make replication look better than it is ("dramatically decreasing the likelihood that those findings will end up getting reproduced in the wild", Delhi talk 2026, ASR).

**Likely silent or mildly skeptical:** the energy, "Bond villains" and marketing paragraphs. He does not write in that register.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 2 harvest claims he would push:**
1. "AI currently helps scientists and hurts science." Individual incentives (3x papers, 4.8x citations, earlier promotion) drive a collective narrowing toward data-rich, well-lit questions. So governance must fix incentives, not only models.
2. "Verification is the bottleneck, and it needs its own funded science." That means code, data and instrument-provenance checks at scale, done by humans and AIs together. Journals are "one of the only institutions" with an incentive, and funders and universities "have never seriously taken that responsibility."
3. "Design for disagreement, not for trust." Interfaces should show multiple perspectives and surprises instead of one authoritative answer. AI should be used to find what is surprising (perplexity), not what is typical.
4. "Diversity of perspectives is the scarce resource, for AIs and for humans." That means plural, institutionally checked agent ecosystems, not a single oracle.

**Session 5 interventions:**
- **A negative-knowledge commons:** registered reports, null results, replication archives and lab notebooks of dead ends, gathered deliberately as a public record and as training data.
- **A verification layer for science:** shared AI-plus-human auditing of code, data and instruments, with dedicated funding.
- **"Capture indices" and diversity-aware funding portfolios** that measure concentration in fields and review panels, alongside collective licensing for training data.
