## 2. Core recent positions

1. **AI tools that do our thinking make us less competent, and atrophy is plain physiology.** "The brain is an organ, like a muscle. If I outsource all of my thinking to something or someone else, it will atrophy just as your muscles do." (`david-krakauer/av/2026--david-krakauer-emergence-and-scaling-of-agi.md`, 2026)
2. **Tools are either complementary or competitive, and ubiquitous opaque AI falls in the collapse regime.** "AI is now reaching billions of people, and it is an opaque tool appealing to users with limited prior competence, thereby placing it in a regime that predicts competence collapse." (`david-krakauer/by/2026--competitive-and-complementary-tools.md`, 2026)
3. **Order of learning matters: competence first, tool second.** "This is why arithmetic should be taught before the calculator and programming before using coding agents." (same file, 2026)
4. **Use matters too: verified use of an LLM augments, uncritical use diminishes.** "When its output is reconstructed and verified, it transfers a method and augments intelligence. When its answers are accepted uncritically... it diminishes intelligence." (same file, 2026)
5. **LLMs have capabilities, not intelligence. Intelligence is "less is more."** "Intelligence is a property of understanding and only incidentally a matter of capability." (`david-krakauer/by/2025--large-language-models-and-emergence.md`, 2025) Also: "the way that AI has evolved is in the direction of confusing being very knowledgeable with very intelligent." (`av/2026--david-krakauer-emergence-and-scaling-of-agi.md`, 2026)
6. **They are intelligent the way a calculator is.** "I'm not saying they're more intelligent than us at all... I think they're intelligent the way a calculator is." (`av/2026--...emergence-and-scaling-of-agi.md`, 2026) He is nuanced, though: psychometrically, "AI models exhibit general intelligence suppressing specialized intelligences," and intelligence belongs to "the expanded individual-tool system." (`by/2026--the-rise-and-fall-of-g-in-agi.md`, 2026)
7. **Superintelligence opens the way to super-stupidity, and the danger is undetectable error.** "The danger is that an AI will be wrong in ways humans can no longer detect because the very capacities that would catch the error have been outsourced to the machine." (`david-krakauer/by/2026--what-makes-humans-stupid.md`, 2026)
8. **Hallucination is structural, not a bug.** It is "a structural feature of systems that optimize for appeal and plausibility rather than truth." (same file, 2026)
9. **He proposes a science of stupidity and design principles for tools that enhance rather than replace.** It "would promote the development of design principles that distinguish tools which enhance cognition from tools which replace it." (same file, 2026)
10. **LLM-assisted writing is homogenising slop.** "I now receive on a daily basis tens of emails written as collaborations between humans and LLMs, and it's 100% rubbish... eventually everyone will sound the same." (`av/2026--...emergence-and-scaling-of-agi.md`, 2026)
11. **Judgment is not impact. Citation metrics corrupt creative science.** "Peer review is one of those impact metric obsessed constraints on creative work." (`david-krakauer/by/2023--the-long-shadow-of-complexity.md`, 2023; rough transcript)
12. **Divorce expertise from authority, and "follow the science" misunderstands science.** "The politicization of science is coming in part from experts who want to be authorities and we have to undo that." (`david-krakauer/by/2022--the-way-we-were-sfreporter.md`, 2022; quoted in a journalist's profile)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Would strongly endorse:**
- **Cognitive atrophy, and the "vicious circle."** His own 2026 texts use nearly the same words: "in a vicious spiral, the less the user thinks the more dependent they become on their tools. That is until the tool disappears and the whole system collapses" (`by/2026--what-makes-humans-stupid.md`). His model supplies the mechanism: hysteresis, a collapse threshold, a critical period.
- **"human judgment... becomes the scarce, decisive ingredient"** and **"a flawed result can propagate for years"**. This matches his undetectable-error argument.
- **"designing tools and workflows that amplify and reward it, rather than replace it."** This is his complementary/competitive distinction almost verbatim.
- **Scepticism of "intelligence" claims and of hype.** He talks of "moonshine" and says "I don't know why people are excited about the technology if that's the likely omega point" (`av/2026--...md`). He mocks self-declared AI "thought leaders" (`by/2026--what-makes-humans-stupid.md`).
- **Perverse incentives** ("publish or perish," impact factors). He has criticised impact metrics for years (`by/2023--the-long-shadow-of-complexity.md`).

**Would push back on or complicate:**
- **"hallucinates"... "a slightly charming and eccentric tic."** He agrees with the substance but uses "hallucination" himself without scare-quotes as a term for structural error. He wouldn't police the verb.
- **"a vast statistical model... confusing a statistical model for a knowing mind."** He is friendly to the conclusion but not to the ontology. He rejects the mind/matter framing ("the strong functionalists, the immaterialists, who are basically modern dualists, don't take matter seriously enough", `by/2026--worldviews-jim-rutt-show.md`). In the PNAS paper he allowed that LLMs may have "a novel form of 'understanding'" and are "exotic, mind-like entities" (`by/2023--the-debate-over-understanding-in-ai-s.md`). His line is "capability without intelligence," not "no mind at all." He would likely resist a flat binary.
- **The concept note's optimism about AI** ("it could also help us fix them", "an invitation to build"). He is more pessimistic: "It's one of the reasons why I'm not very optimistic about technology... the long-term implication of technology is the significant diminution and dilution of what it means to be a human" (`av/2026--...md`).
- **"keeping humans meaningfully in the loop" as sufficient.** His model says presence in the loop is not enough. What matters is the order of learning and the transparency of the tool. An uncritical human in the loop still collapses.
- **Peer review and credentials as the fix for authority.** He is sceptical of peer review and metrics and prefers institutional "judgment" plus letting "the world" decide impact over time.
- **"Scholars as the ultimate annotators" paid for curation.** Probable ambivalence. Tying scholarly worth to a market for AI training labels runs against his "judgment vs. impact/market" distinction ("before long, you are just a mirror of the market", `by/2023--the-long-shadow-of-complexity.md`). This is inference.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Opening remarks (his "harvest"):**
- AI is the most powerful *cognitive artifact* ever built, and the question is whether it is complementary or competitive. "Superintelligence is only interesting to the extent that it makes me more intelligent, not... more stupid or more servile or more dependent."
- Stupidity is not the absence of intelligence but an active, scaling phenomenon. Fluent, confident, wrong output is the signature case.
- Intelligence is "doing more with less." Evaluate AI and scholarship by parsimony and understanding, not by volume or capability.
- Science is humanistic: "The purpose of science in the universe is to make the universe intelligible to us, not to control it" (`av/2026--...md`).

**Session 5 interventions he would propose or back:**
1. **Tool-resistant education and sequencing norms.** "Competence before reliance." During learning, allow "only tools students could in principle do without" (`by/2026--competitive-and-complementary-tools.md`). For scholarship, this means training researchers in reading, reviewing and analysis before handing them AI pipelines.
2. **Transparency and "reconstruct-and-verify" standards for AI tools used in research.** Tools should expose enough of their working to be internalized. Verified use should be the norm, and uncritical acceptance should be flagged (same file).
3. **A funded "science of stupidity" / science of epistemic degradation.** This would measure competence, reliance and error detection across the scholarly system and study "the institutional conditions under which collective intelligence degrades into collective stupidity" (`by/2026--what-makes-humans-stupid.md`).
