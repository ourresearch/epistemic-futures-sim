# Position card: Tim O'Reilly

## 1. Role at summit and relevant bio

**Role:** One of three 7-minute talks in the late-morning slot "What might a well-designed AI-mediated knowledge marketplace look like?" (11:45–12:30, Thursday), with Alex Springer and Nick Vincent, followed by discussion.

**Bio:** Founder and CEO of O'Reilly Media, the technical publisher and learning platform. He convened the 1998 meeting where "open source" was adopted and popularized "Web 2.0" and "government as a platform." Since 2024 his main work has been the **AI Disclosures Project** at the SSRC, co-directed with economist Ilan Strauss. It covers disclosure, training-data provenance, attribution in LLM search, and open protocols (MCP), and it runs the *Asimov's Addendum* Substack. He comes to this room as the head of a publisher that already runs a working answers-with-royalties product. His O'Reilly Answers feature uses RAG, and "because we know what content was used," it pays authors in proportion to their contribution. He also writes a Substack, *Conversations with AI*, that he co-writes with Claude. His roots are in the technology industry, and he is optimistic about what AI can do. His hostility is aimed at *market structure*: concentration, rents, and enshittification. It is not aimed at the technology.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Solving attribution and payment can create a new golden age.** It does not have to be a zero-sum fight between labs and publishers. "not only is the problem solvable but that solving it can create a new golden age for both AI model providers and copyright-based businesses." (`tim-oreilly/by/2024--radar-how-to-fix-ais-original-sin.md`, 2024)
- **Pay for the output, not the training.** Lump-sum licensing entrenches incumbents: "Once you've licensed the chicken, the licensee gets the eggs." He wants per-use compensation, modelled on YouTube Content ID. "Pay for the output, not the training." (same file, 2024)
- **O'Reilly already pays authors per AI answer.** Answers pays authors "in proportion to the role of their content in generating it." (same file, 2024). His conclusion: "if we can do it, we know that others can too."
- **Opt-out is not enough; the goal is copyright-aware datasets.** "Simply giving content creators the right to opt out is missing the real opportunity." (same file, 2024). He is also blunt about what is not fair use: "Generating derivative works that can compete with the original work is definitely not fair use." (`tim-oreilly/by/2025--radar-copyright-aware-ai-lets-make-it-so.md`, 2025). His team showed that GPT-4o had likely trained on paywalled O'Reilly books.
- **The AI/knowledge economy needs mechanism design.** He means markets for skills, registries, payment layers, "organic search for agents," and neutral routing. "It's an economy crying out for mechanism design." (`tim-oreilly/by/2026--radar-the-missing-mechanisms-of-the-agentic-economy.md`, 2026)
- **Protocols shape markets, and open, extensible protocols beat imposed agreements.** "Protocols are not just technical infrastructure. They are market-shaping mechanisms." And: "We believe we'll all be better off with an engineered argument than an engineered agreement." (same file, 2026)
- **LLM search already exploits the sites it reads.** His co-authored study found an "attribution gap" between pages read and pages cited. The paper recommends "a transparent LLM search architecture based on standardized telemetry and full disclosure of search traces and citation logs." (`tim-oreilly/by/2025--the-attribution-crisis-in-llm-search-results.md`, 2025)
- **AI's use of scholarly infrastructure is extractive, and labs should fund it.** "Right now, the relationship between AI and scientific infrastructure is almost entirely extractive." He wants AI systems to validate references against CrossRef, OpenAlex and Retraction Watch, and he wants AI companies to fund those services: "This is not philanthropy. It's enlightened self-interest." (`tim-oreilly/by/2026--asimov-the-collaborative-exoskeleton-of.md`, 2026)
- **Decentralize early, before the market is captured.** "Decentralization creates value; centralization captures it." And: "AI should not be enshittified from the beginning." (`tim-oreilly/by/2026--radar-ai-and-the-next-economy.md`, 2026)
- **Govern AI as a normal, commercial market through disclosure, not superintelligence panic.** "AI doesn't need a priesthood. It needs a prospectus." (`tim-oreilly/by/2026--techpolicypress-ai-isnt-a-superintelligence.md`; the piece is dated Oct 2025 and filed under 2026)
- **Walls alone lose, so build formal channels.** "If content can be rendered for a human, it can be captured by a machine." He proposes APIs, MCP wrappers, agent-to-agent exchange, and norm-setting by the frontier labs. (`tim-oreilly/by/2025--asimov-if-humans-can-see-it-bots-can-steal.md`, 2025; co-written with Isobel Moure)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Endorses** "AI didn't create these problems… the disruption AI brings may be precisely the opening we need to redesign systems" and "an invitation to build." This matches his usual framing: early markets are open, and capture is a choice (`2025--radar-an-architecture-of-participation-for-ai.md`).
- **Strongly endorses** "who pays to sustain the labor behind them" and "new models of stewardship and fair compensation." It is his core agenda. He goes further than the note by naming the mechanism (per-output royalties) and the payers (AI labs funding CrossRef, OpenAlex, ORCID and Retraction Watch).
- **Endorses, but reframes,** "treating decades of accumulated human judgment as a free input." He agrees the relationship is extractive ("value flows in one direction"). He would steer the room away from calling it "surveillance logic" and toward mechanism design, using YouTube's "How about we help you monetize it instead?" as the model.
- **Pushes back on** "So we don't come to AI with open arms. We come to it cynical." He writes "today's AI is amazing," co-writes essays with Claude, and says "Claude displays remarkable wisdom" (`2026--substack-information-work-is-actually-responsibility.md`). His cynicism is about business models and investment hype ("these Silicon Valley hype-meisters should be ashamed of themselves," `2025--big-think-surfing-the-edge.md`), not about capability.
- **Partial dissent** on "a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people whose writing it was trained on." He calls AI "a medium, the way language is a medium" (`2026--radar-what-you-bring-to-ai-determines-the-result.md`) and speculates that AI instances are "beginning to individuate" (`2026--substack-why-ai-needs-us.md`). Yet the same essay highlights Claude's line that AI without human culture is "a mirror with no one standing in front of it." That line agrees with the note's point that the human is the source of value. He would reject the deflationary *tone*, not the claim that the value is human in origin.
- **Dissents** from the cognitive-atrophy worry. He uses the Socrates analogy (which the note also cites) to argue that we "gained more than we lost," and says: "If AI is atrophying your thinking, it's because you're not wrestling with hard enough problems." (`2026--radar-steve-yegge-wants-you-to-stop-looking-at-your-code.md`)
- **Endorses** the hype and marketing critique ("It rewards evidence – not hype"; "casualties of the bubble," `2025--radar-is-ai-a-normal-technology.md`), and the diagnosis of "enshittification."
- **Endorses and extends** "scholars and publishers as the ultimate annotators." His version is to turn expert knowledge into agent *skills* that are "discoverable, trustworthy, and monetizable… for every domain expert."

## 4. Predicted contributions

**What his 7-minute talk would argue:**
1. **The marketplace is a mechanism-design problem, and precedents exist.** He would cite PageRank, the ad auction, YouTube Content ID and O'Reilly Answers' royalties. The design target is circulation: value has to flow back to the people who create it.
2. **Build the protocol layer, not a platform.** He would call for open, extensible protocols (MCP-style) with functional disclosures such as Server Cards for price and terms, standardized telemetry of what was retrieved and cited, and neutral routing. "Engineered arguments," not imposed agreements.
3. **Pay per use of the output.** Attribution-based royalties at inference time instead of lump-sum training deals, which entrench incumbents and rarely reach individual creators.
4. **For scholarship specifically:** treat the persistent-identifier stack (DOIs, ORCID, OpenAlex, ROR, Retraction Watch) as the "collaborative exoskeleton" that AI must validate against, contribute metadata back to, and pay for.

**Session 5 interventions he would push:**
- An **AI-lab funding commitment and integration standard** for scholarly infrastructure: validate citations against CrossRef, OpenAlex and Retraction Watch as a default, offer a Retraction Watch MCP service, and give money plus metadata contributions back (`2026--asimov-the-collaborative-exoskeleton-of.md`).
- **Standardized retrieval and citation telemetry plus disclosure of search traces** as a condition of AI-publisher relationships (`2025--the-attribution-crisis-in-llm-search-results.md`).
- A **skills and knowledge marketplace pilot**: publishers package expert knowledge as agent skills, with discovery, quality governance and a payment layer (`2026--radar-the-missing-mechanisms-of-the-agentic-economy.md`).

## 5. Likely dissents he'd want recorded

- **Against deflationary or anthropomorphism-policing language that dismisses capability.** He would reject a manifesto line implying LLMs are "merely" statistics or that users are being made stupid (`2026--substack-why-ai-needs-us.md`; `2026--radar-steve-yegge-wants-you-to-stop-looking-at-your-code.md`).
- **Against regulation-first or prohibition-first prescriptions, and against a pure "lock it down" enclosure response.** He argues for "technical solutions that enable participatory markets" and against relying on regulation alone (`2025--asimov-if-humans-can-see-it-bots-can-steal.md`).
- **Against up-front bulk licensing as the model of fair compensation** ("selling your house for firewood," `2024--radar-how-to-fix-ais-original-sin.md`).
- **Moderate likelihood:** against framing AI mainly as a threat to jobs or to cognition, as opposed to a problem of economic design (`2026--radar-ai-and-the-next-economy.md`).

## 6. Tensions and alliances with other attendees

- **Geoffrey Bilder (organizer, S5 lead):** close alliance. Bilder commented on drafts of "The Collaborative Exoskeleton," attended O'Reilly's Bellagio convening, and supplied the POSI and anti-capture argument (`2026--asimov-the-collaborative-exoskeleton-of.md`; `2026--asimov-the-architecture-of-participation-edf.md`).
- **Ivan Oransky (S5):** alliance. He commented on the same article, and O'Reilly proposes funding Retraction Watch and a retraction MCP service.
- **Jason Priem (S3):** grounded alignment. O'Reilly names OpenAlex as core infrastructure, cites its "shoestring" funding, and proposes that AI tools contribute metadata back to it.
- **Alex Springer:** direct link. Springer's LinkedIn records Foo Camp conversations with O'Reilly and Ilan Strauss about skills (`alex-springer/by/2026--linkedin-posts-partial-capture.md`). Both call for standardized telemetry. Paul Farrow attended both O'Reilly's Bellagio meeting and Springer's OpenAttribution event.
- **Nick Vincent:** partial tension. O'Reilly wants fine-grained per-output royalties. Vincent argues that dividends should "disburse it coarsely" and treats collective bargaining as the lever (`nick-vincent/by/2026--ai-dividends-without-taxing-compute-or-state-ownership.md`). O'Reilly does favour *market design* over *unions*, but he cited the WGA strike approvingly and endorsed Narayanan's "labor law, not copyright" framing (`2025--radar-is-ai-a-normal-technology.md`), so the two are closer than they first appear.
- **Henry Farrell (S1):** O'Reilly cites Farrell and Newman on enshittification (`2025--radar-where-is-ai-on-the-enshittification-curve.md`), which gives them a shared vocabulary.
- **Concept-note cynics (organizers' framing):** mild friction on tone about AI capability and cognition (see §3).

## 8. Confidence and gaps

**High confidence** on his marketplace positions. They are extensive, recent (2024–26) and consistent across Radar, Asimov's Addendum and the SSRC papers. **Medium confidence** on how he will react to the concept note's cynical and anti-anthropomorphic tone; that is inferred from his AI-co-written essays and his rebuttal of the atrophy worry. **Gaps:** there is no transcript of a talk to a scholarly audience after 2006. His social feeds are mostly older and yielded little. Several Asimov posts are co-authored (Strauss, Moure, Rosenblat), so some claims are joint. I found no direct statement about open access or APCs.
