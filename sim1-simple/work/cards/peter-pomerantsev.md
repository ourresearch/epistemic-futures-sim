# Peter Pomerantsev — position card

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Session 1 ("The Epistemic Landscape") participant.** He is a Senior Fellow at the SNF Agora Institute, Johns Hopkins, where he co-directs the Arena Initiative (he ran Arena at LSE 2017–20). He was born in Kyiv in 1977, worked in Russian TV in Moscow in the 2000s, and wrote *Nothing Is True and Everything Is Possible* (2014), *This Is Not Propaganda* (2019) and *How to Win an Information War* (2024, about Sefton Delmer's WWII counter-propaganda). He co-hosts *Autocracy in America* with Anne Applebaum, and has testified to US House/Senate committees and advised the UK "fake news" inquiry.

He brings a propaganda scholar's and practitioner's view:
- Information disorder is about power, identity and agency more than facts.
- Authoritarian information tactics (Russia, now the US) came before and shaped today's crisis.

He is not an AI specialist. His own writing barely touches LLMs. His AI signals come mostly from reposts about "LLM grooming," the practice of seeding training data with propaganda.

## 2. Core recent positions

1. **The media is new; lying is not.** "The internet does not introduce new types of speech. What is new is the technology: the systems of targeting, scaling, amplification, sucking your data and manipulating your every vulnerability." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2025--observer-elon-musk-and-the-new-world-order-the-hijacking-of-the-globa.md` (2025)
2. **Abundance became a weapon: "censorship through noise."** "More information was supposed to mean a more informed debate, but we seem less capable of deliberation than ever." He quotes Tim Wu: states now see "speech itself as a censorial weapon." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2019--jods-to-unreality-and-beyond.md` (2019, MIT Press JoDS)
3. **Fact-checking is necessary but not enough.** "Debunking is an essential and noble endeavor... but much research shows that fact-checking tends to rebound when it bumps up against people's partisan biases." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2025--noema-journalism-in-an-age-of-authoritarianism.md` (2025). Also: "The old idea that 'more information protects us' no longer works." `peter-pomerantsev/social/x-timeline.jsonl` (own thread, 2026-03-12)
4. **Belief is about identity and belonging, not evidence.** "The competition with the big lie is not just, or even primarily, about fact checking. It's a competition between different models of belonging." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2023--observer-we-cant-fight-the-republican-partys-big-lie-with-facts-alone.md` (2023). His 2026 thread opens: "The Biggest Lie we Tell Ourselves is People Want The Truth" (x-timeline, 2026-03-12)
5. **Propaganda aims at helplessness, so the remedy is agency.** "The ultimate aim of authoritarian propagandists is often to sow so much doubt and confusion that it leaves people passive and ready to give up their agency to a strongman leader." Noema (2025). The defense is "metacognition" and "becoming the author of your own narrative." (x-timeline, 2026-03-12)
6. **Regulate mechanisms and transparency, not content.** "Focus not on content per se but on the right of the citizen to understand how the information environment around them is being shaped... This lack of transparency is in itself a form of censorship." `2019--jods-to-unreality-and-beyond.md` (2019). Restated: "free speech is not only about the right to express yourself, it's also about the right to receive information." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2025--spectator-the-anti-semitism-algorithm.md` (2025)
7. **Build public-interest digital infrastructure on the Reith/BBC model.** A democratic internet would have "the digital equivalent of public parks, libraries, town halls." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2023--guardian-the-web-is-a-blade-runner-nightmare-but-there-is-a-way-to-st.md` (2023). Wikipedia is "a glimpse of the internet that might have been." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2021--atlantic-how-to-put-out-democracys-dumpster-fire.md` (2021, with Applebaum; backs Zuckerman's ad-tax fund)
8. **Tell humans from "replicants."** He wants accounts "tethered to real people: the difference between humans and replicants would be clear," while keeping anonymity as a right. `2023--guardian-the-web-is-a-blade-runner...` (2023); self-sovereign identity in `2021--atlantic-how-to-put-out...` (2021)
9. **Process matters more than machine-made consensus.** On Google's AI "Habermas machine": "This may somewhat miss the point – which is to get people to go through the process of discussing and reaching a consensus. It's the process that is just as important as the result." `2025--observer-elon-musk...` (2025)
10. **Measure trust, not clicks.** Media should "develop metrics for increasing trust and fostering constructive dialogue across divides." Noema (2025). Also `2020--newstatesman-can-the-bbc-survive-in-an-age-of-fracture.md`: "An honest subjectivity, paradoxically, can be the path to a more objective discourse."
11. **The US is abandoning the field while autocracies advance.** "Washington is in the process of destroying these legacy international media outlets that it created in the Cold War." `peter-pomerantsev/by/2025--foreignpolicy-how-to-fight-putin-on-the-information-battlefield.md` (2025). "As Russia, China and Iran augment their information war tools America retreats from the cognitive domain…" `peter-pomerantsev/social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl` (own post, 2025-09-06)
12. **AI-specific (thin, mostly reposts).** He notes a Russian campaign "used AI to generate fake online personas" (`2025--spectator-the-anti-semitism-algorithm.md`). He reposted work on the Pravda network "flood[ing] LLMs with pro-Russia content" and "Disinformation warriors are 'grooming' chatbots" (bluesky reposts, 2025-02-27 and 2025-07-17; X retweets 2025-11-28).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Strong endorsement: "AI didn't create these problems."** His whole career says this. The "propaganda of unreality," which "arrived first in Russia" in the 1990s, pre-dates social media and AI (`2019--jods-to-unreality-and-beyond.md`). He will add that the rot is political (post-Cold War collapse of future-oriented narratives), not only technological or scholarly-institutional.
- **Strong endorsement of "an engine for promoting itself... generate [advocates] on demand."** Troll farms, "manufacturing consensus," and bot "replicants" are exactly this (`2023--guardian-the-web-is-a-blade-runner...`). He will generalize it from hype to state propaganda and LLM grooming.
- **Endorses "writing is now cheaper than reading" and the flooding worry, through "censorship through noise"** (2019).
- **Strongly endorses the note's line on governments "stripping power from watchdogs... controlling the very institutions that produce and safeguard knowledge."** He will push it harder, using Orbán/Putin/Trump parallels and US retreat from international media (FP 2025).
- **Pushes back on the note's implicit theory of trust.** The note's remedy is better signals of rigor ("harder-to-fake signals"). His research says trust and belief track identity, belonging and agency. Better signals and more facts will not win back publics who distrust the institutions issuing them (`2023--observer-we-cant-fight...`; Noema 2025). He will want the diagnosis to include demand-side psychology and trauma, not only supply-side signal integrity.
- **Endorses the historical-arc framing.** He also argues from precedent: radio became a tool of Coughlin and Goebbels before Reith; "there was a similar panic at the start of the 20th century too" (Lippmann) (`2025--observer-elon-musk...`; `2021--atlantic-how-to-put-out...`).
- **Cognitive surrender/atrophy: likely in scope, reframed as agency.** He has no text on AI deskilling. But the Habermas-machine point (the process of reasoning together is the value) and the "passive... give up their agency" diagnosis match the note's worry. His likely version is *civic* surrender: outsourcing judgment to strongmen or systems, answered by metacognition and participation, not by individual brain-training.
- **Moral panic: he will warn against panic-driven content regulation.** He criticized the UK "legal but harmful" regime and the "information war" framing because it risks adopting the Kremlin's logic of "information sovereignty" (`2019--jods-to-unreality-and-beyond.md`).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 1 harvest claims:**
- "The crisis predates AI and is political: authoritarians learned to rule by flooding, not censoring. AI lowers the cost of that flood and adds new targets (training data)."
- "The fact-checking paradigm has hit its limits. Belief is identity. Any diagnosis that treats the problem as missing or bad information will fail."
- "The deepest harm is loss of agency: people passive, cynical, ready to hand judgment over. That is the 'cognitive surrender' worth putting in scope."
- "Opacity is itself censorship. Citizens can't see why they see what they see."

**Session 5 interventions:**
- A **transparency / right-to-receive-information standard** for AI and platforms, including provenance, researcher data access, and human-vs-synthetic labelling.
- **Public-interest digital infrastructure** (civic spaces, public-service media and knowledge institutions), funded by a levy on data-driven advertising, Zuckerman-style.
- **Trust-and-agency metrics** for knowledge institutions (media, publishers) in place of engagement metrics. Monitoring of **"LLM grooming"** of training corpora by state actors is an inferred extension.

## 5. Likely dissents for the manifesto

- Against a **facts-and-signals-only remedy** that ignores identity, belonging and emotional drivers of belief (`2023--observer-we-cant-fight...`; Noema 2025).
- Against **content-based speech regulation or "harmful information" framing**; he prefers transparency (`2019--jods-to-unreality-and-beyond.md`).
- Against **AI-mediated consensus tools** replacing human deliberative process (`2025--observer-elon-musk...`).
- Possibly against a manifesto that treats the crisis as **scholarly-internal and apolitical**, silent on authoritarian capture and US retreat (inferred from FP 2025 and Noema 2025).

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Henry Farrell**: SNF Agora colleague, with mutual citation. Pomerantsev draws on Farrell's reading of Musk's X ("as my Johns Hopkins University colleague Henry Farrell writes," `2025--observer-elon-musk...`). Farrell cites Pomerantsev 2014 on flooding (`henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-markets-bureaucracy-democracy-ai.md`). Both think the problem is publics and belonging, not individual misinformation. Expect them to reinforce each other in S1.
- **Katherine Maher**:
  - *Alliance* on public-service media (Reith/BBC; he wrote "Can the BBC survive," 2020) and on Wikipedia as the model internet (2021).
  - *Tension* on journalism's stance. He calls for media "less about 'objectivity' and more about helping effect change" (`2025--observer-elon-musk...`) and for journalists to protect democracy "rather than just chronicling its demise" (Noema 2025). Maher, under congressional attack, insists NPR be "scrupulously impartial" with a strict editorial firewall (`katherine-maher/by/2025--congressional-testimony-doge-subcommittee.md`).
- **Selena Deckelmann (Wikimedia)**: likely ally, given his praise of Wikipedia (2021), but not directly evidenced.
- **Blaise Agüera y Arcas / Google**: implicit skepticism of Google's "Habermas machine" (2025). This is a critique of the tool, not the person.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

**High confidence** on:
- the pre-AI roots of the crisis
- the critique of fact-checking
- identity/belonging and agency
- transparency-not-content regulation
- public-interest infrastructure
- the anti-authoritarian framing

**Low-to-medium confidence** on anything AI-specific. He has almost no first-person writing on LLMs, and his AI signals are mostly reposts, which show attention, not endorsement. He may play the "politics and psychology" voice more than weigh in on scholarly publishing, where he has no track record (peer review, open access, preprints are absent from his corpus).

**Gaps:**
- LSE Arena reports (2017–20) are not in the corpus.
- BBC Radio 4 and podcast output is unenumerated.
- FT pieces (9) are paywalled.
- The 2026 FT essay on Putin's narrative is referenced but not held.
