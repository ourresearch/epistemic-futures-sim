# Position card: Ramón Alvarado

## 1. Role at summit + relevant bio

**Role:** Session 2 participant ("AI as Epistemic Actor: Risks, Capabilities, and Governance"). He is the room's philosopher of computational science: the person who will police the words "intelligence", "trust", "opacity", and "actor".

**Bio:** Associate Professor of Philosophy and of the Data Science Initiative at the University of Oregon, where he is also Data Ethics Coordinator and a member of the university AI council. He is Editor-in-Chief of *Global Philosophy* (Springer) and was 2025 President of IACAP (International Association for Computing and Philosophy). He co-authors the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* entry on computer simulations (with Eric Winsberg, revised 2026). His PhD is from Kansas (2020, with John Symons), and his book is *Simulating Science: Computer Simulations as Scientific Instruments* (Springer 2023). His signature ideas:
- AI is an **epistemic technology**, not an intelligence.
- The only trust AI can properly receive is **epistemic trust**.
- AI's **opacity** is insurmountable, especially about the *source and nature of error*.
- Data-science tools cause distinctive **epistemic injustices**.

A newer line of work covers **epistemic loneliness** and the "epistemic partner". He is not a refusenik. He celebrates the technical achievement, rejects "use or refuse" dichotomies, and draws on Humphreys and Bacon to argue that instruments often outperform human judgment.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Drop the word "intelligence".** "whatever intelligence may be, it does not play a part in these technologies. Furthermore, the continued use of the term is indeed misguiding in a fundamental way." (`ramon-alvarado/by/2025--artifactual-epistemology-and-environmental-intelligence.md`, 2025). On X, 2026-05-21: "AI is the opposite of intelligence. And that's fascinating."
- **AI is an "epistemic technology".** "technologies which are exclusively and intrinsically designed to enhance our capacities as knowers" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2023--ai-as-an-epistemic-technology.md`, 2023). His criterion is that AI works in epistemic contexts, on epistemic content, through epistemic operations. The term is "not a success term": a bad LLM still qualifies, "like a bad 🪛" (X, 2024-09-06).
- **Recognising AI's epistemic capacities needs no mind-talk.** "Acknowledging the Epistemic capacities of machines does not require or imply anything to do with consciousness or whatever idea of agency we inherit from either anthropocentric or biocentric frameworks." (`social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2026-05-26). He accepts artifacts as "epistemic agents (NOT sentient/intelligent)" (X, 2024-04-18).
- **The only appropriate trust is epistemic trust, and opaque AI may not earn it.** "If AI technologies are essentially and representationally opaque … explainability is simply not possible. If this is so, we cannot allocate epistemic trust." (`…ai-as-an-epistemic-technology.md`, 2023). Also: "whether or not AI can, in fact, be trusted remains an open question." (`ramon-alvarado/by/2022--what-kind-of-trust-does-ai-deserve-if-any.md`, abstract)
- **The black-box problem is about failure, not workings.** "what is at play in the challenge of epistemic opacity is not just any kind of general transparency regarding how something works, but rather the access … to the nature and source of error." (`ramon-alvarado/by/2025--challenges-for-computational-reliabilism-in-ai-and-other.md`, 2025). On X: "inner workings are less important than inner failings" (2023-09-24). He also wrote that "Opacity is often the opposite of empowerment: we cannot wield, we cannot inquire, we cannot know, we cannot challenge, we cannot fix." (X, 2023-09-22)
- **A good track record does not license trust in science.** Computational reliabilism faces challenges that are "serious but maybe even insurmountable" (`…computational-reliabilism…md`, 2025). "in science and other socially consequential contexts not knowing how/why a technology fails is catastrophic and bad practice" (X, 2023).
- **Instruments often outperform human judgment.** He does not accept "human judgment first" uncritically. "in some nontrivial instances … it is our artifacts that will determine what counts as evidence in decision-making and in which the responsibility of discriminating what counts as relevant information cannot be but delegated to them." We should "not assume that it is always something suspect" (`…artifactual-epistemology…md`, 2025).
- **Against the "use or refuse" dichotomy and abstinence.** "Download open source models, fine tune them or untune them with weird data … make them feral." (`social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-10-07). Also: "'avoiding' LLMs doesn't seem like the right/realistic approach" (X, 2024-07-20). He tells Pope Leo that "La dicotomía de uso o rehuso nunca ha sido adecuada" ("the use-or-refuse dichotomy has never been adequate") (X, 2026-06-03).
- **Power is being ceded through epistemic tech.** "si … 'el saber es poder' y si la #IA es una tecnología epistemica, entonces estamos cediendo poder" ("if knowledge is power and AI is an epistemic technology, then we are ceding power") (Bluesky/X, 2026-06-03).
- **Epistemic injustice.** Data-science tools produce "the diminution of individuals with respect to their standing as knowers or their credibility as sources of testimony" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2022--epistemic-injustice-and-data-science-technologies.md`, with Symons). With LLMs: "testimonial diminishing of expertise from the administrative class will be even more ubiquitous: 'but ChatGPT said…'" (X, 2024-08-28).
- **Fluency is mechanized, so teach and assess differently.** "writing on its own can't no longer be taken as a mark of intelligence since it has been provably mechanized. So, we must teach it differently" (X, 2026-05-21). He also asks whether AI-polished prose means the author "did the reasoning" (X, 2026-08-09).
- **Scholarly-publishing failures, from his work as an editor.** Hallucinated references are "a morally unacceptable waste of time … for reviewer or editor" (X, 2026-05-02). But on reference-checking: "AI exposed this vulnerability. Maybe AI can fix it?" (X, 2026-04-02). He says AI "will make our wild world more transparent" (Bluesky, 2025).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Strongly endorses** the "Why is it so hard to talk clearly about AI?" section. "Artificial intelligence simply asserts what is most in dispute" is almost exactly his HDSR 2025 argument, down to the Herbert Simon "complex information processing" citation. He also agrees that "developing a clearer, shared vocabulary is itself one of the concrete … things this Summit can create". "Epistemic technology" is his candidate term.
- **Qualified endorsement of "a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions…".** He agrees the systems are statistical artifacts ("LLMs have no semantics, not even weird ones", X 2024-06-22). But he rejects the *deflation of capability*: artifacts "can carry out … epistemic tasks … in ways that neither we nor any other biological entity could" (HDSR 2025). He objects to lamenting technology "uncritically".
- **Pushes back on the session title "AI as Epistemic Actor"** if "actor" implies mind or agency in the inherited sense. He would accept it in an artifactual sense: epistemic *operations* without intelligence. Expect a friendly amendment to "AI as epistemic technology".
- **Endorses and sharpens** the "black-box problem" and "veracity, trust, opacity". He will insist that transparency obligations target **error: its sources, nature and failure modes**, not just explanations of inner workings.
- **Pushes back on the "human judgment is the scarce, decisive ingredient … keep humans in the loop" paragraph.** His Humphreys/Bacon argument says instruments can and sometimes must decide what counts as evidence. He wants human roles justified epistemically, not by default.
- **Partly endorses the model-collapse worry.** "It's not just 'regression the meh' … but full model collapse. This has serious implications … for some of the uses in science" (X, 2024-07-26).
- **Endorses the fluency-as-cheap-signal argument** (X, 2026-05-21, above).
- **Mildly sceptical of legislative fixes for naming.** On whether law should force companies to drop anthropomorphic product names: "I think anthropomorphic perspectives are silly too, but I don't know if the answer lies in [law]" (X, 2026-05-02).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 2 harvest claims:**
1. "Call it what it is. These are epistemic technologies (artifacts that perform epistemic operations on epistemic content), not intelligences. The vocabulary shift is a governance act."
2. "The black box that matters is *error opacity*. We can measure error rates but not the nature and source of errors. In science, where feedback loops are slow, that makes track-record-based trust ('computational reliabilism') insufficient."
3. "The only trust owed to AI is epistemic trust, earned by epistemic warrants, not by marketing, analogy to aspirin, or user comfort. Until those warrants exist, reliance should be bounded and context-specific."
4. "Epistemic injustice is an AI harm of its own: 'but ChatGPT said…' downgrades human testimony and expertise."

**Session 5 interventions:**
- A **shared vocabulary or glossary** for the manifesto: epistemic technology, epistemic trust vs. reliance, error opacity, epistemic injustice.
- **Error-provenance disclosure norms** for AI used in scholarship: documenting known failure modes and "strange error" classes, and requiring that AI-assisted outputs be traceable for error analysis. Editors should check references and citations, and perhaps AI should help do so.
- **Critical-appropriation programmes rather than abstinence:** scholars and students testing, modding and red-teaming open models ("test the tests with which we test it", X 2024-07-20), and redesigned assessments now that fluency is mechanized.

## 5. Likely dissents he would want recorded

- **Against attributing intelligence, understanding, consciousness or rights to AI.** HDSR 2025: "we would hardly ask questions regarding sentience, social relationships, or legal rights of predictive statistical models with a text output". This would put him against any Salib-inspired AI-rights language. His 2024 post hedges ("rights … best answered by @Floridi's framework on information ethics"), so the dissent is against *mind-based* rights claims, not all discussion.
- **Against blanket "refusal", "abstinence" or anti-AI framing** (Bluesky 2025-10-07; X 2024-07-20; X 2026-06-03).
- **Possibly against an unqualified "humans must stay the final judge of evidence" plank** (HDSR 2025, section on Humphreys and Bacon).

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Peter Salib (tension on AI standing, overlap on method).** Salib's AI-rights agenda is exactly the kind of question Alvarado says dissolves once AI is described operationally. Yet both say capability talk requires no consciousness. Salib: "does not mean machines that are conscious, sentient…" (`peter-salib/by/2026--ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`). Alvarado: "You just don't need consciousness or agency for machines to do Epistemic tasks" (X 2026-05-26). They also disagree on hype. Alvarado thinks "most misconceptions about what AI can and can't do come in fact from AI advocates and hype drivers" (X, 2024-07-23), while Salib's opponents are the capability sceptics.
- **Esther Dyson (partial alliance).** Both see AI as artifact or tool ("It really is an it", Dyson). But Dyson locates opacity in "unexplainable people" and business models (`esther-dyson/by/2025--substack-don-t-fuss-about-training-your-ais-train-your-babies.md`), while he locates it in the artifact's error structure. Complementary rather than opposed. They also differ on literacy: her "theory of business mind" versus his "understand the tech well enough to 'mod' it" (Bluesky 2025-10-07).
- **Concept-note authors (alliance)** on the "talk clearly about AI" section, as detailed in section 3.
- **Kenneth Cukier (historical tension).** Alvarado and Symons 2016 criticised the "death of theory" Big Data camp and quoted Cukier and Mayer-Schönberger as its exemplars (`ramon-alvarado/by/2016--can-we-trust-big-data-applying-philosophy-of-science.md`). Cukier is the closing synthesizer.
- **Sherry Turkle (probable alliance, unverified here):** his loneliness work and his ChatGPT-in-bioethics commentary argue that chat intermediaries short-circuit "the intrinsically interpersonal, critical and reason-giving aspect of healthcare" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2023--chatgpts-relevance-for-bioethics.md`). But he also said AI "may be able to address … epistemic loneliness … only to a certain extent" (X/Bluesky 2026-01-01).

## 7. Distinctive vocabulary

"epistemic technology" / "epistemic enhancer" (`…ai-as-an-epistemic-technology.md`); "epistemic trust" (`…what-kind-of-trust…md`); "essential/insurmountable opacity", "agent-neutral / agent-independent opacity" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2021--explaining-epistemic-opacity.md`, Bluesky); "error-related opacity", "computational reliabilism", "warrant transmission" (`…computational-reliabilism…md`); "strange error" (Bluesky); "artifactual epistemology", "affordance mixtures", "biocentric imaginaries", "fighter jet vs hummingbird" (`…artifactual-epistemology…md`); "epistemic injustice", "testimonial diminishing" (`…epistemic-injustice…md`, X 2024); "epistemic loneliness" / "epistemic partner" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2025--what-is-epistemic-loneliness.md`); "probabilistic stitching" as hallucination (X 2026); "use or refuse" dichotomy; "make them feral" (Bluesky 2025).

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** on vocabulary, epistemic trust, and opacity. These are consistent from 2021 to 2026 across papers and posts.
- **Medium confidence** on concrete governance proposals. He is a conceptual clarifier and has written little policy. He questions vague calls to "regulate big tech" ("What does it mean to 'regulate big tech'? Which tech?", X 2024-05-11) and asks "which people?" about democratic oversight. Expect precision demands more than proposals.
- **Gaps:** the 2022 trust paper, the radiology paper and the book are abstract-only. There are no AV transcripts (the DatOS videos are untranscribed). Social posts include retweets, so only his own posts are quoted here; the Bluesky repost of "weak ass tools" was excluded as not his.
