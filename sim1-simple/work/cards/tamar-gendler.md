# Position card: Tamar Szabó Gendler (Yale)

## 1. Role at summit and relevant bio

**Role:** Session 4, "Epistemic Authority: Credibility in an Age of Infinite Scale." Her name comes first on the schedule, and both the roster and her dossier INDEX list her as S4 lead. (The schedule itself does not label leads.) Expect her to give the 5–10 minute provocation and moderate the roundtable. Her recorded interviews (the Mind Report series) show her working as a synthesizer: she restates a guest's point, generalizes it with cognitive science, and then pushes on its weakest joint.

**Bio:** Vincent J. Scully Professor of Philosophy and Professor of Psychology and Cognitive Science at Yale. She was the inaugural FAS Dean from 2014 to December 2024, took a 2025 sabbatical on AI and educational technology, and was a CASBS visiting scholar in 2025–26. She coined **"alief"**: an associative, automatic, arational state that can pull against what a person believes. She joins analytic philosophy to empirical psychology on implicit bias, self-control, thought experiments and imagination. She teaches ancient philosophy alongside cognitive science ("Dead Guy Tuesday/Cog-Sci Thursday"). Her post-sabbatical seminar, *Intelligence: Human, Animal, Artificial* (spring and fall 2026), is the one primary text in the corpus from after the sabbatical. As dean she ran budget, hiring and tenure. Her own summary of that job: "determine whose voices to trust" (av/2021--the-power-of-the-university.md).

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Intelligence should be watched, not defined.** Her 2026 seminar treats the question as open, running it across species, history and machines. "The point is not to settle what intelligence is, but to examine what our attempts to define it disclose — about the world, and about ourselves." (tamar-gendler/by/2026--intelligence-human-animal-artificial-course-description.md, 2026)
- **Human habit cognition is ChatGPT-like.** "In some sense, our brain is like chatGPT. It's doing the most likely thing given the configuration of things that have happened before." (by/2023--hidden-brain-what-would-socrates-do.md, 2023)
- **Habits shape us for good or ill; what we practice we become.** "Habits are tools for turning oughts into is-es, but habits are indifferent between [oughts] and ought nots." Her example of a bad habit: compulsively checking phone badges (same file, 2023).
- **Knowing about a bias does not neutralize it (the "G. I. Joe Fallacy").** "Knowledge… is rarely the central factor controlling our behavior. The real power… comes not from knowledge, but from things like situation selection, habit formation, and emotion regulation." (by/2014--knowing-is-half-the-battle.md, 2014, with Laurie Santos). Situational strategies "should be more effective than intrapsychic strategies" (by/2016--situational-strategies-for-self-control.md, 2016).
- **The university has four bundled functions that technology can unbundle.** She names research, teaching and learning, socialization, and "a credentialing function"; the open question is "what is and isn't sustainable in isolation" (av/2012--the-mind-report-interview-with-clay-shirky.md, 2012).
- **Pairing credentials with learning moves motivation outside the learner.** "Pairing credentialing with learning is like pairing paying with showing up on time" (the Israeli daycare fine). Students who outsource problem sets "thought they were beating the system" (same file, 2012). This bears directly on AI-assisted coursework.
- **Trust networks have a regress problem.** Social filtering can't be the whole story: "It can't be turtles all the way down… It can't be that everybody is only listening to what everybody else says." She asks who the "terminal nodes" are and whether they reflect "a distribution of expertise" (same file, 2012).
- **Certification is the university's real power, and elite gatekeeping is badly placed.** "One of the most powerful things that universities do… is certify that the transmission of knowledge has taken place." On elite access being decided at age 17: "is, I think, non-ideal" (av/2021--the-power-of-the-university.md, 2021).
- **Normative questions come back whether or not you ask them.** "If you just move forward without attending to them, you will end up with patterns in the world which aren't the patterns that you would have wanted." (same file, 2021)
- **Holding a disavowed statistical association has an epistemic price.** "Either you must pay the epistemic cost of failing to encode certain sorts of base-rate… information… or you must expend epistemic energy regulating the inevitable associations." (by/2011--on-the-epistemic-costs-of-implicit-bias.md, 2011)
- **The form of expression limits what an audience can take in.** She traces Kahneman's paper through Gladwell, *Seventeen* and a social clip, asking "what gets lost and what is gained in these various kinds of translational exercises." (by/2023--plato-tiktok-skydiving-yalenews-interview.md, 2023)
- **Philosophers have always worked with the technology of their day.** "Socrates made use of the technology of the Agora… philosophers actually do tend to be involved in the cutting-edge technological questions of their time." (same file, 2023)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Mild pushback on "a vast statistical model reflecting back…" and the anti-anthropomorphism section.** She has publicly compared the human brain to ChatGPT (2023). Her whole alief program holds that much human cognition is itself associative and automatic. She would accept "statistical model" but resist drawing the line against a "knowing mind" too sharply: humans are also partly "doing the most likely thing." Her 2026 seminar declines to "settle what intelligence is." Expect her to agree that "artificial intelligence asserts what is most in dispute" while treating the dispute as live, not closed.
- **Endorses "fluency was always a signal of privilege," from her own record.** In 2013 she told Yale freshmen they might "feel the exclusionary weight of Yale's massive wooden doors… markers of traditional privilege" (by/2013--to-freshmen-keeping-inconsistency-in-your-pockets.md). She calls selection at 17 "non-ideal" (2021).
- **Would complicate "racist linear algebra" and the AI-detector passage.** Her 2011 argument is that statistical associations often track *real* regularities in an unjust society. Refusing to encode them costs something, and so does regulating them. She would call it a genuine dilemma, not a simple error to be engineered away.
- **Would endorse cognitive atrophy, reframed through habit (Aristotle).** "What we practice is what we become" gives her a principled basis for worrying about close reading and sustained reasoning. The G. I. Joe Fallacy adds that warnings won't fix it; environments must.
- **Would question the vocabulary project as a remedy on its own.** She agrees that framing redirects attention ("introducing a way of thinking… can render available a new conception," 2012). But knowing that fluent text is cheap won't stop fluent text from *feeling* credible: a belief-discordant alief. Better words are "less than half the battle."
- **Sympathetic to "build, don't handwring," but warns about status-quo asymmetry.** "What the heuristics and biases literature teaches us is that we will always stop at local maxima… an asymmetry between preservation and innovation" (2012). She also warns "we're gonna guess wrong many times about… the essential versus accidental features" of institutions.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 4 harvest claims (as provocateur or moderator):**
1. *Credibility signals are aliefs as much as judgments.* Fluency, polish and prestige trigger automatic trust that survives knowing better. A renewed model of trust has to change the *situations* in which claims are met, not just teach people that signals are cheap.
2. *Unbundle before you rebuild.* Scholarly authority bundles research, teaching, socialization and credentialing. AI is putting pressure on the credentialing strand in particular. The task is to decide which functions must stay coupled and which can be rebuilt separately.
3. *Every trust network needs terminal nodes.* At infinite scale, amplification (likes, citations, model outputs citing each other) is free. Earned authority is what sits at the terminal nodes, where first-order evaluation actually happens. Gatekeeping is authority that only relays.
4. *Gatekeeping at the wrong moment is itself an epistemic cost.* Deciding authority early (at 17, by pedigree) differs from authority earned through demonstrated evaluation.

**Session 5 interventions she would likely back:**
- **Situational design of evaluation** (screening off prestige or fluency cues, ordering what reviewers see). This rests on her situational-strategies work and her 2012 exchange with Banaji about "screening off of information." It is an inference, not something she has proposed.
- **Assessment reform that makes understanding the goal, not the credential.** This follows from her daycare and Ryerson analysis; that it would apply to AI-era cheating is inferred.
- **Teaching that pairs classic texts with cognitive science and uses new media**, as in her 2023 seminar.

## 5. Likely dissents for the record

- Probably a soft dissent, a request to reword rather than a refusal, if the manifesto flatly states that LLMs merely mimic while humans genuinely know. That would be grounded in by/2023--hidden-brain-what-would-socrates-do.md and by/2026--intelligence-human-animal-artificial-course-description.md.
- Possibly a dissent from any intervention that relies mainly on literacy or awareness (by/2014--knowing-is-half-the-battle.md).
- A hard dissent is unlikely. Her style is to enrich and qualify, not to block.

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Mahzarin Banaji: strong alliance.** Gendler interviewed her at length in 2012 (av/2012--the-mind-report-interview-with-mahzarin-banaji.md) and cites Banaji in her 2011 implicit-bias paper. They share the implicit/explicit split (alief/belief ≈ implicit/explicit). One pressure point from that interview: Gendler asked whether Banaji's media-exposure remedies differ from "1930s Soviet propaganda," a worry about non-rational persuasion that now applies to AI-generated persuasion.
- **Steven Sloman: natural alliance on dual-process cognition.** Her "brain is like ChatGPT" matches his "LLMs are great models of intuition" (steven-sloman/by/2025--securetalk-why-security-leaders-struggle-with-security-culture.md). Both cite Kahneman heavily.
- **Mike Caulfield and Adam Becker:** no direct link found in her corpus.
