# Henry Farrell — position card

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Session 1 ("The Epistemic Landscape"), listed first; the roster and his dossier INDEX mark him as S1 lead, so he probably gives the provocation.** He is SNF Agora Institute Professor of International Affairs at Johns Hopkins SAIS and works in political economy: trust and institutions (*The Political Economy of Trust*, 2009) and, with Abraham Newman, "weaponized interdependence" (*Underground Empire*, 2023). Since 2023 his main public project has been reframing AI. With Alison Gopnik, Cosma Shalizi and James Evans (also an attendee) he co-wrote "Large AI models are cultural and social technologies" (*Science*, 2025). He also wrote "AI as Governance" (*Annual Review of Political Science*, 2025/26), gave the Oxford Astor lecture "AI as Social Technology" (2026), and writes about AI weekly in his Substack *Programmable Mutter*. He is Shalizi's long-time co-author and a colleague of Pomerantsev at SNF Agora. In the room he will talk like a political economist and institutionalist, not a technologist or an ethicist.

## 2. Core recent positions

1. **LLMs are cultural and social technologies, not agents or proto-AGI.** "Large Models should not be viewed primarily as intelligent agents, but as a new kind of cultural and social technology, allowing humans to take advantage of information other humans have accumulated." `henry-farrell/by/2025--large-ai-models-are-cultural-and-social-technologies.md` (2025)
2. **They are lossy summarizers, like prices and bureaucratic categories.** "Large Models are 'lossy JPEGs' of the data corpora on which they have been trained"; hallucinations are endemic "because they have no conception of truth and falsity." Same file (2025).
3. **The real stakes are political economy: who gets what, and concentrated ownership.** "Concentrated power may make it easier for those who own the systems to skim the benefits of efficiency at the expense of others." Same file (2025). Also: "AI weakens the position of those upon whom it is used and who provide its data."
4. **LLMs depend on human knowledge, so its producers must be paid.** "LLMs should not be viewed as a substitute for high quality human generated knowledge. They should instead be viewed as an obligate complement to such knowledge." And "enough of the proceeds need to go to the actual knowledge producers for the system to be self-sustaining." `henry-farrell/by/2024--pm-the-map-is-eating-the-territory-the-political-economy-o.md` (2024)
5. **Homogenization is the core cultural danger, more than recursive "model collapse."** He found NotebookLM "took an argument that was at least notionally surprising, and yanked it hard in the direction of banality." He applies this to science: AI peer review will "quietly and simply strain [novel claims] out." `henry-farrell/by/2024--pm-after-software-eats-the-world-what-comes-out-the-other.md` (2024)
6. **Organizational rituals that assess people (peer review, recommendation letters) lose legitimacy once they are automated.** "Letters of recommendation, peer reviews and even scientific papers themselves will become less trustworthy." `henry-farrell/by/2024--pm-a-new-piece-in-the-economist-there-s-a-killer-app-for-l.md` (2024, with Marion Fourcade)
7. **Cheap text breaks costly signals** (pooling vs. separating equilibria). "LLMs make it much more difficult than in the past to distinguish sincere hard effort from automated boilerplate." `henry-farrell/social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl` (own post, 2025-11-25)
8. **He rejects both triumphalism and "AI-con" denialism, and weights triumphalism as the bigger danger.** "Neither AI Rationalism or AI-Con Thought is all that helpful... We ought to be much more worried about the claims of the triumphalists than the denialists." `henry-farrell/by/2026--pm-ai-has-limits-even-if-many-ai-people-can-t-see-them.md` (2026). Also: "We need a regeared humanism that understands the technologies as they've unfolded." `social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl` (2026-03-29)
9. **Human judgment can't be optimized away, because trade-offs are political.** Automation should go "up to the point, and only up to the point that it supports the capacities of... managers and rank-and-file to exercise individual human judgment." `henry-farrell/by/2026--pm-ai-isn-t-management-try-explaining-that-to-matthew-prin.md` (2026)
10. **Using more AI to fix AI's problems causes arms races.** "Abelard Snazz believes that the solution is always more robots, even when robot solutionism is obviously the root cause of the problem." `henry-farrell/by/2026--pm-the-downside-of-robot-solutionism.md` (2026)
11. **The problem is malformed publics, not individual misinformation.** "The fundamental problem... is not that social media misinforms individuals... but that it creates publics with malformed collective understandings." `henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-we-re-getting-the-social-media-crisis-wrong.md` (2025). He treats AI-misinformation fears as possibly "overblown," but flags the "modal drift" risk that plausible LLM errors get past human vigilance. `henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-markets-bureaucracy-democracy-ai.md` (2025); `henry-farrell/by/2024--pm-kevin-roose-s-shoggoth.md` (2024)
12. **Countervailing power and organized interests matter more than deliberative mini-publics.** He calls the OpenAI-style deliberation grants "democracy, only without the politics." SAG-AFTRA is his model: "Don't moan, organize!" `henry-farrell/by/2023--pm-there-s-a-model-for-democratizing-ai.md` (2023); `henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-the-ai-democracy-debate-is-weirdly-narrow.md` (2025); `2024--pm-the-map-is-eating...` (2024)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Strong endorsement: "AI didn't create these problems... accelerating."** His whole framing is historical continuity with print, markets and bureaucracy. Each of those "provoked justified concerns about the spread of misinformation and bias" (Science 2025). He also points out that "the crisis in everything from local newspapers to academic journals" came before LLMs.
- **Endorses the misnomer and vocabulary critique.** He uses "AI" "under intellectual duress" (`2024--pm-the-map-is-eating...`). The Shalizi guest post he ran prefers Simon's "complex information processing" to "artificial intelligence" (`henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-on-feral-library-card-catalogs-or-aware-of-all-internet.md`; that is Shalizi's text on Farrell's newsletter). He will back the note's proposal for a shared vocabulary.
- **He is an ally of "a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people whose writing it was trained on," but he complicates it in two ways.**
  - *Not "intentions," and not a faithful reflection.* The model is a lossy, centripetal compression. It is most accurate on the common case and strips out the rare and new. Structuralist readings he takes seriously (Weatherby) say the outputs have "no direct intentionality behind them" and are still "a kind of culture" (`2025--pm-large-language-models-are-cultural-technologies-what-mi.md`).
  - *Not only a mirror, but a social technology that reorganizes what it summarizes.* Like markets and bureaucracies, it "reorganize[s], transform[s], and restructure[s]." He pushed back when Blaise read the thesis as "card catalogs": "cultural technologies can be, and very often are generative systems that discover and use deep structures" (`social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2026-01-19). Expect him to call the concept note's phrase too deflationary on capability and too silent on power.
- **Endorses: the hype machine and marketing spend, and "separate what these systems can do from 'five years away.'"** See his "feral thought experiments" critique of AI-2027-style futurism (`2026--pm-our-future-is-being-devoured-by-feral-thought-experimen.md`).
- **Endorses the "writing is now cheaper than reading" / effort-as-signal section.** His costly-signalling posts (2025) and his ritual-legitimacy piece (2024) are almost the same argument.
- **Endorses the enclosure / unpaid-labor / "ultimate annotators" section, with a political twist.** "Obligate complement" gives the economic case. He will insist that recognition comes through organized bargaining power, not goodwill.
- **Pushes back on "model collapse" as half of the vicious circle.** He calls the recursion result culturally resonant more than scientifically decisive: "I suspect that it owed its success more to its cultural resonances than its scientific results" (`2024--pm-after-software-eats...`). He will swap in homogenization plus human–model feedback loops.
- **Qualifies "cognitive atrophy."** The corpus has no Farrell text on atrophy as such. His framework is Simon/Mercier: individual cognition was always limited, and collective institutions do the thinking. So he will probably (a) warn that individual-brain atrophy talk resembles Socrates-on-writing moral panic and (b) move the question to institutions. Where is judgment-bearing work being hollowed out (managers, peer reviewers, street-level bureaucrats)? How are publics being malformed? The closest direct texts: "it is not the souls of AI that we are reshaping, but our own ones" (Bluesky 2026-03-29), and Shalizi's worry about "access to the exoteric forms of tradition, without internalizing the habits that went along with them" (2025 guest post).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 1 harvest claims:**
- "Stop arguing about whether it's intelligent. LLMs are a new cultural and social technology, like print, markets and bureaucracy, and the questions are who controls them and who gets what."
- "The main epistemic risk is centripetal homogenization, not noise. Models strain out the rare and the novel, which is the lifeblood of science and scholarship."
- "Cheap fluent text is collapsing costly signals and the legitimacy of assessment rituals (peer review, letters). That is structural, not moral panic."
- "Disinformation is partly overblown. The deeper damage is to collective institutions and publics, and to the bargaining position of knowledge producers."

**Session 5 interventions:**
- Organize knowledge producers (scholarly societies, publishers, libraries, Wikimedia) as a **countervailing power** that bargains collectively over training use and proceeds, on the SAG-AFTRA model.
- A **joint social science + CS research program** on LLMs as cultural technologies. For example, test whether AI review can *recognize novelty*, and build diversity-preserving model ecologies (with Evans).
- A **norm limiting automation of judgment roles** (peer review, editorial decisions) to support, not substitution, with a warning against AI-vs-AI arms races such as detectors.

## 5. Likely dissents for the manifesto

- Against a **"model collapse" headline**. He would reframe it as homogenization (`2024--pm-after-software-eats...`).
- Against **individual "cognitive atrophy" as the frame**, if it slides into Socratic moral panic. He would want collective/institutional deskilling instead (inferred; see §3).
- Against **deliberative or sortition-based "AI governance" proposals that give no binding power** (`2025--pm-the-ai-democracy-debate-is-weirdly-narrow.md`; `2023--pm-there-s-a-model-for-democratizing-ai.md`).
- Against **techno-fixes to AI-created problems** (AI detectors, AI reviewing AI) (`2026--pm-the-downside-of-robot-solutionism.md`).
- Mild: against wording that suggests LLMs are *merely* parrots or mirrors (Bluesky 2026-01-19, 2026-03-29).

## 8. Confidence and gaps

**High confidence** on the cultural-technology thesis, political economy and compensation, homogenization, signal and ritual collapse, anti-doom/anti-boom, and the countervailing-power approach to democracy. These are repeated across 2023–26.

**Medium confidence** on cognitive atrophy. There is no direct text, so his position is inferred from the Simon/Mercier collective-cognition framing and the "our own souls" post. Also medium on how hard he will push against model collapse: he is skeptical, not dismissive.

**Gaps:**
- "AI as Governance" (Annual Review) appears in full only through the PM repost.
- The Shalizi–Farrell AI-and-bureaucracy paper is forthcoming and not in the corpus.
- Only ~300 of 2,112 Crooked Timber posts were read in this pass (corpus selection).
- No explicit statements on open access, preprints or scholarly publishing economics beyond peer review.
