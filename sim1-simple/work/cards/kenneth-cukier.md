# Position card: Kenneth Cukier

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Role:** Co-leads Session 6, "Closing Synthesis," with Kara Miller: a 15–20 minute "sensemaker synthesis" covering "what emerged, what remains contested, what demands action," followed by an open floor. His framing is likely to shape the language of the manifesto more than almost anyone's except the conveners'.

**Bio:** Cukier is Deputy Executive Editor of *The Economist*. He has worked there two decades as tech correspondent, Tokyo correspondent, data editor and commentary editor, and he ran the 2018 "Open Future" project and hosted the *Babbage* science podcast. He co-wrote *Big Data* (2013) and *Framers: Human Advantage in an Age of Technology and Turmoil* (2021), both with Mayer-Schönberger, the latter also with de Véricourt. He is a Quaker, advises AIandFaith.org, and teaches Woodbrooke courses on AI and religion ("Angel or Armageddon?"). He is writing a solo book on spirituality and AI and co-runs a "Chief Word Officer" newsletter on writing with AI. In 2025–26 he spoke on AI and war at Davos-side events and the UN AI for Good summit ("Encode the Geneva Conventions into AI weapons"). On the question of AI he is a **pro-AI humanist**: bullish on machine learning for discovery, firm that humans have capacities machines lack.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **"AI" is a misnomer.** "GENIUS. And yes, AI is not about intelligence and not artificial." (`kenneth-cukier/social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-10-30)
- **Humans are "framers" and machines are not.** Humans have "an innate sense of causality, counterfactuals and constraints, which AI lacks." (x-timeline, 2024-02-01). "LLMs hallucinate, underscoring the fact that they have no inherent sense of causation." (`kenneth-cukier/by/2024--aiandfaith-featured-interview-with-kenneth-cukier.md`, 2024)
- **LLMs work because of the humans in their training data.** "When LLMs produce the strong answers, it's because of the training data — which itself embodies people's mental models... Human frames are a part of AI systems that make them work." (AI&F interview, 2024). Also: "Let's celebrate the training data (ie, us, marvelous humans)." (x-timeline, 2024-03-07)
- **LLMs reflect text, not the world.** "The 'world model' is a reflection of the text, not authentic to reality - and like a house of mirrors, it's prone to awful distortions." (x-timeline, 2023-11-22). "The data (text) is only a tiny reflection of reality." (x-timeline, 2023-08-16)
- **Machine learning in science can see what humans cannot.** "There are already scores of papers where AI exceeds human knowledge, notably Google's 2018 retina-reveals-heart-disease paper." (x-timeline, 2024-06-21). In 2018: "I fear a world without AI." (`kenneth-cukier/av/2018--why-i-fear-a-world-without-ai.md`)
- **Cognitive atrophy is real, and he says so with gallows humour.** "Four-fifths of ChatGPT users couldn't quote from essays they wrote minutes earlier... Yes, our civilization is doomed." (x-timeline, 2025-07-13)
- **AI output is average, so trusted brands matter more.** "AI delivers the median; excellence is a 'tail event'. Outlets will need to distinguish themselves more than ever." (x-timeline, 2023-10-10, on AI in media)
- **Against the utopian/doomer split; judge each case.** He regrets people "pro tech versus anti tech — utopians and doomers — without discriminating... 'what tech specifically' and 'for what usage' and 'by whom'?" Quoting Niebuhr, technology "makes good people better and bad people worse." (AI&F, 2024)
- **Frame pluralism is the answer to epistemic conflict.** "All frames, even bad ones, should be tolerated save for one — a frame that denies other frames." (AI&F, 2024). "We want pluralism... so that these frames that are in tension actually do get a chance to clash and tussle." (`kenneth-cukier/av/2021--finding-our-way-through-challenges.md`)
- **Open infrastructure, with calibrated secrecy.** On an arXiv third-party service: "The services layer has always been open, so outsiders built on it. No permission to ask; no royalty to pay. Perfect!" (x-timeline, 2024-09-08). But against blanket algorithmic disclosure: for basic applications "it's a valuable trade secret... [high-stakes uses] can be open to public authorities but not rivals." (x-timeline, 2023-04-04)
- **Nothing is inevitable.** Takeaways from a 2025 interview: "1. nothing is inevitable; 2. AGI is impossible yet we already have superintelligence." (x-timeline, 2025-01-18)
- **Every information explosion has needed new intermediaries.** "After Gutenberg invented printing... there was an explosion of information. It took the creation of a new intermediary... which we now call a journalist – to make sense of it." (`kenneth-cukier/by/2012--no1shimbun-hacks-and-hackers-data-driven-journalism.md`, 2012)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **"'Artificial intelligence' simply asserts what is most in dispute":** **He would endorse this** (tweet of 2025-10-30). He also agrees that LLMs mirror their training text ("reflection," "house of mirrors").
- **"A vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people whose writing it was trained on":** **He would half-endorse this.** He agrees about LLMs, but would resist extending it to "AI" generally. He separates deep learning on scans and data ("I had in mind deep-learning applied to medical scans," 2024-02-04) and stresses discovery ("AI exceeds human knowledge").
- **The note's tone on the tech industry (Bond villains, "halo has tarnished," "we come to it cynical"):** **He would push back.** In 2022 he called Microsoft and Google "the white hats" ("Not perfect. But they're the white hats," x-timeline 2022-11-16). He treats cynicism as a failure of discernment. In 2018 he argued "we might have a moral obligation to actually use personal information for the social good" and that privacy regulators "should" have "applauded" DeepMind (`av/2018--why-i-fear...`).
- **"Human judgment... becomes the scarce, decisive ingredient":** **He would strongly endorse this.** It is the thesis of *Framers*. He would re-voice it as causality, counterfactuals and constraints.
- **Cognitive atrophy:** **He would endorse this** (the essay-quoting tweet), and he dislikes "workslop" (2025-10-03).
- **Model collapse:** nothing in the corpus.
- **"Ultimate annotators":** **He would endorse this.** RLHF is "the very essence of 'constraints'" and a human contribution (AI&F).
- **Historical arc and durable new institutions:** **He would endorse this.** His Gutenberg-to-journalist line in 2012 is the same story.
- **Opacity and "concentration of power":** **He would complicate this.** He has argued for trading explainability for performance ("allow a little bit of faith in the algorithm as long as it's constantly being revalidated," 2018) and against blanket transparency mandates (2023). In 2018, though, he framed "man versus algorithm" as liberalism's 21st-century struggle (`by/2018--economist-technology-and-its-discontents.md`).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Framing of the closing synthesis (predicted):** He is likely to organize the day around **human framing as the scarce input**: what machines cannot do (causality, counterfactuals, constraints), and therefore what institutions must protect and reward. Likely moves:
- A **both/and "pro-AI, pro-human"** frame (his own phrase, 2025-09-05) that rejects both "utopians and doomers." He will probably praise the note's "neither complacency nor techno-doom."
- An **Economist-style triage** into what emerged, what is contested and what demands action. He will probably be generous to disagreement ("think it possible you may be mistaken," Cromwell via Quakerism, AI&F), casting contests as frames that must "clash and tussle."
- **"Nothing is inevitable"** as the call to action, and "training data is us" as the moral basis for compensating human curators.
- Wit and aphorism: "AI delivers the median," "house of mirrors," "all logos, no mythos" (x-timeline, 2024-01-22).

**Likely Session 5 interventions:**
1. **Invest in human judgment, not only detection.** Build training, tools and incentives that strengthen framing, such as reward structures for review and curation. This follows his claim that human frames are why LLMs work.
2. **Keep open, permissionless "service layers" on scholarly infrastructure** (the arXiv tweet), paired with graduated transparency to public authorities for high-stakes algorithms rather than full public disclosure (2023).
3. **For Q3 ("who isn't in the room"): faith communities and humanists.** "There's a major role for faith groups to have a voice in the public sphere to shape the evolution of the technology." (AI&F, 2024). Possibly also practitioners in data and ML for science.

## 5. Likely dissents to record

- **Against deflationary or anti-industry framing** if the manifesto calls AI merely "statistics" across the board, or treats big tech as uniformly malign (x-timeline 2022-11-16, 2024-06-21; `av/2018--why-i-fear...`). As a synthesizer he may voice this as "contested" rather than dissent.
- **Against strong transparency or anti-data mandates** (x-timeline 2023-04-04/05; the 2026-02 debate with Carissa Véliz on data trading: "the value of data (with ethical practices) is still the right one").
- **Against AGI claims.** He would want the manifesto to deny machine consciousness or "complete AGI" (x-timeline 2024-04-19: "Complete AGI is impossible and a machine can never be conscious because humans have the capacity for spirituality"). This is idiosyncratic, and he may reserve it for his own voice.

- **Medium confidence** on how he would handle scholarly-publishing specifics. The corpus has almost nothing on peer review, open access or copyright and AI training (only the 2006 biotech-IP paper and the arXiv tweet).
- **Gaps:** no *Babbage* transcripts; no Economist journalism 2019–26; the 2025 Index on Censorship piece "Is AI friend or foe?" was not fetched; the 2025 *Digital Disruption* episode ("Go All In on AI") has no transcript. His 2018 pro-data, anti-privacy-regulation stance may have softened: he now emphasizes "ethical practices."
