# Position card: Selena Deckelmann

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Role:** Session 1 participant ("The Epistemic Landscape"). She shares the table with Katherine Maher (former Wikimedia Foundation CEO), Leslie Chan, Peter Pomerantsev and lead Henry Farrell.

**Bio:** Deckelmann has been Chief Product and Technology Officer (CPTO) of the Wikimedia Foundation since August 2022. She runs the product and engineering teams behind Wikipedia, Commons and Wikidata, including site reliability under AI-crawler load. Before that she spent years at Mozilla, rising from crash-data analyst to SVP responsible for Firefox, and earlier was a PostgreSQL contributor and community builder (PDXPUG, Open Source Bridge, Postgres Open, Ada Initiative advisor). She is an engineer-executive, not an academic. Her public voice since 2023 is institutional and pragmatic: Wikipedia as *human-generated, human-governed* knowledge whose value rises in a machine-generated internet, and whose infrastructure and volunteer base are now under strain. Her 2006–2017 personal blog is mostly technical and community material. She has no scholarly-publishing track record.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Wikipedia's value is its human process, not its text.** "Wikipedia contains trustworthy, reliably sourced knowledge *because* it is created, debated, and curated by people." The answer to whether AI could replace it: "No — it would not be the same" (`selena-deckelmann/by/2023--wmf-wikipedias-value-in-age-of-generative-ai.md`, 2023).
- **Model collapse makes human content scarce and valuable.** "for LLMs to be good and to get better, they'll need a steady supply of original content, written by humans, making Wikipedia and other sources of human generated content even more valuable" (same, 2023). She cited the model-collapse paper by name.
- **AI must augment, never replace, human knowledge creation, and must credit it.** LLMs "should not ever impede or replace the human creation of knowledge. This can be done by always keeping humans in the loop and properly crediting their contributions." She lists three principles: Sustainability, Equity, Transparency (same, 2023).
- **Openness is not negotiable. The ask is for reciprocity.** "our systems as our content being free, but the infrastructure is not." Training on Wikimedia content "is within the licenses"; companies should attribute, use Wikimedia Enterprise, and "think about paying to support the future of Wikipedia" (`selena-deckelmann/by/2025--marketplace-tech-ai-search-vs-human-search.md`, 2025).
- **She rejects paywalls and pay-per-crawl.** On Cloudflare's pay-per-crawl: "we're not gonna put a pay per article blocker … up. That's, like, not a thing that we're gonna do." She wants "new norms for developers" and APIs instead. On bots: "We must prioritize the human traffic" (`selena-deckelmann/av/2025--wikimania-2025-ai-keynote-panel-technology-in-a-changing-wor.md`, 2025).
- **Crawler load is a real, measured harm to human access.** "when we exceed our capacity … it impacts human access to knowledge"; crawler traffic has "saturated our network links" (Marketplace 2025; Wikimania 2025).
- **Disintermediation erodes the volunteer and donor base.** "Wikipedia pageviews have been declining … with fewer visits to Wikipedia, fewer volunteers may grow and enrich the content, and fewer individual donors may support this work" (`selena-deckelmann/by/2025--diff-shaping-2026-2027-annual-goals.md`, Dec 2025).
- **The human curators are thinning, which predates AI.** Extended-rights users matter more than ever "yet their numbers are shrinking on the largest Wikipedias" (Dec 2025). In 2023 she cited record-low active admins (`selena-deckelmann/by/2023--wcna-speech.md`).
- **AI will be weaponized over truth.** "Truth is more contested than ever before, and AI will be weaponized in fights to define truth online" (`selena-deckelmann/by/2024--meta-multigenerational-letter.md`, 2024).
- **She is wary of moral panic, from institutional memory.** "some pundits treat every big tech change as the impending death of Wikipedia. And over two decades later, that just has not been true" (`2023--wcna-speech.md`).
- **AI should practice "radical inclusion" and be multilingual.** "Wikipedia is the internet's chisel"; AI builders should "start practicing radical inclusion"; "It also means being profoundly multilingual"; "knowledge is human" (`selena-deckelmann/av/2024--why-ai-should-be-more-like-wikipedia.md`, TED 2024, ASR transcript).
- **Responsible AI can mean not using AI.** "one of the responsible uses of AI, like not using it." She gives an example of community-governed practice: human review of machine translation, and small, locally produced or self-hosted models such as WMF's "Mint" (Wikimania 2025).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Endorse:** the model-collapse and human-scarcity logic ("future models train on what earlier AI produced…"). She made the same argument in July 2023 and turned it into a sustainability demand on AI companies.
- **Endorse:** "human judgment … becomes the scarce, decisive ingredient." This matches her human-in-the-loop, revertible ML tools and the WMF CEO's "'Human-led, tech-enabled' means that the humans still lead", which her 2024 letter links to.
- **Endorse:** the note's statistical, non-anthropomorphic framing, at least partly. She herself scare-quotes what an LLM "knows" (2023). But she speaks of "AI" and its duties loosely in talks, so she is not a vocabulary purist.
- **Endorse with qualification:** "a commons increasingly subject to enclosure … extractive pressures that AI training brings". She agrees the infrastructure is being strained and free-ridden. She rejects enclosure as a *remedy*: the content stays free for commercial use, and what should be paid for is infrastructure and attribution.
- **Push back:** "who pays … fair compensation". She favours voluntary reciprocity, norms, attribution and paid API products over licensing restrictions or metering.
- **Push back (probable):** "researchers, scholarly publishers … the ultimate annotators." Her model of trusted curation is *open, uncredentialed volunteers* ("anyone could edit … radically inclusive"). She would widen "annotators" to Wikipedians and communities, and would care about recognition more than payment. Her 2025 planning asks how to give editors "more feedback and recognition". *Inference; she has not addressed the phrase.*
- **Push back (tone):** the note's cynicism about the tech industry. She is a career tech insider who frames AI as "harnessing fire" (quoting Audrey Tang), and she says "There's nothing inevitable about new technology". That is agency, not doom.
- **Endorse:** "AI detectors … non-native speakers". It fits her emphasis on multilingualism and her report that bad machine translation creates cleanup work for communities (Wikimania 2025).
- **No evidence** on cognitive atrophy.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 1 harvest claims (2–4):**
1. **Genuinely new:** AI answer engines disintermediate the commons. People consume Wikipedia content without visiting, which cuts the flow of new volunteers and donors that produces the human content models depend on. The model-collapse loop runs *through* the commons's business model.
2. **Measurable, not panic:** crawler traffic that ignores human interest patterns and defeats caching is an infrastructure cost the commons carries for commercial AI. The distinction to draw is that content is free but infrastructure is not.
3. **Pre-existing, now accelerating:** shrinking cohorts of admins and patrollers, trouble retaining newcomers, contested truth, regulatory and censorship pressure. AI sharpens each.
4. **Equity gap:** what LLMs "know" excludes offline, pre-internet and non-English knowledge. That amplifies existing inequities.

**Session 5 interventions (1–3):**
- **Norms and infrastructure for responsible reuse:** crawlers identify themselves, use APIs (e.g. Wikimedia Enterprise) instead of scraping, attribute in AI products, and fund the upstream commons.
- **Transparency and community control of AI tools:** published model documentation and performance by demographic, revertible machine edits, community decisions on deployment, and hosting small or local language models (Mint) for under-served languages.
- **Recognition and pipelines for human contributors:** newcomer onboarding, tools for patrollers, recognition. These keep the human source "multigenerational".

## 5. Likely dissents to record

- Against paywalls, pay-per-crawl, or licence restrictions on open content as the answer to extraction (`av/2025--wikimania…`; `by/2025--marketplace…`).
- Possibly against a credentialed or publisher-centred definition of "trusted human curation" that sidelines open volunteer communities (`av/2024--why-ai-should-be-more-like-wikipedia.md`). This is weaker and inferred.

## 6. Tensions / alliances with attendees

- **Katherine Maher (S1):** former WMF CEO (2016–21), with a TED talk on "What Wikipedia teaches us about balancing truth and beliefs" (`katherine-maher/INDEX.md`). A natural ally on Wikipedia's consensus model as an answer to epistemic polarization. Deckelmann's TED talk makes the same argument about polarized editors producing more neutral articles.
- **Leslie Chan (S1):** allies on multilingualism, equity, and community-governed commons. His co-authored paper cites Wikipedia as a quality commons without huge funds (`leslie-chan/by/2023--mastodon-over-mammon…`). Tension: Chan asks "Who benefits from unfettered openness?" while she affirms that commercial AI training "support[s] our mission".
- **David Weinberger (S3):** sympathetic. He proposes chat AIs that summarize Wikipedia and check against reliable sources (`david-weinberger/by/2023--kmworld-truth-lies-and-large-language-models.md`) and has posted in defence of Wikipedia against political attacks (Bluesky, 2025-06).
- **Alex Springer (Open Attribution) and Nick Vincent (marketplace talks):** her standing ask for attribution and for AI firms to support upstream data sources converges with an attribution and data-value agenda. The link is by affiliation only; no direct interaction is in the corpus.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** on the WMF institutional stance: human-centred AI, open licensing plus reciprocity, crawler strain, sustainability.
- **Medium** on her personal views beyond WMF talking points. Most 2024–26 text is institutional (Diff planning posts, interviews). The TED and Wikimania transcripts are ASR (mildly lossy).
- **Gaps:** the 404 Media episode (Jan 2026, "How Wikipedia Will Survive in the Age of AI") has no transcript. Her Mastodon is uncollected. X posts are sparse on AI. There is nothing on scholarly publishing, peer review, cognitive atrophy, or the concept note's hype critique.
- Expect her to speak as a builder with operational data (traffic, admins, pageviews) rather than theory. She is constructive and consensus-seeking. Her 2011 post "Where meritocracy fails" shows a long-standing view that apparent merit masks privilege, which fits the note's fluency-as-privilege point (`by/2011--where-meritocracy-fails.md`).
