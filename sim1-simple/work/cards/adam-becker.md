# Position card: Adam Becker

## 1. Role at summit and bio

**Role:** Participant in Session 4, "Epistemic Authority: Credibility in an Age of Infinite Scale" (with Gendler, Banaji, Caulfield and Sloman). He is the room's hardest critic of AI hype, and he won't sit neutrally in any "AI could help fix science" framing.

**Bio:** Freelance science journalist. PhD in computational cosmology (Michigan, 2012). Author of *What Is Real?* (2018) and *More Everything Forever: AI Overlords, Space Empires, and Silicon Valley's Crusade to Control the Fate of Humanity* (2025). He has held posts as Science Journalism Fellow at the Santa Fe Institute (Krakauer's institution) and Science Communicator in Residence at the Simons Institute. Since April 2026 he has hosted the weekly podcast *Dreaming Against the Machine*, "about what a realistic good future might look like" (`adam-becker/by/2026--personal-site-about-page.md`). His recent output is almost entirely about tech-billionaire ideology, AGI and superintelligence claims, EA and longtermism, and the energy costs of AI. In his 2026 *Scientific American* cover story he applied the same hype-versus-reality method to quantum computing.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **The danger is the people building AI, not AI itself. Doomers and accelerationists share one fantasy.** "The real existential threat isn't AI—it's the powerful people building it." (`adam-becker/by/2025--atlantic-useful-idiots-of-ai-doomsaying.md`, 2025). He calls the rationalist/EA and e/acc camps "both living in the same fantasy world" (`adam-becker/social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-04-08).
- **LLMs are text generators with no conception of the world. Hallucination is their normal operation.** "Hallucinations ultimately derive from the fact that LLMs aren't capable of distinguishing between truth and falsehood. They are just machines that generate what the philosopher Harry Frankfurt called 'bullshit.'" (`…atlantic-useful-idiots…`, 2025). Also: "They're constantly hallucinating. That's all they do." (`adam-becker/by/2025--rollingstone-qa-what-is-up-with-these-tech-billionaires.md`, 2025).
- **"AI" is a marketing term, and "AGI" is vague on purpose.** "Calling the systems that we have now 'AI' is a kind of marketing tool." And: "'AGI' is ill-defined, and the vagueness is strategically useful for the people who talk about this stuff." (Rolling Stone, 2025). He prefers "text generation engine". His description of the output is "homogenized thought-like product" (`adam-becker/av/--radio-better-offline-adam-becker.md`, n.d., ASR).
- **Anthropomorphism is pareidolia. He goes back to ELIZA.** "Claude isn't cheating any more than Claude is thinking." (Atlantic, 2025). ELIZA and pareidolia recur in Rolling Stone (2025) and Better Offline.
- **Intelligence is not a single scalable quantity, and it is social.** "intelligence is really a feature of communities or cultures rather than individual people" (`adam-becker/by/2025--cspan-booktv-more-everything-forever-transcript.md`, 2025). He rejects "scale is all you need" and synthetic data: "Like, dude, that... Why, why would it? No." (Better Offline).
- **He rejects the claim that AI accelerates science.** Billionaires "must believe that AI can be used to … accelerate scientific research, despite all evidence to the contrary." (`adam-becker/by/2025--guardian-tech-oligarchs-gambling-our-future.md`, 2025). He also amplified the line "AI has generally been less successful and revolutionary in science than it appears to be" (bluesky, 2025-05-20) and a paper "showing that LLMs are always going to make factual errors" (bluesky, 2025-09-21).
- **LLMs are ethically indefensible to use, for three reasons.** "LLMs and generative AI are fruit of the poisoned tree". The three reasons are plagiarism, ecological cost, and "the exploitation and psychological damage inflicted on the people who do content moderation work on these models" (bluesky, 2025-05-21). "They're ethically indefensible." (same thread).
- **He personally refuses LLMs, citing cognitive habit.** "I don't use ChatGPT or any LLMs in my work because I want my writing to sound like me… But also, writing is thinking, and thinking is a habit." (bluesky, 2025-11-08; 514 likes).
- **Tech wealth is not expertise.** Tech leaders "have a great contempt for expertise." Replacing workers happens "because the executive class is in the grip of a mass delusion" (`adam-becker/by/2025--arstechnica-qa-silicon-valley-billionaires-want-the-impossible.md`, 2025). On being a tech billionaire: "all that being a tech billionaire really means for sure is that you know a lot about being rich" (C-SPAN, 2025).
- **Credibility can be laundered through juxtaposition and hidden funding.** In his Thiel-funded *Inference* exposé he wrote that "The inclusion of demonstrably pseudoscientific writing alongside the work of highly regarded researchers puts the two on equal footing" (`adam-becker/by/2019--undark-junk-science-or-the-real-thing-inference.md`, 2019).
- **AI degrades shared truth.** To ProPublica: "AI degrades our communal understanding of what truth is. Good journalism relies on that." (bluesky, 2025-12-17).
- **His remedies are political, not technical.** Organize labor, use "informed, factually accurate public ridicule," and "tax their wealth away" (Rolling Stone, 2025). On climate: "The biggest barriers to solving the climate crisis are political, not technological." (bluesky, 2026-02-18).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Strongly endorses (near-verbatim overlap):**
- The language critique: "artificial intelligence" as a misnomer, anthropomorphic verbs, "hallucinates," and ELIZA. This is his standard material (Rolling Stone 2025; Atlantic 2025; Better Offline).
- "A vast statistical model reflecting back…" His phrasing is "smeared-out, averaged voice" (Ars 2025).
- The hype manufacturing machine (the OpenAI marketing spend) and "always just five years away." He cites Altman's AGI timeline and the three-wishes climate plan (Rolling Stone; Guardian 2025). He also says "policymakers and politicians are taking those short timelines way too seriously" (bluesky, 2025-05-22).
- The energy and "social permission" point. His "best case" for current AI is efficiency, and "if that can't be done, then I think we've got to regulate the [expletive] out of them" (C-SPAN 2025).
- The fluency signal cheapening. He has watched it happen: crackpot physics emails "have become notably cleaner and better formatted, and it's clearly LLMs" (bluesky, 2025-11-19).
- Cognitive atrophy ("writing is thinking, and thinking is a habit").
- Rejecting techno-doom. He goes further than the note: he treats doom as a *product* of the hype ("useful idiots").

**Pushes back or dissents:**
- **"It could also help us fix them… an invitation to build."** He would contest any framing in which AI is part of the fix for science ("despite all evidence to the contrary", Guardian 2025). He'd want the manifesto to say AI's benefits are unproven and its harms present.
- **The "annotator" paragraph as a "hopeful corollary."** He cites content-moderation labor as a core *ethical* objection (bluesky 2025-05-21). He would push hard against celebrating the annotator economy, and against casting scholars as "ultimate annotators" *for* AI firms.
- **"Fluency was always a signal of privilege."** He partly agrees about gatekeeping but rejects "democratizing" claims for generative output: "The process by which you make the art is part of the art itself" (C-SPAN 2025). He'd resist any suggestion that fluency's loss is net positive if LLMs replace it.
- **"Neither complacency nor doom."** He'd accept this, but insist that the power analysis (billionaires, concentration) be named, not stay implicit.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Harvest claims (Session 4):**
1. Wealth and platform scale are not epistemic authority. Tech leaders' claims about science should be judged against domain experts like anyone else's ("contempt for expertise", Ars 2025).
2. Credibility signals must include *funding transparency*. Mixing junk with real work under one masthead launders legitimacy (the *Inference* case). The same logic applies to AI-generated text mixed into the record.
3. LLM output should never carry authority by itself. "You can't use an LLM in any context where being wrong is a problem." (bluesky 2026-04-25, quoting a founder friend; 346 likes).
4. Drop the vocabulary: say "text generation engine," not "AI"; drop "AGI" and "superintelligence" from serious policy discourse.

**Session 5 interventions:**
1. A manifesto clause and a shared vocabulary guide that bans anthropomorphic verbs and "AGI" framing in summit outputs.
2. Public funding for local and science journalism and for "ways of helping people discern what's real and what isn't," rather than AI tools (C-SPAN 2025).
3. Disclosure norms: funders and AI-vendor relationships on any tool, journal or report making epistemic claims (extending *Inference*).

## 5. Likely dissents for the record

- **Against any roadmap item that deploys LLMs in scholarly evaluation or synthesis.** Grounds: unreliability ("fruit of the poisoned tree", bluesky 2025-05-21) and the "accelerate scientific research" myth (Guardian 2025).
- **Against framing AI as inevitable ("it's here to stay").** He calls inevitability a device that "absolves them of responsibility" (C-SPAN 2025).
- **Possibly against the "ultimate annotators" framing,** if it means supplying labor to AI firms (content-moderator exploitation).
- **Against taking x-risk or AGI safety seriously as epistemic priorities:** "We don't need to spend more time giving air to AI doom cultists or AI hype hucksters." (bluesky 2026-02-11).

## 6. Tensions and alliances with named attendees

- **Blaise Agüera y Arcas (organizer, Google):** a substantive clash. Blaise has argued that AGI is already here (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2023--noema-agi-is-already-here.md`; `…/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`). Becker holds that LLMs "have no conception of the world" and that AGI is "hopelessly ill-defined" (bluesky 2026-03-13). The corpus has no direct exchange between them. He has mocked Google's AI search specifically: it "doesn't understand anything at all" (bluesky 2025-12-12).
- **Benjamin Bratton:** tension on substance. Bratton's 2026 piece "Agentic AI and the next intelligence explosion" (`benjamin-bratton/by/2026--agentic-ai-and-the-next-intelligence-explosion.md`) uses exactly the concept Becker dismisses as sci-fi lineage (C-SPAN 2025). No direct exchange.
- **Peter Salib:** tension on substance. Salib writes on AGI deterrence and "AI rights for human safety" (`peter-salib/by/2026--ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`; `…/2025--nuclear-deterrence-in-the-age-of-agi.md`). Becker considers taking AGI x-risk premises seriously to be the "useful idiot" error (Atlantic 2025).
- **Henry Farrell:** an ally. Farrell reviewed *More Everything Forever* warmly and extended it ("as Becker argues, we need to discard the imagined futures", `henry-farrell/by/2025--pm-we-need-to-escape-the-gernsback-continuum.md`; `…/2025--pm-vico-s-singularity-republished.md`).
- **Kara Miller (closing synthesizer):** interviewed him on *It Turns Out* in March 2026 (`kara-miller/INDEX.md`). The synthesis may well carry his framing.
- **Steven Sloman:** likely agreement that intelligence and knowledge are communal. Becker's line is "a feature of communities or cultures" (C-SPAN 2025). Sloman writes on the "community of knowledge" (`steven-sloman/by/2023--a-cup-of-knowledge-choiceology.md`). This is an inferred alignment.
- **Mike Caulfield:** a real clash. Caulfield explicitly eye-rolls at the "everything is hallucination" argument, which Becker makes ("That's all they do"). See Caulfield's card.
- **David Krakauer:** Becker was an SFI journalism fellow. There is no evidence of disagreement.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

**High confidence** on his AI/AGI/billionaire positions. They are consistent across the book tour, op-eds and about 1,200 Bluesky posts. **Gap:** the book text itself is not in the corpus (summary only). **Gap:** he has said nothing on scholarly publishing, peer review, open access or commons, so his Session 3-relevant views are inferred from plagiarism and "built on theft" comments. **Gap:** there is no direct statement about any fellow attendee except via Farrell and Miller. **Caveat:** Better Offline is ASR-transcribed, with inferred speaker labels. Tone risk: he is combative online but "carefully argued" in long form (Newitz, C-SPAN). Expect sharp but not disruptive interventions.
