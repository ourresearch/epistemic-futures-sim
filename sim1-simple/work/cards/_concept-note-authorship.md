# Who drafted the concept note? (evidence-based guess)

**Verdict:** **Geoffrey Bilder** was most likely the principal drafter, with roughly 65–70% confidence. **Amy Brand** is the most likely co-editor and contributed the licensing, compensation and "human judgment" material. **David Krakauer** probably shaped the cognitive-atrophy paragraph. **Blaise Agüera y Arcas** almost certainly did not write the language section. The note reads as one voice lightly merged from several inputs ("this author", singular).

## The "this author included" fluency sentence

This is ambiguous. Brand is a trained linguist and academic, which fits "grammar, register, and academic prose … decades perfecting it". Bilder is a prolific stylist and light-verse writer who lives in France, and the sentence sits inside the trust-signal history that is characteristically his. There is no direct corpus match for either. Because "this author" implies a single drafter and the surrounding argument is Bilder's, I lean **Bilder**, but a Brand interpolation is plausible.

## Unexplained phrases

No attendee dossier contains "writing is cheaper than reading", "trough of disillusionment", "ultimate annotators" or "racist linear algebra" (corpus-wide grep). "Publish, then filter" appears only in Tim O'Reilly's 2015 posts, where he credits Clay Shirky. "Social permission" appears only in unrelated senses (Anil Dash, Agüera y Arcas). So these phrases were most likely coined or borrowed during drafting rather than lifted from an organizer's prior writing. The nearest antecedents are Priem's 2010 "No one can read everything" and Bilder's 2011 argument for separating filtering from publishing.
