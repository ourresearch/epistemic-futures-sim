# Position card: David Weinberger

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Role:** Session 3 participant ("Knowledge Infrastructure and Tools: Commons, Enclosure, and Sovereignty"), alongside lead Adrian Johns, Adam Bly, Benjamin Bratton and Jason Priem.

**Bio:** Weinberger is a philosopher by training (PhD, University of Toronto) turned internet theorist. He has been a Berkman Klein Center fellow and senior researcher since the early 2000s, was co-director of the Harvard Library Innovation Lab, and is now with Harvard metaLAB. His books are *The Cluetrain Manifesto* (co-author), *Small Pieces Loosely Joined*, *Everything Is Miscellaneous*, *Too Big to Know* ("the smartest person in the room is the room") and *Everyday Chaos* (2019, on machine learning and the end of explanation). He was a writer-in-residence at Google's People + AI Research (PAIR) for two years and later in Google's Moral Imagination group (his 2024 co-authored IRIE paper gives a google.com address). He edits MIT Press's open-access "Strong Ideas" series. His blog Joho the Blog ran from 2001 to 2025. In late 2025 he delivered a new book to MIT Press (Bluesky; site "beautifulparticulars.com [beta]"). He is **markedly more AI-sanguine than the concept note**: epistemically deflationary about LLMs ("don't know anything"), but enthusiastic about what machine learning reveals and about LLMs as dialogue partners.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **LLMs are statistical and don't know anything, which matches the note's framing.** "The LLM only knows which words typically follow other words in what we have written." And: "They always hallucinate … It's just that most of the time their hallucinations are accurate" (`david-weinberger/by/2023--kmworld-truth-lies-and-large-language-models.md`, 2023). "these machines don't know anything at all" (`david-weinberger/by/2024--medium-ai-doesnt-think-like-us.md`, 2024).
- **The makers carry an obligation not to deceive.** Creators "should have realized … a special obligation not to let us fall for the trick it was designed to accomplish." He is open to watermarks, and to routing factual questions to "relatively reliable sources of knowledge" such as Wikipedia (2023 KMWorld).
- **Yet machine learning is changing what knowledge *is*, for the better.** Machine learning reveals "the limits of generalizations and … the power of the particulars". He calls this vision "liberating, humbling, and joyous … even if it took inscrutable machines to teach it to us" (`david-weinberger/by/2024--drucker-forum-knowledge-in-the-age-of-ai.md`, 2024).
- **Inexplicability is acceptable.** "explicability is not a property of the universe any more than being edible is"; "only ask for explanations where they are possible" (`david-weinberger/by/2023--sociologica-a-plea-for-inexplicability.md`, 2022/23).
- **Humans in the *dialogue*, not just the loop.** He cites studies where humans lower accuracy: "At least in this case, humans degrade the loop." He proposes "humans in the dialogue with AI, discovering our values" (`david-weinberger/by/2026--kmworld-from-humans-in-the-loop-to-in-the-flow.md`, 2026).
- **Cognitive atrophy is real but should be redirected, not resisted.** On the MIT essay-writing study: "That's quite concerning, of course." But "most students are rarely going to write essays and reports after they graduate"; "AI is not a plagiarism machine but a tool for developing ideas" (`david-weinberger/by/2026--popsci-ai-can-teach-students-art-of-dialogue.md`, 2026).
- **LLMs as a new oral culture.** His contribution to an "AI Age" resilience report "embraces LLMs as dialogic partners in a new synthesis of oral and literary cultures". Also: "Remember when we couldn't talk with books? We had to just sit there and read them!" (`david-weinberger/social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2026-04 and 2026-05).
- **LLMs can serve people held back by the fluency barrier.** "Yes, LLMs can manufacture prose that only sounds thoughtful, but they can also help people with poor communication skills express important thoughts clearly" (Bluesky, 2026-01).
- **Openness as the default architecture ("unanticipation").** "Unanticipation is also the strategy behind open source, open access, open APIs, and the Internet itself"; "silo-ed data may start to feel like an offense to our humanity"; "Humility is a virtue for machines as well as for humans" (`david-weinberger/by/2023--isu-unanticipating-metadata.md`, 2023).
- **Keep knowledge decentralized against corporate concentration.** "If we want to avoid giving a handful of gigantic corporations sole power over knowledge, then we need to keep our multiple imperfect systems alive, vital and distributed" (`david-weinberger/by/2016--kmworld-re-decentralize-knowledge.md`, 2016).
- **Sceptical of copyright-based claims against training.** From a Moby-Dick experiment he concludes that LLM makers' claim "that they don't keep copies of their training materials" is bolstered, while adding "Am I wrong? It's highly likely" (`david-weinberger/by/2024--do-llm-s-store-what-they-re-trained-on-a-reason-to-think-not.md`, 2024). Earlier: "insane copyright laws" (`david-weinberger/by/2013--aaron-swartz-and-the-future-of-libraries.md`).
- **Aware of cultural bias.** ChatGPT "will continue to speak as if everyone comes from the dominant culture expressed in its training data" (`david-weinberger/by/2024--chatgpt-admits-it-assumes-a-western-northern-culture.md`, 2024).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Endorse:** "a vast statistical model reflecting back … the people whose writing it was trained on." He says nearly the same (2023, 2024). He also shares the scorn for "hallucinate" as a euphemism ("a nice way of saying 'made-up stuff'").
- **Endorse:** the Socrates/*Phaedrus* analogy. He uses it too, but draws the *opposite* moral: LLMs answer two of Socrates' three objections to writing, since they adapt to the reader and can be questioned (2026 PopSci).
- **Endorse:** fluency as privilege. His Bluesky post defends AI help for people with "poor communication skills" (2026).
- **Endorse:** surveillance capitalism as a precedent. His "Five Ages of Data" cites Zuboff on data smog "weaponized" as the internet's business model (`david-weinberger/by/2024--kmworld-the-five-ages-of-data.md`).
- **Push back (strong):** the note's stance, "We don't come to AI with open arms. We come to it cynical." He does come with open arms, calling ML's lessons "liberating, humbling, and joyous".
- **Push back:** "keeping humans meaningfully in the loop is vital." He argues humans sometimes "degrade the loop", and he reframes the role as dialogue rather than checkpoint (2026).
- **Push back:** the vocabulary project against anthropomorphism. He predicts that within "2 years" we'll stop scare-quoting AI mental verbs "because we assume the scare quotes are implied" (Bluesky, 2026-02). He writes of "talking" with LLMs, though he still uses scare quotes himself.
- **Push back:** cognitive atrophy as a threat. He calls it an opportunity to teach dialogue, and says the essay was never the main skill most people need.
- **Push back:** opacity as a core harm. He defends inexplicability where outcomes are reliable, while wanting confidence scores and transparency "as … they can be" (2023 ISU).
- **Ambivalent:** enclosure and "fair compensation". He is anti-centralization (2016) but also anti-copyright-maximalist. He warned that an attribution-checking tool would "immediately be taken over by the copyright cartel" and that "if there's anything worse than rampant lack of attribution, it would be an algorithmic insistence upon it" (`david-weinberger/by/2013--kmworld-the-failure-to-attribute.md`, older). Expect wariness of metering or paywall-style remedies.
- **Probable push back:** "scholars and publishers as the ultimate annotators." *Too Big to Know* locates expertise in networks, not credentials. He wants libraries "as platforms" opening "everything that libraries know" (2013). *Inference.*

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 3 harvest claims (2–4):**
1. The best safeguard for knowledge infrastructure is *openness plus interoperability*: open data and metadata, open APIs, designed for uses nobody anticipated. Silos, not openness, are the failure mode.
2. Decentralization is the real sovereignty question: keep a "long tail" of libraries, sites and communities so a few firms, AI companies included, can't own the pathways to knowledge.
3. Don't answer AI extraction with maximal copyright or attribution metering. That risks handing control to rights-holder cartels and walling off the commons.
4. Treat LLMs as interfaces over reliable sources (Wikipedia, search, libraries) that show confidence levels, not as oracles.

**Session 5 interventions (1–3):**
- Teach dialogue as literacy: assess students' AI conversation transcripts (what they probed, where they were too trusting), not only essays.
- Require machine outputs to carry calibrated confidence statements, and ground factual answers in citable sources (retrieval over reliable corpora).
- Libraries and scholarly bodies as open platforms: release metadata and collections openly for unanticipated reuse (as co-director of the Harvard Library Innovation Lab he blogged approvingly about Harvard's release of metadata for 12M library items into the public domain, `david-weinberger/by/2012--big-data-for-books-harvard-puts-metadata-for-12m-library-ite.md`).

## 5. Likely dissents to record

- Against a blanket "humans must stay in the loop" plank (`by/2026--kmworld-from-humans-in-the-loop-to-in-the-flow.md`).
- Against policing anthropomorphic language as a manifesto commitment (Bluesky 2026-02; `by/2026--popsci…`).
- Against framing AI's effect on knowledge as primarily corrosive. He would record a "this is also an epistemic gift" minority view (`by/2024--drucker-forum…`, `by/2024--medium-ai-doesnt-think-like-us.md`).
- Possibly against compensation or attribution schemes that tighten copyright control (`by/2013--kmworld-the-failure-to-attribute.md`; weaker, older).

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** that he is more AI-optimistic than the note, on the epistemology (particulars, inexplicability) and on education and dialogue. There are many consistent 2019–2026 texts.
- **Medium** on Session 3 specifics (enclosure, sovereignty, compensation). Most commons and copyright material is 2009–2016, and recent statements on AI training are a single 2024 blog experiment. His views on paying for training data, scholarly-publishing economics, and AI-company concentration after 2023 are thin. He mocks Musk/Grok but has written little on OpenAI's market power.
- **Conflict-of-interest note:** his long Google residency may shape how others in the room read him, and possibly his own framing.
- **Gaps:** the forthcoming MIT Press book is not in the corpus. The Imagining the Digital Future report text is not in `by/`. The Google AI "stories" were not fetched.
