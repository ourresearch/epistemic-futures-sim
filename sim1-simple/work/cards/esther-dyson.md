# Position card: Esther Dyson

## 1. Role at summit + relevant bio

**Role:** She is listed first for Session 2 ("AI as Epistemic Actor: Risks, Capabilities, and Governance"), so she is probably its lead and provocateur (dossier INDEX: "summit role: session 2 lead").

**Bio:** Investor, journalist, and long-time tech analyst. She ran *Release 1.0* and PC Forum for about 25 years (to 2007). She was ICANN's founding chair (1998–2000), later sat on its At-Large advisory body, and joined the fight against the 2019–20 .ORG sale. Since then she has worked on health: Wellville was a 10-year nonprofit in five small US communities, deliberately "term-limited". She now writes the Substack "In the pool with Esther" (2025–26), and her forthcoming book is *In Defense of Limits: Human Time and AI Scale* (MIT Press, March 2027; working title *Term Limits*). That book gives her a direct tie to Amy Brand and the host press. She is an active angel investor in AI and health startups (Abridge, Ezra, Latimer, Onix, fact-checking companies). She calls herself a "cynical idealist" and a "conditional optimist", not a doomer. Her through-line is that **the business model behind AI is the real problem**, not AI itself. Her remedies are not sweeping statutes. She prefers targeted accountability mechanisms (registries, liability insurance, disclosure) and a culture shift toward human attention, friction, and "theory of business mind".

## 2. Core recent positions

- **The danger is business models and unaccountable people, not "evil AI."** "People talk about unexplainable AI when they should be more concerned about the unexplainable humans running the companies that develop the AI." (`esther-dyson/by/2025--substack-don-t-fuss-about-training-your-ais-train-your-babies.md`, 2025). Earlier: "it's the business model behind the LLMs, stupid! … transparency is key - not so much about the specific algorithms, but about the money flows" (`esther-dyson/social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2024-06-05).
- **AI is a tool, and accountability must attach to humans who can suffer.** "The best way to enforce accountability for an AI's actions is to link it to people … who *can* suffer and be held accountable." (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-know-your-agent.md`, 2026). Also: "It is truly just following orders … It is humans' moral duty to know – and be accountable for – what they are asking for." (`esther-dyson/by/2025--substack-raci-thoughts-on-ai.md`, 2025).
- **Her concrete AI-governance project is "Know Your Agent", a .agent registry run through ICANN.** She advises the Agent Community Foundation's bid: "a system for honorable, public-facing AI agents to be registered and authenticated, with a strong regime of 'Know Your Agent'" covering "provenance" and "accountability". The rule-setters should be "unconflicted parties from outside the heavy hitters". (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-know-your-agent.md`, 2026)
- **Mandatory liability insurance rather than big regulation.** "what we need is not a huge and quickly-out-of-date regulatory regime, but a few simple rules" (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-don-t-just-weigh-the-risk-prevent-the-damage.md`, 2026). Chatbot and agent companies should have to buy insurance, and the SEC should require them to disclose premiums. Her model is fire insurance: "Good insurance companies are known by the risks they turn down". She is explicitly against "grandiose, all-encompassing laws" (`…know-your-agent.md`).
- **Chatbots are services, not speech.** "Social media offerings and now chatbots and AI agents are not (free) speech; they are services and tools … akin to drugs or medical devices that mess with your mind." (`…prevent-the-damage.md`, 2026)
- **"Information diabetes" and "ultra-processed information" are her name for cognitive atrophy.** "Don't outsource your understanding of the world to AI summaries and 30-second videos … You just might assemble a new truth that AI summaries would dismiss" (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-stop-thinking-like-an-llm.md`, 2026). Her twist on the concept note: as models gain context, **humans are becoming more like "adjunctless LLMs"**: "We are thinking like LLMs - fluidly, easily, but without understanding".
- **Sycophantic bots are "parasites", not predators.** "AI can act more like a parasite than a predator, keeping you alive to suck on." (`esther-dyson/by/2025--startuphealth-the-case-for-limits.md`, 2025). Also, "sycobots … *create* vulnerability in otherwise healthy minds" (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-alert-new-forms-of-diabetes-on-the-rise.md`, 2026).
- **Train people, not just models: "theory of business mind" over media literacy.** Kids must ask "Why am I seeing this news? Are they trying to get me angry—or just using me to sell ads?" and this is "less about traditional media literacy" (`…train-your-babies.md`, 2025). She calls it "theory of business mind" (`…the-case-for-limits.md`, 2025).
- **Small, curated, expert-owned models beat giant general ones.** She praises Onix's expert-curated "small language models", where experts keep 70% of revenue. "The trick is to start small, with a limited corpus, rather than to start large and try to control things with guardrails" (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-trade-offs-and-transparency.md`, 2026). This is a working example of the concept note's "ultimate annotators" idea.
- **LLMs use a public asset and should pay for it.** "the LLMs use this collective asset of information that's public … they should be paying … like a land right fee or a water fee" to fund care workers (`esther-dyson/av/2024--technology-trust-time-with-esther-dyson.md`, 2024; ASR transcript). In 2024 on X she imagined LLMs that "(pay to) train on well-reported firewalled publications and deliver artisanal news" (`social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2024-03-12).
- **It's a bubble, but the technology is real.** "A bubble does not mean the thing is fake … It means there is too much air in the system." She also says "Billions of people have access to a device that can act like a private advisor" (`…the-case-for-limits.md`, 2025).
- **Put friction and human time back.** "Friction is not inherently 'bad' or 'good'" and "You can't outsource your own attention." (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-in-defense-of-friction.md`, 2026). She supports AI-funded "Universal Basic *Service*" over UBI (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-the-case-for-universal-basic-service.md`, 2026).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Endorses** "AI didn't create these problems … but … accelerates". This is her standard framing: AI "amplif[ies] your power" and the harms are old (tobacco, opioids, ad-tech) and now scaled (`…prevent-the-damage.md`; `av/2024--ih-221-esther-dyson-ai-thoughts.md`).
- **Endorses, with her own vocabulary,** the cognitive-atrophy half of the "vicious circle". "Information diabetes" and "stop thinking like an LLM" are close paraphrases of the atrophy paragraph. She says nothing about model collapse.
- **Partly agrees and partly pushes back on the anti-anthropomorphism paragraph.** She agrees AI is "only a tool" and "There is nothing 'there' on the other side" (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-couples-counseling-us-and-our-ai.md`). She also calls AI "one of the world's fuzziest terms" (`av/2024--ih-221…`). But she does not use "statistical model" language, and she wants a *new pronoun*: "it's not really an it or a they … We need a new pronoun for AIs" (`av/2026--embracing-term-limits-money-and-power-at-scale-esther-dyson.md`). That fits the concept note's call for new vocabulary. She treats agents as things that "initiate actions, negotiate … change the world" and therefore need registration.
- **Strongly endorses** "surveillance capitalism" and "enshittification" as the core diagnosis. "Theory of business mind" and "parasites/customer retention" are her versions of it.
- **Pushes back on the tone of "we come to AI cynical".** She is a self-described "conditional optimist" and investor. She invests in AI note-taking and diagnostic companies and thinks AI "should replace a good deal of what doctors currently do" (`…the-case-for-limits.md`). She will not endorse a manifesto that reads as anti-AI.
- **Endorses the "annotators" and compensation paragraph in spirit,** through "water fee" royalties and expert-owned SLMs. But her preferred beneficiaries are care workers, teachers, and doulas, as much as scholars.
- **On "not another cautious consensus report":** she will like concrete, small, "targeted" mechanisms. She distrusts "grandiose" frameworks and "committees and laws" alone: "We need a movement and a culture shift, not just committees and laws" (`…new-forms-of-diabetes…md`).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 2 provocation and harvest claims:**
1. "Unexplainable AI is the lesser problem; unexplainable *people and business models* are the black box that matters. Transparency should target money flows and objective functions, not weights."
2. "AI agents need identity and accountability infrastructure: Know Your Agent. Registration, provenance, and a human or organisation that can be sued, backed by mandatory liability insurance. It should be run by an unconflicted, community-governed body, not the labs."
3. "The epistemic risk is humans coming to think like LLMs: fluent, context-poor, outsourcing understanding. Governance must protect human time, attention and friction, not just police model outputs."
4. Possibly: "Small, curated, expert-owned models (Onix-style) are the trustworthy pattern: start from a vetted corpus, not from guardrails on everything."

**Session 5 interventions she would push:**
- A **"Know Your Agent" provenance and accountability registry** for public-facing agents, extended to scholarly or knowledge agents. This could be framed as the knowledge community backing the .agent Community Priority bid or an analogous registry.
- **Mandatory liability insurance plus SEC-style disclosure** for AI knowledge services, using insurers as private inspectors.
- A **levy or "water fee" on training on the public and scholarly commons**, earmarked for human curators, teachers and care workers. Or a "theory of business mind" curriculum in place of generic AI literacy.

## 5. Likely dissents she would want recorded

- **Against legal personhood or rights for AI** as the accountability route. Her own words: AIs "do not experience fear of death or moral jeopardy. You can't just throw an AI into jail … It won't suffer", so accountability must run to humans (`…know-your-agent.md`, 2026). If Salib's A-corp or AI-rights ideas enter the manifesto she will likely want this recorded.
- **Against sweeping, comprehensive AI statutes** in favour of targeted rules (`…know-your-agent.md`; `…prevent-the-damage.md`). This could put her against a manifesto plank calling for broad regulation.
- Possibly **against pure anti-AI framing.** She signed the 2023 pause letter but framed it as "careless/evil people and destructive business models USING powerful AI" (`social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2023-03-29), and she calls "AI doomsterism" a hallucination (2023-09-23).

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Peter Salib (tension, strong and grounded on both sides).** She holds that only humans can be held accountable. Salib and his co-authors argue that thin, human-principal identity "will only take us so far" and propose A-corps and AI private-law rights (`peter-salib/by/2026--why-law-needs-a-new-entity-to-govern-ai-agents.md`). **Partial alliance:** both want KYC-style agent registries. His "thin identity" is close to her "Know Your Agent". On copyright they also clash: her "water fee" against his "copyright isn't … a welfare program for artists" (`peter-salib/by/2025--copyright-should-not-protect-artists-from-ai.md`). The two also differ on UBI versus her UBS.
- **Tim O'Reilly (alliance):** she names him as a backer of the Agent Community Foundation and its AI Disclosures Project (`…know-your-agent.md`).
- **Amy Brand / MIT Press:** publisher of her 2027 book (INDEX cross-link).
- **Adam Bly:** she disclosed being an investor in his AI startup (`social/x-timeline.jsonl`, line ~2612, older). This is a relationship, not a position.
- **Ramón Alvarado (partial alliance):** both reject "intelligence" mystique and see AI as a tool or artifact. But his black-box focus is on error opacity inside the system, while hers is on the people and business models around it.
- **Sherry Turkle:** a likely ally on sycophantic companions and "pretend" relationships (Dyson retweeted Turkle on "fake friendship" in 2017). Dyson is more open to purpose-built AI therapy bots (`…couples-counseling…md`).

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** on her governance instincts: targeted accountability, insurance, registries, and business-model critique. These are repeated across 2025–26 Substack posts and interviews.
- **Medium confidence** on what she will say about *scholarly* knowledge specifically. Almost none of her 2024–26 writing is about peer review, publishing or science. She will speak by analogy from health care, media and ICANN.
- **Gaps:** there is no text of the 2027 book (summary only). Interview transcripts are ASR with inferred speaker labels. Her X timeline is thin after 2025. She has written nothing on model collapse, opacity-as-interpretability, or open-weights debates.
