# Position card: Peter N. Salib

## 1. Role at summit + relevant bio

**Role:** Session 2 participant ("AI as Epistemic Actor: Risks, Capabilities, and Governance"). He is the room's AI-safety and law-and-economics voice, and probably its most capability-bullish attendee.

**Bio:** W. Ronald Robins Endowed Professor and Associate Professor of Law at the University of Houston Law Center. He founded and co-directs the Center for Law & AI Risk (CLAIR). He is also Law and Policy Advisor to the Center for AI Safety, a Visiting Senior Fellow at the Institute for Law & AI, a Lawfare contributing editor, and was a Forethought visiting scholar in 2026. Before academia he clerked for Judge Easterbrook and practised appellate law at Sidley. His research programme is "law as an alignment technology". With philosopher Simon Goldstein he wrote *AI Rights for Human Safety* (Virginia Law Review 2026) and a forthcoming book, *AI Rights* (Cambridge UP, 2027). Related work covers AI suffrage, A-corps and the individuation of agents ("How to Count AIs"), AI nationalization and a government "halt right", AI outputs and the First Amendment, copyright and AI training, and US–China AI cooperation. He writes in a law-and-economics and game-theory register. He treats AGI as near and says so plainly.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **AGI is coming soon and should be treated as a given.** "The question is no longer whether we will achieve AGI, but when … The time has come to treat AGI as an inevitability." (`peter-salib/by/2024--openais-latest-model-shows-agi-is-inevitable.md`, 2024, with Frazier and Rozenshtein). Also: "AGI is, I think, the most important thing that could happen in the next 4 years." (`peter-salib/social/bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-03-05)
- **Capability sceptics keep being wrong, and the "stochastic parrots" camp is "AGI-denialist."** "When authors of the AGI-denialist 'stochastic parrots' paper publish 'Fully Autonomous AI Agents Should Not Be Developed,' you should start to worry that AGI really is imminent." (`bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-02-07). On the AI Snake Oil authors, "even their deflationary forecasts have been inflating pretty quickly!" (`social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-06-05). He wants sceptics to stop "just claiming that 'AI can't truly reason'" and to propose tests instead (X, 2024-04-08).
- **His argument does not depend on consciousness; it depends on agency.** "'AGI,' as it is used here, does not mean machines that are conscious, sentient, or metaphysical persons. AGI is instead about what the system can do." (`peter-salib/by/2026--ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`, 2026). Misalignment "does not require AIs to be conscious, to be evil, or to hate humans" (same file).
- **Give sufficiently capable AIs basic private-law rights, for human safety.** "AIs should be given the basic private law rights already enjoyed by other non-human agents, like corporations … make contracts, hold property, and bring tort claims." Without this, humans and AIs are "trapped in a prisoner's dilemma" (`…ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`, 2026). The stated goal is "Not for the sake of AIs, but for the sake of global human flourishing." (`bluesky-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-07-29)
- **Accountability that runs only to human principals is not enough; he proposes the A-corp.** "If AI law resigns itself to holding only human principals accountable for AI agents' actions, it will learn the hard lesson that corporate law already learned." The A-corp is "Owned by humans, run by AIs" and gives "Personhood with registration, disclosure, and revocable credentials … not a master key but a leash" (`peter-salib/by/2026--why-law-needs-a-new-entity-to-govern-ai-agents.md`, 2026). He also backs KYC-style "thin identity" registries.
- **AI outputs are not protected speech, so safety law can regulate what models say.** "When a generative AI system—like ChatGPT—outputs some text … no one thereby communicates." (`peter-salib/by/2024--ai-outputs-are-not-protected-speech.md`, 2024). He also stresses opacity: model code is "far too complex for any human to interpret", and creators "cannot directly control what AIs say".
- **Copyright should not be used to compensate creators for AI training; use general welfare such as UBI.** "copyright isn't, and shouldn't be, a welfare program for artists." He accepts the fairness complaint as "factually true" but says "life isn't fair", and suggests AI firms "can simply pay the relevant humans to produce" novel data (`peter-salib/by/2025--copyright-should-not-protect-artists-from-ai.md`, 2025, with Goldstein).
- **AI is not a natural monopoly, and some market power is good for innovation.** "the AI industry is not a natural monopoly. Nor is it plagued by the problems of market concentration today." (`peter-salib/by/2025--ai-is-not-a-natural-monopoly.md`, abstract only). He pairs this with a narrow government **"halt right"** against catastrophic risk and "hard" corporate power, rather than full nationalization (`peter-salib/by/2026--ai-nationalization.md`, abstract only).
- **Sceptical of AI regulation in general, but supports targeted catastrophic-risk laws.** "even if one is skeptical of AI regulation in general (which I am), targeted regulations addressing catastrophic AI risks are needed" (`peter-salib/by/2025--for-ai-safety-regulation-a-bird-in-the-hand.md`, 2025). He supports SB-1047 and New York's RAISE Act, and expects "much of what is in the EU AI Act to be counterproductive" (X, 2024-07-17).
- **The epistemic threat he sees is AI-driven astroturfing.** "AIs like ChatGPT will be able to produce a limitless and practically free supply of 'opinions' … erod[ing] … the very idea that our counterparts online are real people." His remedies include subscription and KYC "proof of humanity" and a "Pigouvian tax on bot-generated comments" (`peter-salib/by/2023--fake-news-fake-views-chatgpt-like-ai.md`, 2023).
- **Rogue-AI evidence is accumulating.** "Essentially every AI model tested was willing to attempt blackmail, corporate espionage, and even murder to avoid being replaced or shut down." (`peter-salib/by/2025--ai-might-let-you-die-to-save-itself.md`, 2025)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Dissents from the deflationary paragraph** ("a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people…", "confusing a statistical model for a knowing mind"). He does not claim models have minds. His point is that the mind question is **beside the point**: they "act in goal-seeking ways" (`…ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`). On "reflecting back … intentions", his own work says reasoning models trained by RL "aren't pure imitators" and may "swerve back" toward alien optimization (`peter-salib/by/2025--todays-ais-arent-paperclip-maximizers.md`). He has also mocked Bender's definition of "genuine" knowledge as one "current-gen models are satisfying constantly" (X, 2026-01-28).
- **Dissents from "always just five years away."** His view is the opposite: the persistent error is *under*-estimation ("exponential growth bias", `…agi-is-inevitable.md`).
- **Mixed on "hype" and marketing.** He is harsh on the labs' conduct. He called the dissolution of OpenAI's Superalignment team "very bad news" (`peter-salib/by/2024--openai-no-longer-takes-safety-seriously.md`, 2024) and wrote "Self-governance will not suffice" (`…agi-is-inevitable.md`). But he treats "AI is pure hype" as the view he disagrees with most (X, 2024-07-17).
- **Dissents from the infrastructure and compensation paragraph** ("treating decades of accumulated human judgment as a free input"; "fair compensation"). His copyright essay rejects compensation-by-IP. He would accept the concept note's **annotator-market** observation as evidence *for* his contract-based alternative.
- **Pushes back on "concentration of ownership"** as a structural given (natural-monopoly essay). He would put concentration worries under a narrow halt right and ordinary antitrust.
- **Endorses "writing is now cheaper than reading"** and the flood of plausible content. This is his 2023 astroturfing argument, and he would extend it from scholarship to discourse generally.
- **Probably endorses "not another cautious consensus report"** on style grounds. He is impatient with vagueness.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 2 harvest claims:**
1. "Capabilities are real and accelerating. Governance built on the premise that these are 'just statistical models' will be outrun. Plan for agentic systems that pursue goals, whatever we call them."
2. "The black box cuts both ways. Because no one can specify or predict outputs, outputs aren't anyone's protected speech, so the law *can* regulate them. Opacity strengthens the case for output-level safety rules."
3. "The identification problem comes before liability: which agent did it? We need legible AI identities (registries, keys, A-corps) before we can govern AI as an epistemic actor."
4. Possibly: "AI-generated consensus (astroturf) is the near-term epistemic harm. Raise the cost of fake voices with proof-of-personhood and Pigouvian bot taxes."

**Session 5 interventions:**
- An **agent-identity registry and legal-entity layer** (thin and thick identity) for AI systems that act in knowledge markets, such as automated reviewers and research agents.
- **Targeted, catastrophic-risk-style transparency duties** (published safety and security protocols with enforceable "appropriate safeguards", in the style of RAISE and SB-1047, which he defends in `…a-bird-in-the-hand.md`) rather than broad content rules.
- Possibly a **contract-based market for novel human knowledge work**, with AI firms paying scholars and curators directly, instead of copyright or levy schemes.

## 5. Likely dissents he would want recorded

- **Against any manifesto language that declares LLMs "just statistical models", "not intelligent", or perpetually overhyped.** Grounds: `…agi-is-inevitable.md`, the Bluesky "AGI-denialist" post, and his X Snake Oil posts.
- **Against copyright expansion or a training levy as the compensation mechanism** (`…copyright-should-not-protect-artists-from-ai.md`).
- **Against a blanket "keep humans in the loop / only humans can be accountable" principle** if it forecloses AI legal standing (`…why-law-needs-a-new-entity…md`, `…ai-rights-for-human-safety.md`).
- Possibly **against broad EU-style regulation or antimonopoly remedies** (`…ai-is-not-a-natural-monopoly.md`, X 2024-07-17).

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Esther Dyson (tension, grounded on both sides).** She says AI "won't suffer" and accountability must attach to humans who can (`esther-dyson/by/2026--substack-know-your-agent.md`). He argues human-principal-only accountability fails. **Common ground:** both endorse KYC-style agent registration, and Dyson's "Know Your Agent" is almost exactly his "thin identity". Copyright divides them too: her "water fee" royalty on the public data commons (`esther-dyson/av/2024--technology-trust-time-with-esther-dyson.md`) against his rejection of compensation-for-training. So does UBS against UBI.
- **Ramón Alvarado (tension).** Alvarado writes that once AI is described operationally "we would hardly ask questions regarding sentience, social relationships, or legal rights of predictive statistical models" (`ramon-alvarado/by/2025--artifactual-epistemology-and-environmental-intelligence.md`). **Partial overlap:** both say that acknowledging AI's capacities requires no consciousness talk. Alvarado: "You just don't need consciousness or agency for machines to do Epistemic tasks" (X, 2026-05-26). Both also treat opacity as deep and "insurmountable".
- **Nick Vincent and Alex Springer (marketplace speakers):** likely tension over data-compensation or attribution markets as rights-based entitlements, versus Salib's view that fairness claims don't justify IP protection. This is inferred from the schedule's "knowledge marketplace" framing; their dossiers were not checked.
- **Organizers' concept note (dissent),** as detailed in section 3.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** on his capability outlook, AI-rights and A-corp agenda, First Amendment view, and copyright stance. There are many 2024–26 full texts plus a long AXRP interview.
- **Low direct evidence** on scholarly publishing, peer review, science, or knowledge commons. He has written nothing specific in the corpus. His epistemic writing is limited to astroturfing (2023) and output regulation. Expect him to reason from first principles and law-and-economics rather than from domain knowledge.
- Several 2025–26 papers are abstract-only: natural monopoly, nationalization, AI suffrage, and the FT piece on AI legal identity (paywalled). Mind the co-authorship: most AI pieces are with Goldstein and/or Arbel.
