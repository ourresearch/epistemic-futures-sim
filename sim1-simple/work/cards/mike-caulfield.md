# Position card: Mike Caulfield

*(The summit materials spell the name "Caufield". The correct spelling is Caulfield.)*

## 1. Role at summit and bio

**Role:** Participant in Session 4, "Epistemic Authority" (with Gendler, Banaji, Becker and Sloman). He is the room's leading *practitioner* of AI-assisted verification. He is also the person most likely to push back against both AI boosters and reflexive AI dismissal, including parts of the concept note.

**Bio:** Inventor of SIFT (Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims) and of the lateral-reading pedagogy now standard in US university information literacy. He wrote *Web Literacy for Student Fact-Checkers* (2017) and co-wrote *Verified* (2023) with Sam Wineburg. He was a research scientist at UW's Center for an Informed Public, studying election rumors. He co-developed Google's "Super Searchers" curriculum, and Google funded his summer-2025 update of that curriculum (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-a-sift-rebuild-in-progress.md`). Since 2025 he has built LLM fact-checking "superprompts": SIFT Toolbox, Deep Background GPT, and the Toulminator. He writes the Substack *The End(s) of Argument*. His current job, in his words, is "half running an academic technology team (currently doing cutting edge things with AI) and half running the team of people that install and support campus audiovisual equipment" (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-the-problem-with-grammarlys-experts.md`, 2026). The corpus does not confirm his current CIP affiliation.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **LLMs should be conduits to sourced human knowledge, not oracles.** "I would propose a simple rule: no answers from nowhere." And: "The chatbot should be a conduit for the information of the world, not an arbiter of truth." (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--atlantic-ai-is-not-your-friend.md`, 2025). He borrows Gopnik's "cultural technologies" framing and invokes Bush's memex.
- **The problem is evidence, not belief-change. The web is a "justification machine."** "misinformation is powerful, not because it changes minds, but because it allows people to maintain their beliefs in light of growing evidence to the contrary." (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--atlantic-internet-is-worse-than-a-brainwashing-machine.md`, 2025, with Warzel). Sycophantic AI is "more convincing, more efficient, and therefore even more dangerous than social media" (Atlantic, May 2025).
- **Use AI to cut the cost of refutation.** On Brandolini's law: "let's reduce the amount of energy required to refute bullshit." The goal is to arm the "antibodies of discourse" (fact-checkers, Wikipedia editors, Community Notes writers) (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-new-sift-toolbox-release-and-a-note.md`, 2025).
- **He was a skeptic until search grounding arrived.** "I started as an AI skeptic… thought, 'This is junk.'" That changed "when they added search capabilities" (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--aace-interview-ai-less-like-programming-more-like-spreadsheets.md`, 2025). "I'm not someone who says 'it's just a prediction machine.'" (same interview).
- **He rejects the "everything is hallucination" argument.** "saying there is no distinction between a hallucination and normal output makes the same error as those who believe that everything that comes from the model is truth." He prefers the terms "clutter," "phantoms" or "mirages" (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-why-the-everything-is-hallucination.md`, 2025).
- **"Publishing-brain" misjudges AI's usefulness.** "The question is whether a person who learns to use these tools will have more evidence-informed opinions on things than someone who does not." Yelling "It's a bad answer box!" "simply accepts the corporate frame" (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-publishing-brain-limits-peoples-understanding.md`, 2026).
- **SIFT for AI starts with the claim, and verification means leaving the LLM.** The draft moves are "Stop and get it in / Investigate the evidence / Find better sources / Track it down." Also: "Get out of the LLM to check the LLM." Because most sharers are now influencers, "the person bringing it to you is just the mailman." (`…a-sift-rebuild-in-progress.md`, 2026). AI also lets you "refine source relevance before you make the jump" (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-look-before-you-leap-how-ai-changes.md`, 2026).
- **Enclosure of the free web is a big, underrated cause of the crisis.** Under the heading "Information doesn't actually want to be free," he writes: "everything is gated now, in a walled garden or behind a paywall." And: "The old web is dead." (`…sift-rebuild…`, 2026). He calls the shift to LLM-mediated links "a cultural loss of staggering proportions."
- **Credibility for AI research tools now depends on a licensed library.** "The status of your product is as determined as much by your demonstrated library as your result." He hopes for "a fairer relationship between sources and these platforms." (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-a-note-on-deep-research-products.md`, 2025).
- **His trust model: expensive signals, reputational stakes, and "position to know."** "ChatGPT… has stakes, but the actual thing producing the thing doesn't have any stakes." The test for any credibility signal: "how hard would it be to fake that?" (`mike-caulfield/by/2024--podcast-guy-kawasaki-verified-methodology.md`, 2024). Back in 2019 he had already written: "The signals are not only cheap, they are in a massive deflationary spiral." (`mike-caulfield/by/2019--hapgood-network-heuristics.md`).
- **Cheap generation raises the value of human judgment.** "Reasoning the noun consumes reasoning the verb" (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-a-surplus-of-reasoning-the-noun-consumes.md`, 2025).
- **Cognitive offloading is real, but refusal is not the answer.** Students worry that AI makes them lazy, and "They are correct to worry." But "the opposite of cognitive offloading is not 'Only use products that are 100% accurate and truthful'" (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-four-tips-for-better-prompting.md`, 2026). And: "what if… the real lesson of cognitive surrender is meaningless work is easily given up?" (`mike-caulfield/by/2026--substack-an-interesting-attribute-tagging.md`, 2026).
- **No unilateral disarmament.** "I don't believe in unilateral disarmament with AI" (AACE, 2025). He is not a booster either: he calls Google's AI Overviews unfit "for anything serious" (`mike-caulfield/social/x-timeline.jsonl`), and he warns that pushing a button to generate "five pages of text that no human has fact-checked yet fundamentally shifts things" (AACE, 2025).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Endorses:**
- The cheap-signal history arc and "fluency" debasement. He wrote it first: "Writing and production used to be a signal of effort and care; now it's not." (AACE 2025). See also his "deflationary spiral" (2019).
- "Writing is cheaper than reading." This is Brandolini's law, which he treats as *the* core problem (2025-04-18).
- "Human judgment matters more, not less." This matches his "surpluses consume" argument (2025-01-22).
- Enclosure and the loss of the free, linked web (2026 SIFT rebuild). The "hallucinate" vocabulary critique, partly: he also wants better terms (2025-06-20).

**Pushes back or dissents:**
- **"So far, [LLMs] have proved most useful where fast, deterministic checks can catch their errors… much of science is the opposite."** This is his clearest dissent. His whole method is to *manufacture* checkability for knowledge work: ask for "sourced information, not answers," then click through. "the totality of my labor here was clicking a link" (2026 Four Tips). He also rejects judging exploratory use by a publication standard ("publishing-brain", 2026). He shares the concern about *unverifiable* claims, though: in 2024 he said that for claims "where you can't directly verify the knowledge that you're given," "I wouldn't trust an LLM unless you really know the subject" (Kawasaki 2024).
- **"A vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions…".** He accepts that this corrects anthropomorphism, but he'd argue it ends the conversation too early (2025-06-20; "not someone who says 'it's just a prediction machine'").
- **"Foisted on us… cynical, ready to hear each sweeping promise…".** He would warn the room about AI-critic "blowback": "a lot of people are super juiced up about this stuff" (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-does-successful-llm-iteration-require.md`, 2025). He left Bluesky posting because "people there aren't very fond of AI" (AACE 2025).
- **Compensation emphasis.** He is less focused on pay than on attribution integrity. On Grammarly's fake "Mike Caulfield expert": "I don't care about that… it is laundering mundane junk through deceptive citation." (2026).
- **Cognitive atrophy.** He accepts the risk for students but doubts it holds for expert or meaningful use (2026-05-28).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Harvest claims (Session 4):**
1. Credibility comes from *expensive, hard-to-fake signals*, meaning reputation and stakes over time, found by checking what the network says about a source. LLMs have no stakes, so they can route you to authority but never be it.
2. "No answers from nowhere." AI systems that mediate knowledge should present maps of named, sourced, contested positions, not opinions.
3. Authority today is lost less to fakes than to *sourcelessness*: link-demoting platforms, paywalls and short video. Restore the linked, free source.
4. Cheap generation raises the value of evaluative skill, so teach the verb (reasoning, SIFT), not just refusal.

**Session 5 interventions:**
1. Fund and test "SIFT for AI" curricula and open verification superprompts (Deep Background) for fact-checkers, librarians and Wikipedia editors. He explicitly asks for someone to fund testing (2026 SIFT rebuild).
2. A provenance and citation standard for AI research tools: which sources, whether they represent the state of knowledge, and whether they are cited accurately (2025 credibility-chain post). Pair it with fair licensing of scholarly libraries.
3. Community-scale annotation of public documents using AI plus a human in the loop, following his MAHA-report annotation layer (`mike-caulfield/by/2025--substack-deep-background-using-o3-to-make.md`).

## 5. Likely dissents for the record

- **Against any blanket manifesto line saying LLMs are unsuitable outside deterministic-check domains, or that people should avoid using them.** Grounds: "unilateral disarmament" (AACE 2025) and "publishing-brain" (2026).
- **Against "everything is hallucination" language in shared vocabulary** (2025-06-20).
- He would agree to caution about chatbot *interfaces*: "the chatbot interface is a bad model for these products when it comes to deeper work" (2026 Four Tips). So the dissent would be framed as "teach use, don't refuse."
