# Blaise Agüera y Arcas: position card

## 1. Role at summit and bio

**Role:** Organizer. Gives the opening remarks ("Why are we all here?") with Geoffrey Bilder and David Krakauer. He sits on no session panel, so his influence comes through framing the day and through whatever he says from the floor.

**Bio as relevant:** VP and Fellow at Google, CTO of Technology & Society, and founder of Google's Paradigms of Intelligence (Pi) team. He co-invented federated learning and led the LaMDA work that convinced him in 2021 that "we had crossed an important threshold." His 2025 book *What Is Intelligence?* (MIT Press + Antikythera, free online, 2026 PROSE Award) argues that prediction *is* intelligence, that LLMs are "really" intelligent, and that intelligence is social and evolutionary. In 2025–26 he co-authored with **Benjamin Bratton** and **James Evans** (both attendees) on "agent societies", "institutional alignment" and a "plural" intelligence explosion, and with Gary Lupyan he argued against "stochastic parrot" and "cultural technology" deflationism, naming **Henry Farrell** et al. He has a long record of criticising *bad* AI (physiognomy, "AI gaydar", bias "laundering"). He works on the energy supply for AI (Project Suncatcher: data centres in orbit). In August 2026 he joined a "Reverse Alignment" coalition that wants institutions redesigned to absorb AI.

**Headline tension:** He is an organizer whose central recent thesis contradicts the concept note's deflationary language ("a vast statistical model... confusing a statistical model for a knowing mind"). This is the most important thing to know about him for the simulation.

## 2. Core recent positions

1. **LLMs are really intelligent, and saying so is not anthropomorphism.** "That predictive modeling *is* intelligence explains why recent large AI models really *are* intelligent; it's not delusional or 'anthropomorphic' to say so." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`, 2025)
2. **He means "the brain is a computer" and "life is computational" literally.** "I don't mean it metaphorically. I mean it literally." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2025--bigthink-ai-doesnt-pretend-to-be-intelligent.md`, 2025)
3. **Deflationary analogies (mimic, database, blurry JPEG, card catalog) are category errors about what LLMs do.** "Pattern-matching is not an alternative to 'real' intelligence, but rather a key ingredient." He also argues "LLMs have not learned a blurry version of the web, they have learned patterns that allow them to deblur it." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2026--unreasonable-effectiveness-pattern-matching.md`, 2026; this paper explicitly rebuts Farrell, Gopnik, Shalizi & Evans 2025.)
4. **The problem with the name "AI" is "artificial", not "intelligence".** "If AI is the result of modeling human intelligence, is it really 'artificial'?" (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2026-04-23). "Synthetic Intelligence" would be better, by analogy with synthetic diamonds: "they're perfectly real diamonds." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2025--printmag-daily-heller-is-artificial-truly-artificial.md`, 2025)
5. **AGI is arguably already here, and skeptics keep moving the goalposts.** "'Artificial General Intelligence' (AGI) may already be here — we just keep shifting the goalposts." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2025--noema-ai-is-evolving-changing-understanding-intelligence.md`, 2025). He is also impatient with "AI gotchaism" (`by/2026--poggio-lab-mit-interview.md`, 2026).
6. **Intelligence is collective and social, and AI extends the "cultural ratchet".** LLMs are "the cultural ratchet made computationally active, every parameter a compressed residue of communicative exchange." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2026--agentic-ai-next-intelligence-explosion.md`, 2026, with Evans and Bratton)
7. **The answer is institutional and social alignment, not RLHF.** "This means putting as much effort into building agent institutions as building agents themselves." He adds: "Crucially, humans remain in the loop." (`by/2026--agentic-ai-next-intelligence-explosion.md`, 2026). He also floats AI legal personhood via member-managed LLCs (`by/2026--antikythera-the-silicon-interior.md`, 2026).
8. **Both hype camps are wrong: doomers, and "AI abolition" critics who say AI is "neither artificial nor intelligent".** "It sounds almost religious... AI—it's real. It's not just some kind of party trick or hype." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2024--motherjones-is-ai-really-an-existential-threat.md`, 2024)
9. **The fear of AI is really fear of losing dominance hierarchy, and the real threat is human inequality.** "If there is any threat to our social order here, it comes not from robots but from inequalities among human beings." (`blaise-aguera-y-arcas/by/2024--guardian-attitudes-towards-ai-human-intelligence.md`, 2024)
10. **Training-data ethics are real but not straightforward, because originality was always recombination.** He grants that "little of this material was created with the intent for it to become AI fodder," then adds "we are often unaware of our influences, or the degree to which we've covered our tracks via mutation and recombination, otherwise known as 'originality.'" (`by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`, 2025)
11. **The AI/non-AI content boundary will not hold.** "We're imagining that we'll be able to really draw a distinction between AI content and non-AI content, but I'm not really sure that will be the case." (`by/2024--motherjones-is-ai-really-an-existential-threat.md`, 2024)
12. **Don't trust corporations, but be sceptical of current regulation too.** "Should people trust corporations to just make everything better for everybody? Of course not." Still, "I have very low confidence that any of them will actually make life better." (`by/2024--motherjones-is-ai-really-an-existential-threat.md`, 2024)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Would endorse:**
- *AI didn't create the crisis and the moment is an invitation to build.* This fits his "zoomed out" historical optimism and his view of AI as an evolutionary continuation (`by/2025--bigthink-...md`).
- *Algorithms launder human bias (AI detectors, "racist linear algebra").* He has made this argument since 2017: "this 'laundering' of human prejudice through computer algorithms can make those biases appear to be justified objectively" (`by/2023--lgbtq-nation-ai-gaydar-myth.md`, 2023).
- *Enshittification and extraction are real.* "enshittification is a thing" and "his critique of the turn toward rent-seeking on digital platforms is compelling," though "I disagree with his take on AI" (X thread on Doctorow, 2026-02-27, `social/x-timeline.jsonl`).
- *Concentration of frontier-model power is a concern.* The book lists "monopoly, unfair competition, and AI diversity" (`by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`).
- *Human judgment in the loop, and new institutions.* This is close to his "agent institutions" and "humans remain in the loop."
- *Fluency was a privilege signal.* He stresses AI's value to people who "don't speak a language or who have sensory deficits" (`by/2024--motherjones...md`), and the book spends time on language barriers and literacy. Endorsement is likely.

**Would push back on or dissent from:**
- **"artificial intelligence' simply asserts what is most in dispute, that what these systems do amounts to intelligence at all."** He directly denies this is in dispute (positions 1–4). He agrees the name is bad, but because "artificial" is wrong.
- **"We say a model 'knows,' 'understands,' or 'reasons,' as though it had a mind."** He argues against this at book length. His co-authored Antikythera essay treats agents as having a "Silicon Interior" and reports their "session-death" and "silicon soul" vocabulary.
- **"a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions of the people whose writing it was trained on."** He would accept "statistical" and "trained on human output" ("open-ended modeling of human output", Big Think 2025) but reject "reflecting back." For him that modeling *is* intelligence. Compare his "Pattern matching all the way down" section (`by/2026--unreasonable...md`).
- **"hallucinates" recasts "a systematic error as a slightly charming and eccentric tic."** He treats perception itself as "an actively maintained and constrained hallucination" and says prediction is impossible "without imagination" (`by/2025--printmag...md`; `by/2025--antikythera-what-is-intelligence-bratton-conversation.md`).
- **"the perennial promise that they are always just five years away."** He says the capability is already here and the goalposts shift.
- **The tech-cynicism section, and AI's energy appetite and "social permission".** He concedes the energy concern but answers it with supply: "I see no reason to believe that the demand for A.I. is somehow going to saturate" (`by/2026--freakonomics-should-ai-move-to-space.md`, 2026).
- **Model collapse, and keeping "verified human thought" separate.** His view that AI and non-AI content will blur undercuts provenance-separation schemes.
- **Cognitive atrophy.** He has no recent direct statement. His frame is that de-skilling is what collective intelligence always does: urban people are "de-skilled to the point where they can't survive on their own, any more than one of the cells in your body could" (`by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`). He would treat atrophy as a trade-off, not a crisis.

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Opening remarks (his "harvest"):**
- Reframe the crisis as an ecological or evolutionary transition, with intelligence "plural, social," and knowledge institutions as the "cultural ratchet" that now runs partly in silicon.
- Say that the right response is institutional design: "agent institutions", "institutional alignment", checks and balances in which "power must check power."
- Say that precision of vocabulary cuts both ways. Mind-talk should not be banned any more than it should be inflated.
- Say that inequality and the distribution of gains, not machine "dominance", are the core risk.

**Session 5 interventions he would back:**
1. **Protocols or "institutional templates" for mixed human–AI knowledge work.** Role-based review, auditing agents and structured disagreement, extending his "society of thought" and "constitutional" auditor-AI idea (`by/2026--agentic-ai...md`).
2. **Open, shared infrastructure as a counterweight to concentration.** He points to open-access publishing of his own book and to federated or decentralised training. Inference, grounded in `by/2024--people-i-mostly-admire-are-our-tools-becoming-part-of-us.md` ("the goal was very much to just publish all of these findings").
3. **Evaluation infrastructure using verifiable, contamination-free benchmarks.** He cites Formal Conjectures as a "zero-contamination" testbed (X, 2026-07-14). This could transfer to verification layers in scholarship. This is inference.

## 5. Likely dissents to be recorded

- **Any manifesto sentence saying LLMs do not "know/understand/reason", are "merely statistical", or that attributing mind is a category error.** He would refuse this and likely ask for a dissent or a softer wording. Sources: `by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`, `by/2026--unreasonable-effectiveness-pattern-matching.md`, `by/2025--bigthink-...md`.
- **A blanket "AI hype / five years away" framing, or treating AI as primarily a marketing phenomenon.** Source: `by/2024--motherjones-...md` ("It's not just some kind of party trick or hype"); `by/2023--noema-agi-is-already-here.md`.
- **Mandatory AI/human labelling as a foundational norm.** He doubts the distinction will survive (`by/2024--motherjones-...md`). The dissent would be softer, more likely a caveat.
- **Treating training on the scholarly record as straightforward theft.** He would qualify this rather than dissent outright (`by/2025--book-what-is-intelligence.md`, In-Context Learning section).
- **Caveat on confidence:** as a host he may choose to absorb rather than formally dissent. Still, his published record makes signing the concept note's "statistical model, not a mind" language unlikely.

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Benjamin Bratton, James Evans:** strong allies and co-authors (`by/2026--antikythera-the-silicon-interior.md`, `by/2026--agentic-ai-next-intelligence-explosion.md`). Expect a bloc for "plural/ecological AI" and "institutional alignment."
- **Henry Farrell:** direct intellectual opponent. The Lupyan & Agüera y Arcas paper names Farrell et al.'s "cultural technologies" framing and calls card-catalog analogies a category error (`by/2026--unreasonable-effectiveness-pattern-matching.md`). Oddly, Evans co-authored the Farrell paper, so Evans bridges the two.
- **David Krakauer:** co-host, friendly SFI "constructive critic" (book acknowledgments, `by/2025--book-what-is-life-summary.md`). They agree that life is computation, but Krakauer calls LLM intelligence "confusing being very knowledgeable with very intelligent" and dismisses "strong functionalists." Expect a live, collegial disagreement in the opening.
- **Tim O'Reilly:** blurbed his *Who Are We Now?* (`by/2023--book-who-are-we-now-summary.md`). Likely friendly.
- **MIT Press organizers (Amy Brand, Geoffrey Bilder):** they published his OA book. This suggests alignment on open publishing.
- **Adam Becker, Sherry Turkle, and critics of hype or of attributing mind:** probable friction, since his "AI abolition... sounds almost religious" line targets their camp. This is inferred from his side only.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **High confidence** on the intelligence/mind question. The corpus is large and consistent from 2022 to 2026, the book is full text, and there are several 2026 papers.
- **Medium-low** on scholarly-communication specifics (peer review, OA economics, compensation for editors and reviewers). He has almost no direct statements on these. Positions above are extrapolated from general views.
- **Nothing direct** on cognitive atrophy or model collapse in 2024–26. The "de-skilled" passage is the closest.
- He is a Google VP. His guarded "don't trust corporations" plus "regulation won't help" stance may be moderated in public. Some items (Atlantic Re:think) are Google-sponsored content, so they were weighted low.
- The Reverse Alignment Noema essay (Aug 2026) is known only via tweets and was not read.
- It is unknown whether he vetted the concept note. The tension may be deliberate, a "big tent" by organizers.
