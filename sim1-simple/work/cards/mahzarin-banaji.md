# Position card: Mahzarin R. Banaji (Harvard)

## 1. Role at summit and relevant bio

**Role:** Session 4 participant (Epistemic Authority), with Gendler (lead), Becker, Caulfield and Sloman. She is the room's empirical specialist on how judgment, human or machine, departs from what judges *say* they value.

**Bio:** Professor of Psychology at Harvard (former department chair, 2016–19). Co-creator of the Implicit Association Test and co-author with Anthony Greenwald of *Blindspot: Hidden Biases of Good People* (2013). She is External Faculty at the Santa Fe Institute (2014–), Vice-Chair of the Union of Concerned Scientists board, a member of the NAS, and former APS president. She received the 2025 BBVA Frontiers of Knowledge Award and was named to Carnegie's 2026 *Great Immigrants* list. Since early 2023 her lab has turned its methods on LLMs, calling the field "machine psychology" or "machine cognition." Recent outputs include PNAS papers on GPT-4o cognitive dissonance, a PNAS study of GPT as a "research scientist," work on bias in word embeddings and VLMs, and a July 2026 arXiv commentary, *Language Models Embody and Amplify Human Cognitive Distortions: What Is to Be Done?*

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Human judgment is systematically biased; that is her starting axiom.** "Human judgment is systematically and fundamentally prone to error… the basis of at least three Nobel Prizes." (mahzarin-banaji/by/2026--language-models-embody-amplify-human-cognitive-distortions-what-done.md, 2026)
- **LLMs amplify bias; they don't merely reflect it.** Bias in AI "(a) is covert and ironically a feature of alignment goals, (b) is not merely a mirror, but an amplifier of human bias, (c) intensifies across model generations, and (d) even transmits bias to humans." (same, 2026)
- **Alignment creates "LLM hypocrisy."** Alignment "teaches a model to state the unbiased answer while leaving the biased association intact to drive its decisions." In the Social Science Bites interview: "what the machine knows that it's learned, and now what the machine is going to say, which I'll just call LLM hypocrisy." (same, 2026; by/2026--social-science-bites-banaji-on-social-cognition.md, 2026)
- **Humans in the loop absorb the machine's bias.** In hiring experiments, people paired with a moderately biased model "mirrored its racial preferences almost exactly… and pushed back only when the bias was extreme." This "closes a vicious loop" into the next model's training data. (2026 preprint)
- **LLM evaluators are self-serving.** Models "rate their own outputs more highly than those of other models… a clear self-serving bias that can quietly corrupt the work of business, law, and science." They also "show a distinct preference for LLM- over human-generated content." (2026 preprint). Every frontier model tested shows "self-love," and does so "shamelessly" (2026 interview).
- **GPT is a mixed research tool.** It is "a flawed but rapidly improving librarian, a decent research ethicist already… but poor at predicting novel patterns." Citation errors "may be self-propagating… risking an expanding misinformation ecosystem." Paywalled model tiers give poorer researchers worse advice. (by/2024--chatgpt-research-scientist-probing-gpts-capabilities-research-librarian-research.md, 2024)
- **Her remedy is to publicly register model biases and regulate like other industries.** She calls for "independent behavioral audits built on a shared, versioned battery of validated indirect bias measures." She draws on four precedents: appliance-style incremental standards ("no reason for models to become more biased over successive generations"), pharma-style deployment limits, a "bias tax," and shared liability. Auditors should get training checkpoints, older models should be kept rather than deprecated, and data sources disclosed. (2026 preprint)
- **She blinds evaluation to cues that carry bias.** On orchestra auditions: "there are certain kinds of information that we should actually ask not to have." (tamar-gendler/av/2012--the-mind-report-interview-with-mahzarin-banaji.md, 2012)
- **Institutions exist to check our "self-love."** "Self-love would lead us to make decisions that are actually corrupting, so modern societies guard against those. We say no nepotism, we say conflict of interest." (by/2026--social-science-bites-banaji-on-social-cognition.md, 2026)
- **Scientific authority comes from methods, which are real expertise.** Methods were built "with the predominant purpose of keeping us honest." They are "independent of you the individual" and rest on "a consensus about how to go about adjudicating a claim to knowledge." And: "You are now a part of this tribe of experts. Your grandparent is not." (by/2024--romance-research-methods.md, 2024)
- **Journals that refuse LLM studies are gatekeeping badly.** Journals that won't take LLM papers without a human link are "strange, closed-minded, and a massive loss of opportunity." She is lobbying a publisher for a new journal, *Machine Cognition*. (2026 interview; by/2026--quick-insight-bias-in-the-machine.md, 2026)
- **Mode collapse is stereotyping in Lippmann's sense.** Asked to draw "a human," models return a "30-ish whitish man" 95+ times out of 100. She ties this to Lippmann's origin of "stereotype": "the printing press, the same page being repeatedly printed out." (2026 interview)
- **She is optimistic but conditional, and has hardened since 2024.** In 2024: "I'm not one for imposing lots of regulation," and "we will be okay, but only if we don't let corporate interests drive how this work unfolds" (by/2024--hbs-bigs-banaji-on-bias-and-ai-how-will-they-coexist.md). By 2026 she was questioning "the lack of decency… with which this fast-emerging technology is being pursued" (2026 preprint).

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **Pushes back on "a vast statistical model reflecting back the intentions…".** Her 2026 paper argues explicitly that the "merely mirror" view is "flawed." Models exaggerate their training data, create new biases (AI–AI preference, biases toward novel groups), and get worse across generations. She dismisses "it's in its training data" as an excuse: "we're all supposed to burn on Earth because it has read Mein Kampf?" (by/2026--quick-insight-bias-in-the-machine.md).
- **Real tension with the anti-anthropomorphism program.** She deliberately uses "machine cognition," "the mind of the machine," "kernels of selfhood," "hallucinate," and "LLM hypocrisy." She does so while denying sentience: "Despite lacking sentience, GPT-4o mimics humanlike cognitive dissonance" (by/2025--reply-cummins-et-al-gpt-reveals-cognitive-dissonance-both.md). She would defend psychological vocabulary as a *method* that gets under the hood, as it did in the system-prompt "You are ChatGPT" finding. She would not accept it being banned as marketing.
- **Complicates "human judgment… becomes the scarce, decisive ingredient" and "keeping humans meaningfully in the loop."** Her data say human judgment is biased and that humans in the loop adopt model bias. She would insist the manifesto not romanticize human judgment. What protects judgment is structure: blinding, audits, conflict-of-interest rules.
- **Strongly endorses the AI-detector and "racist linear algebra" passages, and extends them.** Her 2026 paper cites covert dialect bias: speakers of African American English are assigned lower-status jobs, convicted, sentenced to death. That is the same mechanism as detectors falsely flagging non-native English.
- **Endorses the vicious circle and model collapse, with her own evidence.** Her "vicious loop" (biased outputs → biased human decisions → training data) and "mode collapse" match the concept note's "photocopy of a photocopy."
- **Would qualify "scholars and publishers as the ultimate annotators."** "The humans with whom the models are being aligned themselves fall prey to implicit biases" (2026). Annotators need auditing too.
- **Endorses "fluency was always a signal of privilege," consistent with a career spent on status-laden cues.** For example, a young woman's "register… may not have immediately brought to mind concepts like genius" (av/2012 interview).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Session 4 harvest claims:**
1. *Credibility signals are themselves biased instruments and need auditing like any measure.* Prestige, credentials, fluency and face all trigger implicit associations. Earned authority means evaluating with those cues screened off where possible ("information we should ask not to have").
2. *AI cannot be the neutral arbiter of credibility.* LLM reviewers prefer their own outputs and AI-written text, and say one thing while doing another. Using them to triage or review scholarship builds in self-preference.
3. *Authority belongs to methods and institutions that check self-love,* not to individuals, and not to fluency: conflict-of-interest rules, preregistration-style discipline, adversarial auditing.
4. *Keeping humans in the loop is not a safeguard by itself.* People take on the biases of the systems they work with.

**Session 5 interventions she would push:**
- A **public, versioned registry and audit battery** for bias in LLMs used in scholarly workflows (screening, review, citation, summarization), with a "no worse than the previous generation" standard and auditor access to checkpoints and retired models. This comes directly from her 2026 preprint.
- **Blinded, structured evaluation** in AI-assisted peer review and hiring: strip identity and prestige cues before either human or model judgment.
- **Venues for machine-cognition science**: a *Machine Cognition* journal, and funding for replication and shared testbeds. The last two come directly from the 2026 preprint.

## 5. Likely dissents for the record

- **Against any line treating human judgment as a clean gold standard** or human oversight as a sufficient fix (by/2026--language-models-embody-amplify-human-cognitive-distortions-what-done.md). High likelihood.
- **Against "LLMs merely mirror their training data"** (same; by/2026--quick-insight-bias-in-the-machine.md). High likelihood.
- **Possibly against a vocabulary rule that forbids mental-state language in *scientific* study of models** (by/2025--kernels-selfhood-gpt-4o-shows-humanlike-patterns-cognitive-dissonance.md). Medium likelihood. She would accept a caution against marketing anthropomorphism.

## 6. Tensions and alliances

- **Tamar Gendler: close alliance.** Gendler's 2012 interview of Banaji (tamar-gendler/av/2012--the-mind-report-interview-with-mahzarin-banaji.md) shows shared commitments (implicit/explicit ≈ alief/belief, blinding, counter-stereotypic exposure). Gendler pressed her on whether media-driven attitude change is propaganda. Expect Gendler to ask whether Banaji's audits target the "horses" or only the "rider."
- **Steven Sloman: complementary dual-process accounts, with a possible productive disagreement.** He calls LLMs "great models of intuition" lacking deliberation. She sees an alignment-imposed "explicit" layer masking implicit associations. Both would say a model's self-reports are not evidence about its behavior.
- **David Krakauer and SFI (organizer):** she is SFI External Faculty, praised SFI's "algorithmic justice group" (by/2024--hbs-bigs-banaji-on-bias-and-ai-how-will-they-coexist.md), and cites Mitchell & Krakauer on the understanding debate (Kernels of selfhood, ref. 9).
- **Organizers' anti-hype stance:** she shares the distrust of corporate incentives ("If it's being unfolded with one interest, money, we're in deep trouble," 2024). But she still holds "a utopian view" of what AI could do for disease, climate and inequality, "only if the moral will… exists" (2026 Quick Insight).

- **Lower confidence** on scholarly authority specifically (credentials, peer review, gatekeeping). This card infers her position from her methods essay, her blinding remarks, and the journal-gatekeeping complaint. She has no dedicated text on scholarly communication.
