# Position card: Kara Miller

## 1. Role at summit + bio

**Role:** Co-leads Session 6, "Closing Synthesis," with Kenneth Cukier: the 15–20 minute "sensemaker synthesis" of "what emerged, what remains contested, what demands action," followed by an open floor. She is the only broadcast interviewer on the program, so she is well placed to act as moderator and translator for the public within the synthesis.

**Bio:** Miller is a Boston Globe correspondent who has written "The Big Idea" column since late 2021 and contributes Globe Magazine features. She created and hosted *Innovation Hub* (GBH/PRI, 2011–2021), a weekly hour of interviews with researchers and authors about technology, work, learning and cities; it won the 2021 Clarion Award. Since October 2025 she has hosted *It Turns Out*, an independent, Sloan-funded podcast and YouTube show "about research that changes the way you think," with a Substack newsletter. It was named an Apple "New and Notable" podcast. She has a Yale BA and a Tufts English PhD (2008), has taught at UMass and Babson, and advises the Lemelson Foundation. Her beats are higher education and its economics, the professions and the labour market, Boston's innovation economy and biotech, federal science funding, and children, schools and screens. She rarely writes first-person opinion about AI. Her method is to put a researcher's findings in front of the public, often with a counter-voice, and ask what policy should follow.

## 2. Core recent positions

- **Scepticism of the AI boom and bubble.** She promoted a December 2025 episode with Gary Marcus, "who worries that we've spent 3 years building a booming stock market based mostly on AI. Has Sam Altman sold us a fantasy?" (`kara-miller/social/x-timeline.jsonl`, 2025-12-12). Show notes: "society's all-in wager on large language models could be far riskier than we realize" (`kara-miller/it-turns-out-episode-index.tsv`, 2025-12-12).
- **Attention and deep reading are being eroded, and she includes herself.** "Focusing when your phone is around is hard. It's hard to read long, complicated things. It was hard to focus long enough to write this piece." (`kara-miller/by/2024--globe-please-stop-doing-this-why-we-need-to-finally-ban-phones-fro.md`, 2024)
- **When the evidence is strong, act.** A school phone ban "should be the least controversial, least expensive, least political reform in years." "America is late to the party." (same file, 2024). "All the evidence (cognitive, emotional) suggests we shouldn't." (x-timeline, 2022-09-06)
- **Regulation catches up with technology over decades.** She relays approvingly the Model T analogy: "It took us decades to figure out to be safe — or safer — on the road." Apps are designed "by people who know brain science better than the teachers." (phones piece, 2024, quoting Carl Marci)
- **The gap between research and policy is the problem.** On traffic: "we've had the answer... at least since the 1970s... Scholars have tried to convince politicians... But both Republicans and Democrats have (mostly) been uninterested." (`kara-miller/by/2026--substack-everyone-knows-it-s-a-problem-now-what.md`, 2026)
- **Popular research can be wrong, and debunking is a public service.** On Blue Zones: "Now, the question is: Was the whole premise bunk?" (`kara-miller/by/2026--substack-longevity-research-may-just-be-wrong.md`, 2026). The show notes describe "pension fraud and missing records" behind centenarian claims (episode index, 2026-06-12).
- **Public funding of basic science is fragile infrastructure.** "Government support remains critical to basic science — research aimed at understanding rather than commercializing. That makes it unlikely that corporations will step in to fill the gap." (`kara-miller/by/2025--globe-todays-basic-science-is-tomorrows-game-changing-invention-tr.md`, 2025)
- **Knowledge workers' labour is precarious and the pipeline is breaking.** On the academic job market: "The problem is there are no jobs" (quoting Maren Wood; `kara-miller/by/2024--globe-there-are-no-jobs-phd-graduates-struggle-to-build-careers-in.md`, 2024). She also reports fears of "an unemployable generation" as AI arrives just as mentorship collapses (`kara-miller/by/2025--globe-an-unemployable-generation-is-mentorship-for-young-workers-c.md`, 2025).
- **Who builds AI shapes it.** Her Globe Magazine cover feature carried the dek "the algorithms that power AI reflect those who create them" (`kara-miller/by/2023--globe-where-are-the-women-in-tech-and-ai.md`, 2023).
- **Human inventiveness is what AI cannot replace.** "As the rise of AI has shown, memorization has its limits. But inventiveness and initiative have not yet been programmed." (`kara-miller/by/2023--globe-could-helicopter-parenting-jeopardize-kids-success-and-fuel.md`, 2023)
- **Institutions resist reform.** Through Brian Rosenberg: higher ed "embraces the status quo" and "requires so much consensus that almost no consequential changes ever happen." (`kara-miller/by/2024--globe-colleges-have-become-ungovernable-but-not-for-the-reasons-yo.md`, 2024)
- **Scepticism of tech-billionaire futurism.** She featured Adam Becker's argument that we've "glorified computer science, at the expense of other disciplines. Which can lead people to believe the world is much simpler than it really is." (`kara-miller/by/2026--substack-why-billionaires-want-us-to-live-in-space.md`, 2026)
- **An older worry about infotainment.** Online learning must "put on a show that wins the war of attention"; "The seminal question is whether anything will be lost." (`kara-miller/by/2012--atlantic-most-important-challenge-for-colleges-isnt-price-its-attention.md`, 2012)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

- **"So we don't come to AI with open arms... every sweeping promise as one more 'revolution'":** **She would endorse this** (the Marcus episode, "AI mania"). Her tone is measured rather than cynical. In 2024 she let both Welsh ("massive shift") and Bell ("Would I want to trust that it was correct? Definitely not") make their case (`by/2024--globe-so-you-think-coding...`).
- **"Cognitive atrophy... close reading, sustained reasoning":** **She would strongly endorse this.** It is her most developed theme (phones 2022, 2024), backed by evidence and her own experience.
- **"Some of those pressures are more than half a century old" / "perverse incentives inside our own institutions":** **She would endorse this.** Her higher-ed reporting (Rosenberg; adjunctification; PhD glut) is exactly this "already broken" layer.
- **"Governments... stripping power from watchdogs... controlling the very institutions that produce and safeguard knowledge":** **She would endorse and add examples.** She reported that NIH and NSF grant terminations and funding threats mean "the destruction of the scientific enterprise" (quoting Bawendi, 2025) and that research may shift to China ("China Shock 2.0," Autor, 2026).
- **"Who pays to sustain the labor behind them":** **She would endorse this** through the lens of the labour market: adjuncts, postdocs, the mentorship pipeline.
- **"Its appetite for energy is vast":** **She would endorse this.** "AI's appetite for power is huge - and growing" (x-timeline, 2024-01-05).
- **The historical arc / fluency as a privilege signal:** nothing in the corpus. Her relay of the Model T analogy suggests she would accept the idea that institutions arrive slowly and late.
- **"Not another cautious consensus report":** **She would likely endorse this.** Her signature question is "Now what?", and she criticizes consensus-bound institutions (Rosenberg).

## 4. Predicted contributions

**Framing of the closing synthesis (predicted):** She is likely to be the **public-facing, practical half**. She would restate the day in plain language for a general reader, name concrete stakes for students, parents, early-career researchers and taxpayers, and push each "contested" item toward "who does what next." Expect:
- A **"we already know X; why don't we act?"** frame, borrowed from her traffic and phones pieces, applied to reading and attention, research integrity and funding.
- **Hype-versus-evidence** language: what the research actually shows, what is "bunk," and where AI claims outrun the data.
- **Human stakes and anecdote:** a teacher, a postdoc, a young worker. She is likely to end with open questions, as her pieces do ("But the question remains...").

**Likely Session 5 interventions:**
1. **Protect deep reading and attention in education as a baseline for epistemic health**, for example device-free norms and study of how AI tools affect learning (the phones evidence).
2. **Defend public funding for basic research and its people**: early-career researchers, mentorship and apprenticeship pipelines threatened by both funding cuts and AI replacing entry-level work.
3. **Translate research into public and policy uptake**, for example public-interest research journalism in the model of *It Turns Out*, and plain-language summaries that make evidence travel.
4. **For "who isn't in the room": teachers, students and young workers; women building AI.** (Inferred from her beats.)

## 5. Likely dissents to record

**None likely.** She is a synthesizer who covers disagreement rather than taking sides. If anything she would resist manifesto language that is too abstract or has no implementer.

## 8. Confidence and gaps

- **Medium-high confidence** on her themes (attention, research-to-policy, science funding, higher-ed dysfunction, hype scepticism) and on her synthesizing style.
- **Low confidence** on specific views about AI and knowledge (LLMs in scholarship, peer review, copyright and training, model collapse). The corpus has almost no first-person opinion on these. Most AI content is reported or voiced by guests.
- **Gaps:** no transcripts for 678 Innovation Hub or 22 It Turns Out episodes (including the Marcus AI-bubble episode). Her pre-2013 Globe op-eds and HuffPost/National Journal/IHT work are not collected. There is no evidence she has written on scholarly publishing.
