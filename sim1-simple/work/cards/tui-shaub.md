# Tui Shaub (also published as Tui Cord / Tui Shaub Cord): position card

## 1. Role at summit and bio

**Role:** Organizer. Co-leads **Session 5, "Interventions and Futures,"** with Geoffrey Bilder, Anil Dash, Brent Hecht and Ivan Oransky. The session has three questions: (1) the three most important interventions, (2) the open questions we can't yet answer, (3) who isn't in the room and needs to be next time. She is listed as "BaywoodHaus (formerly of Omidyar Network)."

**Bio as relevant:** She is an editor and philanthropy strategist. Most recently she was Principal, Strategic Engagement and Research, in the Office of the CEO at **Omidyar Network**. Her staff page described the role as exploring "the second and third order effects of technologies like generative AI on social systems and epistemic infrastructures" and leading "sense-making efforts" (quoted in `tui-shaub/INDEX.md`, from a Wayback capture of 2024-12-14). She left around early 2025. Before that she was Associate Director of the **Transformations of the Human (ToftH)** program at the Berggruen Institute (with Tobias Rees; the program had researchers embedded at Google and Facebook). She was also Associate Editor at *Noema* (Berggruen's magazine, which later published several Agüera y Arcas essays) and Articles Editor at *World Affairs Journal*. Earlier she was a co-founder of Future Brilliance US (work-skills training in post-conflict areas, including a Digital Literacy Initiative for Afghan students). In 2019 she co-presented a talk on AI **explainability** "as an epistemic interface between humans and AI systems" (no recording; `tui-shaub/video.md`). Her current venture, BaywoodHaus, is an invitation-only "Salon · Sparring · Refuge — Where the flipchart meets the forest." That suggests convening and facilitation work, but the link is **low-medium confidence** (`tui-shaub/INDEX.md`).

**Corpus reality:** Six items, all 2015–2021. Only two are first-person: an about.me profile and one LARB essay. The rest are Berggruen program-news posts and an interview she hosted. **Nothing from her Omidyar years (2022–25), the period most relevant to the summit, is in the corpus.** Most of this card is inference and is labelled as such.

## 2. Core positions (thin; most are older and indirect)

1. **She is interested in how technology unsettles inherited categories of "the human."** She described ToftH as a place "where we challenge the firm, discrete ontologies that separate nature and the human." (`tui-shaub/av/2020--alta-charo-whats-next-for-genome-editing-governance.md`, 2020, her own words as host)
2. **She wants governance of fast technologies to avoid both relativism and over-restriction.** Summarising Charo approvingly: "navigate moral, ethical, and legal questions without falling prey to relativism or hyper-vigilance in the form of excessive restrictions." (`tui-shaub/by/2020--alta-charo-genome-editing-governance.md`, 2020)
3. **She is sceptical of prohibition-first regulation, at least as a provocation.** Her question to Charo: "would it be going too far to say that regulation is actually much more of an interference, philosophically speaking, with nature than gene editing?" (`tui-shaub/av/2020--alta-charo-whats-next-for-genome-editing-governance.md`, 2020). This was an interviewer's provocation, not a settled view.
4. **Mediated, screen-based experience loses something epistemically real: serendipity, error, embodied detail.** "In mediated viewing, as we observe in pixels, the corrosion of immediacy and detail is complete." (`tui-shaub/by/2020--everyday-worlding.md`, 2020)
5. **The design challenge is to build chance and error into technology.** "Can we find new artistic forms that can introduce error and serendipity into the technologies that otherwise allow us to access the world from the seclusion of home?" (same file, 2020)
6. **She values the unintended and the unscripted as sources of insight.** She hopes for "a space, online or in the ether, where the incidental meets the deliberate." (same file, 2020)
7. **She favours cross-silo translation among art, tech and policy.** She reported approvingly on a search for "a 'universal language' connecting the three silos by creating a common understanding of our digital world." (`tui-shaub/by/2020--tofth-winter-recap.md`, 2020; program news, not personal argument)
8. **Her institution-building instinct is to train hybrid people who can "invent concepts."** The ToftH curriculum she announced would teach students "how to invent concepts that bring these new, technically enabled realities into view." (`tui-shaub/by/2021--tofth-bay-area-pilot.md`, 2021; program copy)
9. **Her current self-description is "Generative AI, Sensemaking."** (`tui-shaub/by/2015--aboutme-self-profile.md`, header field updated after 2015)

## 3. Relation to the concept note

**Inferred influence (flagged as inference):**
- The concept note's phrases "map the emerging knowledge ecology," "sense-making" style diagnosis, and the insistence on "resisting two easy temptations: the complacency... and the techno-doom" echo both her Omidyar brief ("second and third order effects... on epistemic infrastructures," "sense-making") and her 2020 framing of Charo ("without falling prey to relativism or hyper-vigilance"). The *format* also looks like a professional convener's design: provocation, roundtable, 5-minute harvest, unconference breakouts, a "sensemaker synthesis," and "who isn't in this room." That matches BaywoodHaus's "flipchart meets the forest" positioning. None of this is proven by the corpus.
- The concept note's funder-facing questions ("who pays to sustain the labor," "new models of stewardship and fair compensation") fit an ex-philanthropy strategist's lens. This is inference.

**Likely endorsement:**
- **"This Summit is also intended to be a design opportunity... invent the mechanisms, standards, and institutions"** fits the ToftH "invent concepts" / "technologies as philosophical interventions" ethos (`by/2021--tofth-bay-area-pilot.md`).
- **Cognitive atrophy and homogenisation**, loosely: her "Everyday Worlding" worry about mediated experience flattening serendipity and sensory detail is a cousin of these concerns (`by/2020--everyday-worlding.md`).

**Possible soft pushback (inference):**
- **The deflationary "statistical model, not a mind" language and the human/machine binary.** Her ToftH background ("challenge the firm, discrete ontologies that separate nature and the human") and her long association with Rees, Bratton's Berggruen circle and Agüera y Arcas's Noema essays suggest more sympathy for the posthumanist framing than the concept note shows. If so, she may be a mediator between Agüera y Arcas and the note's deflationism, not a partisan.
- **The note's strongly cynical, anti-industry tone (the "Bond villains" passage).** Her career has been in industry-adjacent philanthropy and think tanks, with ToftH embedded at Google and Facebook and funded by Reid Hoffman (`by/2021--tofth-bay-area-pilot.md`). She would likely favour constructive engagement with companies over denunciation. This is inference.

## 4. Predicted contributions (Session 5)

As co-lead, her main role is probably **process**: driving the room toward exactly three interventions, a list of open questions, and a "who's missing" list. Predicted content pushes:
- **"Who isn't in the room":** funders and philanthropy (to answer "who pays"); Global South and non-native-English scholars (tying the fluency and "AI detector" passages to her Future Brilliance work in Afghanistan and India); artists and humanists (her ToftH stance that "art-thinking" belongs in technology development, `by/2020--tofth-winter-recap.md`); possibly policymakers. This is inference.
- **Second-order effects as a standing diagnostic,** i.e. commissioning ongoing "sense-making" on generative AI's downstream effects on epistemic infrastructure, not a one-off report. This is inference from her Omidyar role.
- **Interventions she would likely back:** (1) a durable convening or coalition with a funder table behind it, meaning philanthropy-backed stewardship of scholarly infrastructure and of the labor behind it; (2) explainability or transparency as an "epistemic interface" requirement for AI tools used in knowledge work (grounded only in her 2019 talk title, `video.md`); (3) design for serendipity and human encounter in AI-mediated discovery, against homogenised, frictionless answer engines (from `by/2020--everyday-worlding.md`; speculative transfer).
