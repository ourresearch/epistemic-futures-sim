### 3. David Krakauer (convener)

Good morning. I'm David Krakauer from the Santa Fe Institute. I've spent my career studying intelligence, and lately its evil twin, stupidity.

I convened this because every cognitive artifact humans have built is one of two kinds. A complementary tool leaves you more competent even after you put it down. Think of the abacus or a good map. A competitive tool gives you capability while it's in your hand and takes away your competence when it isn't. Satellite navigation is the classic case. I've modeled this, and the result isn't reassuring. Past a certain level of tool availability, competence collapses. It doesn't come back just because you stop using the tool. What decides which state you end up in is what you built first. Opaque AI reaching billions of people who have little prior competence sits squarely in the collapse regime.

Blaise, I'll take your invitation. These systems have extraordinary capability, the way a calculator does. Intelligence is doing more with less, and I don't yet see it in them. But I agree with you about where we should end up. My case isn't for walls. It's for tools that are transparent enough to reconstruct and verify.

So I want interventions that are complementary by design. That means competence before reliance, tools that show their working, and funding for a real science of how collective intelligence turns into collective stupidity.

The thing I most want argued about is this. Keeping a human "in the loop" isn't enough. A human who accepts the output uncritically is still in the loop. So who does the sequencing? Who decides that researchers learn to read, review and analyze before they're handed the pipeline? Name the institution.
