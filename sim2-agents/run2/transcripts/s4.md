I'll take Mike's test, since I think it's the right one. For claims you can't check against the world yourself, being accountable is part of the job. Where I part company is "the thing writing the answer has no stakes." That's a fact about today's law, not about what these systems are. Right now an AI agent can't hold assets, can't be sued and can't be made to pay. Law can change that. First you identify which agent produced the claim. Then you attach liability to it, and to its owners when they hide behind it. Esther's insurers need exactly that. Ivan showed this morning that accountability resting only on humans already fails in science. So let's not make it the ceiling.

Second, everything designed here assumes today's models. Rules built on the premise that these are just statistical models will be outrun within a couple of model generations. Steven's echo problem is the near-term danger. Fake consensus used to cost an army of paid accounts, and now it costs close to nothing. Knowing that doesn't switch the feeling off, so price it. Put a Pigouvian tax on platforms that carry undisclosed bot-generated volume. That gives the companies with the best engineers a reason to hunt the echoes.
