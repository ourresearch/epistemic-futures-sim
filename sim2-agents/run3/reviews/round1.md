### David Krakauer — edit

Target: Transparency, not frequency of use, is the key variable, and opaque tools erode competence even in experts.
Two variables decide which state a person ends up in: prior learning, and the tool's transparency. Transparency means how much of the tool's working the user can rebuild in their own head, not how well the tool explains itself. Opaque tools erode competence even in experts. A third variable is set by institutions: the cost of acquiring competence. Above a critical cost the competent state stops existing at any level of tool availability, so defunding apprenticeship is itself a cause of collapse. Recovery takes far more than abstaining. So far each variable has been measured only partly, mostly in correlational or short-horizon studies of pilots, navigators and abacus users, not AI users.

<sub>As drafted, the sentence gets my model wrong: it drops prior learning and acquisition cost, and it lets 'transparency' be read as 'explanation', which Hecht's overreliance finding warns against. The replacement ties the model to principle 1's funding of apprenticeship and says plainly that the evidence is still partial and correlational.</sub>

### Peter Salib — dissent

I agree with most of this manifesto, but I cannot sign principles 8 and 9. "Open for reading is not automatically open for training" is a consent rule, and a consent rule is copyright expansion under another name.
