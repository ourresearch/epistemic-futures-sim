# Harvest — Unconference room 3: Cognitive atrophy: real phenomenon or not?

1. **Knowing does not change habits; changing the situation does.** Gendler (the G. I. Joe Fallacy), Miller (college students who all admitted an unhealthy phone relationship without it helping) and Turkle (device-free "sacred spaces") agreed on this. Dyson argued that "theory of business mind" is a questioning habit built young, not a warning label; Gendler held that it is still an awareness strategy, and this was left unresolved.

2. **Fixes need to be collective and hard to reverse, because individual willpower is easily undone.** Gendler warned that situational fixes can be reversed, as when Frog and Toad fetched the ladder. Miller added that phones are a "collective trap," so shared rules such as a statewide school ban hold where individual willpower fails, and Turkle agreed.

3. **Atrophy is real, recovery is harder than abstaining, and the order of learning matters.** Krakauer modeled competence as bistable: it collapses above a threshold of tool availability and returns only when availability falls far lower, with the outcome set by prior learning and by whether the tool is transparent. His rule was "competence before reliance," but he cautioned that much of the evidence is correlational, and Gendler and Miller both said they have no AI-specific data.

4. **Learning happens between people, and nobody is paying for it.** Miller called for deliberate apprenticeship as AI takes over entry-level work. Krakauer said unfunded teaching raises the cost of acquiring competence until the competent state cannot exist. Dyson proposed that AI companies fund teachers and mentors as "Universal Basic Service."

5. **Design the machine, not only the user's environment.** Turkle called for ending AI's first-person "I," especially for children. Dyson blamed retention-driven sycophantic design and urged using AI to remove "stupid friction" to leave time for good friction; she agreed there is no one there but argued "it" doesn't fit either and a new pronoun is needed.

## Minority positions

**Tamar Gendler**
- She did not answer yes or no on whether atrophy is real. She framed the question as what people actually rehearse, since practice entrenches bad habits as readily as good ones. This cuts against the harvest's heading "Atrophy is real."
- She argued that the deeper problem is incentive. Students who outsource work think they are beating a credential system, not cheating themselves. So the fix is to change what the work is for.
- She disputed Dyson's claim that people are learning to think like early LLMs. In her view the brain has always followed the easiest pathway, and what is new is only that the easiest pathway now hands off reading and drafting.

**Kara Miller**
- She said her reporting on AI "points somewhere else" than atrophy of individual faculties. She explicitly moved the room's focus from what happens inside heads to institutions and the disappearance of mentoring.

**Sherry Turkle**
- She widened what can wither beyond the concept note's close reading and sustained reasoning to include empathy, solitude and tolerance of friction. She argued that AI's effect on reading is a bigger neglected issue than its effect on writing.
- She held that companion bots teach people to attach to things, without friction, vulnerability or stake, and that "the better it gets, the worse it gets." She called this the deepest form of atrophy.
- She insisted none of it is inevitable and that conversation can be remembered as well as forgotten. This sits in tension with Krakauer's claim that recovery requires far more than abstaining, a tension the room did not address.

**David Krakauer**
- He argued that the reversibility of fixes (Frog and Toad's ladder) is the smaller problem. The bigger one is that people who never built the skill have nothing to return to. This is a different emphasis from the harvest's second point.
- He said frequency of use is not the key variable, transparency is, and that opaque tools like GPS erode competence even in experts. He placed language models, used uncritically by billions with little prior competence, in the "collapse regime."
- He specified the test the evidence lacks: raise tool availability, then lower it, in the same people, and look for the hysteresis loop.

**Esther Dyson**
- She located the cause of atrophy in business models ("parasites" optimizing retention) rather than in the tool itself. This differs from Krakauer's account, which rests on the tool's transparency.
- She argued that sycophantic bots make healthy minds vulnerable, not just exploit vulnerable ones, because once people are used to no friction, any obstacle feels catastrophic.
