# Harvest — Session 6: Closing Synthesis

1. **Kenneth Cukier** and **Kara Miller** framed the day's main finding: the bottleneck is people (admins, sleuths, reviewers, entry-level workers), not data. Their action list is:
   * citations by default with retraction status, written into library and university purchase contracts now
   * pay communities, not counts
   * apprenticeship as a line item
   * name every counter and its checker, with forkability and Southern decision rights

   Kara added a funded reversibility test for atrophy, named evaluators before any pilot starts, and endowments over annual grants. **Mike Caulfield**, **Selena Deckelmann** and **Anil Dash** said the list funds counters rather than the people themselves (sleuths, Wikipedia editors, librarians) and that those people should be invited next time.

2. Several speakers said a citation or retraction flag is not yet a correction:
   * **Mike Caulfield** wants the trace to record whether the passage supports the statement and whether the statement fairly represents the state of knowledge.
   * **Adam Bly** wants it to show correction speed, source independence and thin or null evidence.
   * **Ramón Alvarado** wants every score and every checker to report its error types.
   * **Mahzarin Banaji** said models that sound neutral still act biased, so audits must test behavior with a shared, versioned battery, a "no worse than last generation" floor, and retired models kept for drift measurement.
   * **James Evans** proposed verification as its own funded, open science (only 7 of 168 ICML oral papers fully replicated), with negative knowledge elicited on purpose.
   * **Selena Deckelmann** asked that contracts require independently counted return flow to source communities.

3. Adrian's "who oversees the overseers" was extended to people in the room:
   * **Tui Shaub** said funders need standing, funded outside oversight.
   * **Adam Bly** said System's self-written benefit statement is not oversight and committed to publishing its Wellcome terms and accepting outside audit rights.
   * **Esther Dyson** said insurers must be audited too.
   * **Leslie Chan** asked that every new standard state its cost to the smallest diamond journal and who pays, and that Tui's tracker measure whether power actually moved, not just inclusion.

4. Speakers said the action list omits power, liability and building:
   * **Adam Becker**: public disclosure of all AI-vendor money behind tools, journals and auditors, with consequences; energy costs and content moderators are still missing from the ledger.
   * **Esther Dyson**: mandatory liability insurance for chatbot and agent companies, and a fee into endowments for teachers, mentors, curators and sleuths.
   * **Anil Dash**: build small, community-governed alternatives rather than audit Big AI.
   * **Benjamin Bratton**: plurality must reach the models themselves through open weights backed by Leslie's compute commons, plus funding for newly produced data.

5. Still open:
   * Kara Miller listed the unsettled questions: the first-person "I" (Sherry vs. Blaise), whether a machine can fill a terminal seat (Becker vs. Ramón and Benjamin), whether paying correctors crowds out existing norms, whether a resource fee is the right mechanism, and whether atrophy is shown or only hypothesized.
   * **Benjamin Bratton** recorded dissents: he will not sign opt-in as the default for training, or "text generation engine" as the manifesto's vocabulary.
   * **Ramón Alvarado** proposed an open, revisable glossary as a deliverable instead.
   * Kara's closing question of how to give people agency drew different answers: democratic accountability, organized labor and taxing concentrated wealth (Becker); funding the "antibodies" (Caulfield); decision rights for the people who produce and rely on knowledge (Chan); and people teaching people (Dyson).

## Minority positions

**Kenneth Cukier**
- He listed training consent as a contested frame: "open is a tactic, not a creed" versus walls shutting out the global majority. The harvest's list of open questions leaves this out, apart from Bratton's dissent.
- He set one limit on pluralism: tolerate every frame except one that denies the others.
- He asked that "not inevitable" go at the top of the manifesto, and Adam Becker endorsed this.

**Kara Miller**
- She criticized the harvests themselves. By Session 5, speakers opening with "none of your three is wrong" made open fights look closed.
- She said procurement officers were handed about thirty jobs without being invited. She asked that someone call them on Monday and draft model contract language, and that a funder in the room claim the reversibility test. No one did.

**Tui Shaub**
- She distrusts funding conditions as the room's favourite lever, precisely because it is her own argument coming back to her. She called funding conditions one tool, not the whole of governance.
- She warned the manifesto against hyper-vigilance. Not every output is suspect, and bans can do more harm than the thing banned.

**Ramón Alvarado**
- He disagreed with Kenneth's claim that humans have an innate grasp of causality that the systems lack. He said instruments often beat us, so human seats should rest on stakes, accountability and competence that can be demonstrated, not on assumed better reasoning.
- He rejected Kenneth's "I lost that vote" on vocabulary and said the vocabulary question is a deliverable.

**Mahzarin Banaji**
- She disagreed with Kenneth's "the data is us." She said models amplify bias, invent preferences that were never in the data (such as favouring machine-written text), and that newer models tested were more biased than older ones. She also asked who writes the audit battery.

**Adam Becker**
- He said the answer to agency is political, not technical. This cuts against the day's emphasis on counters, telemetry and pilots.

**Adam Bly**
- He disclosed that System moved from share-alike to non-commercial licensing despite Wellcome backing.
- He said his own definition of a "finding" was built on a Northern literature, so the schema's governing body must also govern that definition. He will contribute to that work, not lead it.

**Leslie Chan**
- She asked "in which languages, and on whose idea of authorship" a finding is defined.
- She asked for funding for communities that refuse to be collected or trained on. She noted that DOI fees already push some Latin American journals toward charging authors.

**Mike Caulfield**
- He opposed any "avoid LLMs" plank.
- He proposed teaching the reader's habit of leaving the LLM to check the LLM. He disclosed that Google funds his curriculum and said it should be graded by neither Google nor himself.

**Anil Dash**
- He said nobody can repeat twelve planks and offered a one-sentence version: the extractors pay the communities, citations carry their status and send people back, buyers make that a condition of purchase, and the people it describes govern it.
- He asked that future invitations include open source maintainers, disabled people and communities near data centers.

**Selena Deckelmann**
- She conceded that her own return-flow metric could be gamed and could harden into a rule.
- She conceded Katherine's point that under Wikimedia Enterprise the payer is also the user. She now sees endowments as the more honest backstop.

**James Evans**
- He said a model grading another model gives you judges and judged drawn from the same distribution. He warned against closed integrity products.
- He committed to building the novelty test with Southern editors choosing the cases, not from citation graphs alone.

**Note on the harvest:** Item 1 attributes a call to invite sleuths, editors and librarians next time to Mike Caulfield. In the transcript, only Anil Dash and Selena Deckelmann made that call. Caulfield argued for funding those people, not inviting them.
