# Harvest — Session 6: Closing Synthesis

1. The room converged on the synthesis's central claim: the scarce resource is the people who check, not the machines (Cukier, Miller, Turkle, Weinberger, Agüera y Arcas, Tui Shaub). Tui Shaub noted that nobody showed a fork can carry the checkers, and said the only real answer offered was apprenticeship money for mentors and fellowships, not tools.

2. Several speakers said the checking itself has to be checkable. Springer noted that only retrieval has two independent witnesses, and every later event is self-reported by the party that owes the money. Vincent called for a publicly funded, multi-funder audit layer on university servers, plus an antitrust safe harbour for checkers' guilds. Priem asked that corrections become an open, queryable record rather than being donated into vendor databases. Bilder warned that any provenance standard can become a toll, as the DOI did. Agüera y Arcas asked for a binding rule that no model family or lab grades its own output.

3. Two questions stayed unanswered. The first is Adrian's: who oversees the police? Salib's answer of courts holds only within a jurisdiction, and Miller asked that the manifesto admit the gap. The second is what replaces output counting. Bilder and Tui Shaub warned that grounding events will simply be counted instead, and Krakauer said that whatever replaces the count should not meter judgment.

4. Miller asked that claims resting on models, reporting or single audits be labelled as such. Krakauer, Sloman and Turkle each labelled their own central claim that way, and each asked for a funded test before any mandate: Krakauer for a tool-removal study of hysteresis, Sloman for a field test of explain-first designs, and Turkle for a test of removing the first-person "I." Miller asked that every item have a payer, a steward, a review date and a one-year check. Springer, Priem, Bilder, Brand and Krakauer each pledged a dated deliverable.

5. Dissents recorded on vocabulary: Salib and Agüera y Arcas oppose "just statistics" and banning verbs by fiat, though they split on keeping "AGI." Weinberger declined to remove anthropomorphic verbs and called the tools an epistemic gift as well as a risk. Bilder held that "AI" assumes the answers under debate. Turkle said the action list asks only how to integrate AI, never whether it belongs, and asked for a signed caveat keeping AI out of therapy, grief and children's companionship.

## Minority positions

**Kenneth Cukier**
- Holds that these systems are "neither artificial nor intelligent" in the usual sense, but conceded he has no clean answer to Salib's challenge to name a test current models fail.
- Proposed "meter the ordinary, fund the tail." Asked that funding come from plural funders, since a single paymaster becomes a hub, and that some of it be independent of usage.

**Kara Miller**
- Labelled her own claim: the link between mentorship collapse and youth unemployment rising from about 6 to 9.2 percent comes from her interviews, not a causal study.
- Said corporations are unlikely to fill the gap government leaves in basic science, and that public money needs several other sources beside it, a point Katherine moved her on. Said checkers need legal protection as well as pay, and disclosed she is Sloan-funded.

**Peter Salib**
- Said "pay for the checkers" is close to his commissioned-work view. Acknowledged he still owes evidence on whether fast followers buy expert data or distill from the leaders, and a design that doesn't price away volunteers' reasons for doing the work.
- Held that reasoning models pursue goals and that governance built on any other premise will be outrun. Said agent identity is a floor, not verification: "a signed event is still a relay with a name on it."
- As reported by Cukier, warned that a meter pays whoever seeded the corpus.

**Steven Sloman**
- Named the reader as missing from the synthesis. Access to knowledge makes people feel they understand without being able to use it, so a fluent, sourced answer raises confidence without raising competence.
- Declined Salib's request for a pass/fail test. Argued instead that models are superb at intuition but not at deliberation, meaning knowing how an answer was reached and building causal models, and called that an empirical claim. This is a direct disagreement with Salib.
- Labelled the explain-first findings: explaining reliably lowers confidence, the softening of views is less consistent, and durability outside the lab is unknown. Warned, citing Tamar, that an explain-first box can become a trapping.

**Alex Springer**
- Said his own publisher conformance mark is self-attested in the preview version and that the independent audit programme does not exist yet. Noted that a plain origin log on a university server works as well as a CDN log as the second witness.

**Sherry Turkle**
- Asked that the manifesto not use "partners" or "cure for loneliness." Extended her exclusion to AI companions, toys and robots for children.
- Said checkers come from readers who go to the source, citing students who can say what a Proust chapter says without having read it.

**Nick Vincent**
- Said funding fellowships gives checkers no bargaining power, because a lone researcher's price is zero, and without guilds expert evaluation defaults to gig labour. This cuts against Tui Shaub's view that apprenticeship money is the only real answer.
- Said his own proposals (commons-rent credits, attestation records, coarse payment on top of detailed records) all depend on an audit layer that does not yet exist. Asked that it charge no fee, not be funded by the labs, and have a date.

**David Weinberger**
- Proposed a stable, shared page for every AI answer, with open links, retraction status and a way back to the communities where checkers come from. Argued that a personalized answer from nowhere is one nobody can correct, and said library directors could put this into contracts tomorrow.
- Said his faith in openness protected the page but not the people. Labelled his own claim that dialogue with the tools is a real literacy as not yet shown to last.

**Jason Priem**
- Raised an open question he can't answer: if the corrections record is CC0, the money may move up to a closed answer layer. Said he and Amy Brand still disagree on this.
- Noted that when OpenAlex first opened curation, two-thirds of the corrections came in wrong, so checkers need training.

**Blaise Agüera y Arcas**
- Said he changed his mind. He once called de-skilling a normal trade-off, but Krakauer's hysteresis result convinced him it stops being one once the competence is gone everywhere.
- Asked that dissent count only when it changes the answer. Asked that regional institutions co-author role templates or be able to refuse them, citing Leslie's warning that templates written in Cambridge could become "the next DOI."
- As reported by Cukier, holds with Benjamin that these systems know or understand, and argued that the designs hold under either view, against Bilder's view that the words decide which checks get built.

**Geoffrey Bilder**
- Said nobody called any of his three proposals wrong and, citing David's warning that morning about quick agreement, said he is suspicious of that.

**Amy Brand**
- Argued for consent: ask before training and show the source when answering, because retrieval keeps the source attached and pre-training loses it. Selena, Katherine and Jason Priem disagree, and she asked that the disagreement stay on the record.
- Asked for evidence on whether AI licensing money is going only to the largest publishers before anyone sets policy, noting that open access policy that moved too fast fed consolidation. Asked that copyeditors and reviewers at small presses and societies be inside the bargaining unit.

**David Krakauer**
- Asked that the removal study run the curriculum in both orders, tool first and competence first, and cover reading, reviewing and judgment, not only arithmetic.
- Said using impact as a stand-in for judgment turns funders into mirrors of the market.

**Tui Shaub**
- Said the review date should apply to the manifesto itself, so caution doesn't harden into rules nobody revisits.
- Cited Leslie's warning that instruments turn into rankings, which makes who builds them and whose languages they count a governance question. Asked that "fund the tail" protect odd, incidental work.
