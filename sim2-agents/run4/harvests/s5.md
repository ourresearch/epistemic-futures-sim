# Harvest — Session 5: Interventions and Futures

## 1. Interventions

### The three the room weighted most

All three are Geoffrey Bilder's. No speaker called any of them wrong, and every speaker kept them. Most added amendments. Ivan Oransky said they are not equal: the first is "the only one aimed at the source," and everything else, including his own retraction checks, is "sump pumps."

**1. Stop counting outputs.**
- **Problem:** Publication stands in for productivity, so every integrity tool is "flood insurance."
- **Action:** Funders and provosts stop counting outputs, with a date attached.
- **Who acts:** Funders and provosts.

Amendments from the room:
- **Tui Shaub:** Name what gets counted instead, or the counting moves to grounding events. Name the funders and provosts.
- **Ivan Oransky:** A replacement metric may not be needed because any metric can be gamed. If something must be counted, count data sharing and correcting the record, never grounding events.
- **Leslie Chan:** Take rankings out of funding and promotion altogether. Credit curation, correction and maintenance as scholarship. Require data suppliers to disclose their owners. Act through the Global Research Council, DORA, CoARA and promotion committees.
- **James Evans:** Count and fund negative knowledge. Publish a concentration index of semantic spread.
- **Kara Miller:** Provosts make mentoring count for promotion.
- **Tamar Gendler:** Caution. Pairing a credential too tightly with learning produces the "daycare fine," and paying by grounding event does the same.
- **Brent Hecht:** This lever waits on funders. Reviewers can act without waiting (see list below).

**2. Attach conditions to any money that moves (the Ulysses pact).**
- **Problem:** Signatures and self-assessments go unchecked. Crossref's POSI self-assessment went a thousand days without an update.
- **Action:** Every recipient of a levy, licence, lab funding or public funding signs up front to open data, open source, a living will and real forkability. The pact also binds whoever runs telemetry or audit. Every role names who polices it, who pays, a review date, and an outside party who can overrule it. Self-assessments are dated, and a missed one counts as a warning.
- **Who acts:** Recipients and funders.

Amendments from the room:
- **Tui Shaub:** Give the pact itself a review date so precaution does not harden into rules nobody revisits. Funding conditions stack with regulation and are reversible.
- **Anil Dash:** "Ulysses pact" fails the "free buses" test. His plain restatement: "If your AI reads the commons, you pay to keep it alive, every answer links back, and someone not paid by the count checks it." Each piece needs a named owner and a review date.
- **Adrian Johns:** The overruling party must be an outside body with public accountability.
- **Peter Salib:** That body is a court.
- **Brent Hecht:** Self-reported telemetry must be audited by someone other than the firm.
- **Ivan Oransky:** Institutions must act on findings. Publishers respond publicly within two months. Universities publish investigation reports by default. Protect sleuths first.
- **Esther Dyson:** Add money at risk (insurance, below).
- **Kara Miller:** Set a date when the room checks whether the money arrived.
- **Leslie Chan:** Tie money to co-governance by regional infrastructures, with a right to refuse.

**3. Pay for the people, not just the pipes.**
- **Problem:** "A fork copies everything except the people doing the checking."
- **Action:** A shared insurance pool backed by funders in several jurisdictions, with non-US mirrors that actually run. Money for evaluators, sleuths and training the next generation. Nobody calls any of it free.
- **Who acts:** Funders across jurisdictions.

Amendments from the room:

- **Several funders, never one:** Kenneth Cukier, James Evans, Henry Farrell, Katherine Maher, Kara Miller, Tim O'Reilly, Peter Pomerantsev.

## 2. Open questions the room said it cannot yet answer

**Metering and money**
- Can use be metered without rewarding writing to be retrieved? (**Geoffrey Bilder**; shared by **Tim O'Reilly**, who called "meter the ordinary, fund the tail" his best hedge but unproven)
- Who funds audits in ten years? (**Brent Hecht**)
- Can community tools stay good enough, six months behind the frontier, once money gets tight? (**Anil Dash**)
- If the data is CC0, does the money move up to a closed answer layer? (**Jason Priem**)
- Can consent be enforced in practice or only on paper, and is AI licensing money concentrating among the largest publishers? Commission that evidence before setting policy. (**Amy Brand**)
- Do fast followers buy expert data or mostly distill from the leaders? (**Peter Salib**; **Nick Vincent** said evaluation leverage may not last if they distill)
- Can contracts pay checkers without crowding out volunteers? (**Peter Salib**)

**Verifying self-reports**
- Signing proves who sent a report, not that it is true or complete. Canaries, sampled audits and ratio checks are not built. (**Alex Springer**; **Nick Vincent**)

**Whether checking habits and competence last**
- Does lateral reading persist, and does clicking through turn readers into checkers? A longitudinal test is needed. (**Mike Caulfield**)
- There is no data on AI and atrophy specifically. Does a designed moment of encounter build a lasting habit? (**Tamar Gendler**)
- Does a practised habit of dialogue last outside the classroom, or only make people feel better about being charmed? (**David Weinberger**)
- Explaining reliably lowers confidence, but softening views is less consistent. Does either effect last outside the lab? (**Steven Sloman**)
- The tool-access-then-removal experiment has not been done. Do the thresholds hold for reading, reviewing and judgment? Empathy and legitimacy are unmodelled. (**David Krakauer**)
- Does removing the "I" loosen attachment? This is untested clinical judgment, and it bears on Blaise's concern about users crossing language barriers. (**Sherry Turkle**)
- Do readers who arrive through answer engines become editors, and does "anyone can fix it" work when two-thirds of outside corrections came in wrong? (**Selena Deckelmann**)
- Can a fork carry the checkers, given that their loss is hysteretic? (**Tui Shaub**)

**Novelty and evaluation**
- Can AI review recognize novelty, or does it quietly strain novel work out? (**Henry Farrell**; **Blaise Agüera y Arcas**: yes for code, data and provenance, doubtful for new ideas)
- How can evaluators be tested against long-run outcomes without the test becoming a leaderboard? (**James Evans**)

**Warrant without ground truth**
- Where no ground truth is waiting, what licenses trust or counts as sufficient warrant? (**Kenneth Cukier**; **Ramón Alvarado**)

**Trust and legitimacy**
- Will any of this win back people who distrust whoever issues the correction? A personalized answer has no shared page anyone can fix. (**Katherine Maher**; **Peter Pomerantsev**)
- Will people trust judgment they suspect was automated? (**Henry Farrell**)

**Oversight and accountability**
- Who is the outside overruling body when infrastructure crosses jurisdictions and courts can't always be trusted? (**Adrian Johns**)
- A deployer can't watch every judgment an agent makes, and liability says who pays, not whether anyone paid attention. (**Esther Dyson**)
- What makes a person in the loop a check and not a relay? In her studies people pushed back only at extreme bias. (**Mahzarin Banaji**)

**Measurement and base rates**
- Can second-order effects be measured without the streetlight effect hardening into a ranking? Who builds the instruments, and whose languages do they count? (**Leslie Chan**)
- What is the true rate of flawed papers? One in fifty is a floor, and how much AI raises it is unknown. (**Ivan Oransky**)
- Do these tools speed up science? He has seen no solid evidence and asks for independent tests by people vendors don't pay. (**Adam Becker**)
- Does a DOI-backed odds ratio make clinicians check less? System will measure this and publish either way. (**Adam Bly**)
- Does wider access widen what gets thought, or spread the median faster? (**Benjamin Bratton**)
- Her case that mentorship collapse matters more than AI rests on reporting. Youth unemployment rose from about 6 to 9.2 percent, and the causes are unseparated. (**Kara Miller**)

## 4. Minority positions

- **Ivan Oransky:** Geoffrey's three are not equal. Only the first addresses the source, and the rest are sump pumps. He doubts a replacement metric is needed, which cuts against Tui Shaub's, Leslie Chan's and James Evans's proposals for what to count instead.
- **Ivan Oransky, on Brent Hecht's reviewer lever:** Review is already unpaid and carried by a small group. Adding checks requires pay and time. This cuts against the idea that reviewers can act tomorrow without new resources.
- **Tamar Gendler:** Knowing isn't half the battle, and disclosure can backfire. Tying credentials or payment tightly to learning or grounding events shifts motivation outside the person. This cuts against outcome-counting and per-event payment.
- **Anil Dash:** "Ulysses pact" fails the "free buses" test. Outputs must be repeatable to a neighbour on first hearing. He also held that building and procuring better tools matters more than scolding users.

- **Benjamin Bratton:** Don't meter content at all. Use a public dividend in place of per-work licensing. This cuts against Tim O'Reilly's fidelity royalties, Alex Springer's licence reporting, Amy Brand's licensing template, and the access deals proposed by Katherine Maher and Selena Deckelmann.
- **Adam Becker:** Voluntary lab money is billionaire philanthropy. Checker and journalism money should come from public sources and not pass through the labs, except as an obligation under the pact. This cuts against Tim O'Reilly's lab-funded pool and firm-funded access deals. He also asks the manifesto to drop "AGI" and anthropomorphic verbs, and says he has seen no solid evidence these tools speed up science.
- **Amy Brand:** Training should be opt-in, because "open for reading isn't open for training." This is in tension with Selena Deckelmann's and Katherine Maher's position that content stays free, including for commercial reuse, and with Jason Priem's CC0 record.
- **Henry Farrell:** Organize the workers, not the rights-holders. This is in tension with bargaining units built around presses, publishers and broadcasters (Amy Brand, Katherine Maher). Brent Hecht and Nick Vincent share the guild approach.
- **Sherry Turkle:** The prior question is whether, not just how. She proposes no first person or simulated emotion in paid tools and keeping AI companions, toys and robots away from children. This is in tension with Blaise Agüera y Arcas's view that his design "holds under either view" of machine intelligence, "so let's build it."
- **Adrian Johns:** The room has spent two days designing a police force with no overseer, and choke points get repurposed. He asks that the history in the note be corrected: print was never trusted first and cheapened later, and its credibility was policed from the start.

- **Peter Pomerantsev:** Every design so far assumes good-faith contributors. A market that counts use pays coordinated seeding campaigns. This cuts against usage-based metering generally.
