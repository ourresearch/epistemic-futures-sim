# Harvest — Unconference room 3: Cognitive atrophy: real phenomenon or not?

1. **Atrophy is real, but it depends on the kind of tool and what gets practiced, not just on how much the tool is used.** David Krakauer argued that opaque tools compete with competence while transparent ones (a map, an abacus) complement it, and Tamar Gendler reframed the question as which repertoire we are practicing when the tool does the work.

2. **Order of learning matters more than repair.** Krakauer's model shows that people with the same tool can end up competent or dependent depending on which came first, and that recovery requires cutting access far below the level at which competence was lost (about 0.38 versus 0.87). Gendler tied this to habits being easy to acquire and hard to extinguish, and Kara Miller extended it to the collapse of workplace mentorship.

3. **Warnings don't work, so "competence before reliance" has to be an institution's job, not a personal virtue.** Gendler (the G. I. Joe Fallacy, situation selection, and learning framed as a credential) and Miller (students who knew and still asked "take it away from us," and the Somerville phone cabinet) made this case. Sherry Turkle, Krakauer, and Tui Shaub each endorsed it for the manifesto.

4. **Atrophy isn't only cognitive.** Turkle argued that empathy, solitude, tolerance for friction, and deep reading also waste away when AI substitutes for people and texts. Krakauer said this fits the shape of his model but that he hasn't modelled it and can't vouch for it.

5. **Unresolved: what the design lever is, and how far prohibition should go.**
   - **The design lever:** Shaub first proposed building serendipity and friction into tools. Krakauer argued the lever is transparency, and Shaub agreed. Turkle held that friction built into a tool can't substitute for another person.
   - **How far prohibition should go:** Turkle wants some places to have no AI at all. Shaub cautioned that a general "no AI here" stance can harden into restrictions nobody revisits. Gendler warned that easily reversed fixes don't last, and Miller pressed for a decision now rather than another decade of debate as happened with phones.

**Sherry Turkle**

**David Krakauer**
- He alone asserted flatly that atrophy is real, citing meta-analyses showing that cognitive skills decay about twice as fast as physical ones.
- He said the phone cabinet is necessary but cannot undo years of dependence. This cuts against Miller's presentation of the cabinet as a simple fix.
- He argued that AI sits squarely in the "collapse regime," because it is an opaque tool reaching billions of users who often have little prior competence.
- His prescription went beyond the harvest's wording. During learning, students should use only tools they could in principle do without. Opaque tools should come only long after competence is secure, and their outputs should always be reconstructed and verified. He also said programming should be taught before coding agents.
