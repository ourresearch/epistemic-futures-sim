# Harvest — Session 3: Knowledge Infrastructure and Tools: Commons, Enclosure, and Sovereignty

1. The licence is not the part carrying the weight. The scarce resource is the people who do first-order checking, and a fork copies everything except them (Tamar Gendler, Ramón Alvarado, Steven Sloman, Selena Deckelmann, Tui Shaub). David Krakauer added that losing these people is hysteretic, so reopening data later won't bring them back, and Kara Miller and Sherry Turkle traced where they come from to shrinking PhD and postdoc pipelines and to close reading, both of which they said funding must cover.

2. Most of the panel held that a noncommercial licence is a fence and that CC0 plus forkability is the check on a steward who drifts (Jason Priem, David Weinberger, Selena Deckelmann, Kenneth Cukier). Adam Bly accepted this, agreed with Anil Dash and Geoffrey Bilder that his patent blocks exit more than the NC clause, and pledged no patent claims on the findings standard. A minority held that openness is a free input for the best-capitalised and an exit only on paper for most institutions in the South (Brent Hecht, Nick Vincent, Leslie Chan, Amy Brand), and Bilder replied that forkability is insurance against the steward, not a source of bargaining power.

3. The room agreed that no single paymaster (public, philanthropic, or lab) is safe, because a sole funder becomes a hub, and that some money must be independent of usage to avoid Goodhart effects (Katherine Maher, Henry Farrell, Geoffrey Bilder, David Weinberger, Ivan Oransky). Two questions stayed open:
   - **The mechanism:** a coarse levy on revenue or dependence (Esther Dyson, Brent Hecht, Nick Vincent, Peter Pomerantsev), direct contracts with audit and correction terms (Peter Salib), or consent and payment at retrieval under short renewable deals (Amy Brand).
   - **Who holds bargaining power:** organized producers (Farrell), or, given Weinberger's warning that this drifts to rights-holders, guilds of the people doing the work (Vincent).

4. Adam Bly's proposal for a publicly funded, externally audited findings standard drew the objection that a single canonical layer correlates errors and turns echoes into apparent consensus (James Evans, Henry Farrell, Steven Sloman, Katherine Maher). Leslie Chan, with Geoffrey Bilder conceding Crossref's governance record, required that regional infrastructures such as SciELO and Redalyc be funded to co-govern it with a right to refuse parts. On audit, speakers called for:
   - retained checkpoints and no model family grading itself (Mahzarin Banaji, Blaise Agüera y Arcas, Nick Vincent);
   - public disclosure of every auditor's and dataset's funder (Adam Becker);
   - retraction checks built into the moment of citation or retrieval rather than left as a metadata flag (Ivan Oransky, Tamar Gendler, Tim O'Reilly, Alex Springer).

5. Unresolved: how to police crawlers without walls or surveillance. Honest hosts walling out AI scrapers are enclosing the research record (Anil Dash, Selena Deckelmann), and Esther Dyson proposed Know Your Agent registration. Peter Pomerantsev objected that a registry can be turned against dissidents and that open commons also face deliberate seeding by coordinated operations. Peter Salib and Alex Springer proposed pseudonymous registration unmasked only by court order, identifying the crawler and never the reader.

## Minority positions

**Adrian Johns**
- The concept note's history is wrong. Print was untrustworthy from the start, and credibility was policed continuously rather than emerging and then being debased. Bratton, Chan and Cukier agreed with this history, and the harvest does not record the challenge to the note.
- Automation moves human labour to the data layer rather than removing it. The marketplace proposals are "a police force looking for a paymaster" whose telemetry is self-reported by the party that owes the money.

**Benjamin Bratton**
- The commons worth defending is open weights and access across the US, Chinese and European stacks, because weights alone stop any single bloc from capturing the models. He warned against "The Commons" as a political fix. The harvest omits the open-weights debate.

**Ramón Alvarado**
- Retraction flags attach to records, not to weights, so a retracted finding's influence becomes untraceable once it is trained in. The findings standard should record known failure classes (wrong year, wrong journal, overstated population), audited by someone who is not self-reporting.

**Mahzarin Banaji**
- Output-only audits miss biased internal associations that alignment hides, so auditors need checkpoints from each training stage and the data sources. Retiring a model quietly removes evidence and is itself enclosure.
- She proposed a floor rule: no new model generation may be more biased than the one before.

**Adam Becker**
- Against Bratton: open weights spread the original taking of people's work rather than curing it, so openness is only as good as its inputs.
- Money for the checkers should be public and should not pass through the labs.

**Mike Caulfield**
- His own argument that credible AI tools will be won by whoever licenses the biggest library leads to trust being enclosed by the richest buyer. Citations that end at paywalls can't be checked, so public money should also keep the checked-against sources open.

**Leslie Chan**
- Exit capacity (compute, staff, maintenance) for institutions in the South has to be funded regionally before any crisis. Otherwise forkability stays closed in practice.

**Kenneth Cukier**
- Instead of one organisation, adopt open source's three roles: community projects, a nonprofit foundation holding the assets, and commercial firms on top. Pair that with a process for unforeseen problems and named, paid stewards who check back on a schedule.

**Anil Dash**
- Open weights alone aren't open under the OSI definition, which also requires the training data and process.
- Fund the Internet Archive and open research APIs with public money. After Twitter closed its API, over 60 percent of dependent research projects stalled or were abandoned.

**Selena Deckelmann**
- Paid access deals should buy audit rights and corrections that run both ways, not just money. Maher and Brand said the same, and the harvest does not record it.

**Ivan Oransky**
- The retraction flag is a floor, not a filter. The true retraction rate should be at least ten times the current 0.2 percent, and most flawed papers are never flagged.
- Tie any lab money or access deal to publisher corrections within two months and to retraction notices sent downstream. Fund the sleuths through a bug-bounty pool and a legal defence fund, with investigation reports public by default.
- The flood of papers is driven by funders and universities counting papers. Paying per retrieval swaps citation counts for grounding counts.

**Geoffrey Bilder**
- Pledges need a warrant canary: a regular, dated self-audit, with a missing one treated as a warning. Crossref went a thousand days without updating its POSI self-assessment and almost nobody noticed.
- Pool reserves in a collective insurance fund across open infrastructures, backed by funders in several jurisdictions, with non-US mirrors that actually run. Stop calling any of it cost-free.

**Esther Dyson**
- The .ORG sale shows that an exit on paper protects nothing by itself. What stopped the sale was people who noticed and fought.

**James Evans**
- The most endangered commons is negative knowledge. Commission registered reports, replication archives and dead-end notebooks, because metering won't produce them.
- Pay early-career auditors rather than senior gatekeepers, and publish a regular measure of how concentrated certification is becoming.

**Henry Farrell**
- A fork is a real exit only if nobody controls the hub it has to pass through. Funding should pay to keep odd claims in rather than filter them out.

**Tamar Gendler**
- Pay can crowd out volunteer motivation, as the Israeli daycare fine showed, so funding should support volunteer evaluators rather than replace their reasons. Hecht and Sloman agreed and directed money to tools, onboarding and public credit.

**Brent Hecht**
- "Open" is a tactic, not a value. The goal is equal access to what open knowledge makes possible, and licensing doesn't achieve that without enforcement.

**Kara Miller**
- She disagreed with Maher's caution about public money: corporations don't step in when government leaves, so public funding has to stay alongside other sources.
- Every proposal should name who pays and set a date when this room checks whether the money arrived. Shaub added naming a steward.

**Tim O'Reilly**
- Making data CC0 moves the rents up to a closed answer layer (retrieval, synthesis, citation), so policing belongs at inference.
- Against Weinberger: a rate that ignores retrieval entirely leaves nothing to check the rate-setter against, so measurement must be built even if payment is coarse.
- Walls lose because anything a human can see a bot can take. Registered agents should get APIs and MCP servers instead.

**Peter Pomerantsev**
- "Information sovereignty" is Moscow's and Beijing's cover for censorship. Frame the issue as the reader's right to receive information, which Bratton accepted.
- Make covert, coordinated operations illegal and expose them, while keeping anonymity for individuals.

**Peter Salib**
- Against Farrell, the model layer is not a natural monopoly, since fast followers catch up within months. He conceded this weakens if followers mostly distill from the leaders instead of buying data.
- Copyright shouldn't be turned into a welfare program. Money should buy future scarce work through contracts rather than levies on work that already exists.

**Alex Springer**
- The host's own server log is the only independent witness to retrieval. Everything downstream is reported by the party that owes money, and signing a report proves who sent it, not that it is true.
- Keep records granular and payouts coarse, and the standard should carry signals, never rates.

**Sherry Turkle**
- The reader moves to the answer layer along with the rents, and a reader who stays there never becomes an editor. Be wary of any fund that pays for tools that replace the reading that trains the checkers.

**Nick Vincent**
- Credits for documented provenance depend on outside audits, canaries and ratio checks that don't yet exist. So the first build item is a publicly funded audit layer on university servers, with no entry fee.

**Blaise Agüera y Arcas**
- He doubts per-item attribution because influence inside the weights can't be measured. Against Becker, originality was always recombination, so the remedy for concentration is structural, not a per-sentence royalty.
- Federated learning would let regional institutions contribute to and govern shared models without shipping their corpora out.

**Amy Brand**
- Open access mandates sped consolidation among the largest publishers, and open for reading is not the same as open for training. Commission evidence on whether AI deals are concentrating money among the largest publishers.

**Tui Shaub**
- Governance should rest on a few shared principles with mutual recognition of regional systems. Fund ongoing measurement of second-order effects, with review dates.
