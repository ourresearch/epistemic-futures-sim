# Pay for the People Who Check: A Manifesto for Sustaining Human Knowledge

AI did not start the strain on human knowledge. Counting papers, impact factors, conservative peer review and publish or perish were breaking it before, and credibility was always policed.

AI adds speed, scale and real gains, like spotting heart disease in retinal scans. Those gains hold where outcomes can test the machine. Where no ground truth waits, people must frame the questions and check the answers. Cheap, fluent text is destroying signals that worked because they were costly. Unattributed reuse is draining the commons, and the people who check are thinning out.

Nothing is inevitable, but knowing and not acting is its own failure. These principles say what to build, who pays and who can say no. They flag claims that rest on a model, on reporting or on a single audit.

## Principles

**1. The scarce resource is the people who check, not the machines.**
Checkers come from readers who go to the source and from shrinking training pipelines. Pay for checkers, not tokens: mentoring, tools, credit and legal defence for sleuths. Direct fellowships to early-career checkers in guilds with rotating officers and junior votes, since older reviewers more often suppress disruptive work. Most checking is unpaid volunteer work, like that of Wikipedia's shrinking, ageing patrollers. Direct pay can change why people volunteer, so fund onboarding, tools humans can switch off and recognition. That money supports volunteers and never replaces them. Fund verification as research. Every checking role must show it changes outcomes. No model may gate review, editorial or funding decisions until independent tests show it recognizes novel or anti-consensus claims. Publish checkers' fixes as open data. Paid checking inside a vendor's walls is funded enclosure.

**2. Stop counting outputs.**
Output counting drives the flood, and detection alone is a sump pump. About one paper in fifty meets a retraction criterion, but only about one in a thousand is retracted. Funders and provosts should stop using counts of papers, citations and retrievals in hiring, promotion and funding. Each signer names, on the reconvening dashboard, the year those counts leave its criteria. Use after that year counts as a public warning. Signing is not doing: thousands signed DORA, and few acted. Publishers taking licence, levy or AI money respond publicly to credible concerns within two months and publish the retraction or correction. Integrity money goes to sleuths' legal defence before detection tools. Any replacement will be gamed, so meter no judgment. Credit curation, correction, maintenance, data sharing, mentoring and negative results.

**3. No answers from nowhere.**
A personalized answer with no sources is one nobody can correct. Scholarly AI answers must show:
- sources that fairly represent what those in a position to know say, including null and negative results, independently audited against an open denominator: what exists compared with what was cited
- accurate citations, which alone do not pass
- open links checkable outside the model
- a visible field when evidence is thin, conflicting or seeded

Check retraction status at citation as automatically as a compiler checks syntax. Give each answer a stable, shared page that routes back to the communities that make checkers. Where independent communities disagree, show the conflict, not one smoothed verdict. Disclose link-hallucination rates at release.

**4. The checking must itself be checkable.**
We call these epistemic technologies: artifacts that perform epistemic operations on epistemic content, with no claim of intelligence. Reliance rests on a track record. Epistemic trust also needs access to the nature and source of errors, and these systems state errors as confidently as truths. A seal shows sign-off, not checking. Beyond retrieval, events are self-reported by the payer. No auditor is paid by the count, no lab grades its own models, and every funder is findable in a thirty-second lateral read. Tools publish an independently audited catalogue of failure classes across retired checkpoints. Auditors get every training-stage checkpoint and a versioned bias battery that is also applied to human checkers. No generation may test more biased than the last. A public registry with consequences and a named overseer records results on a no-fee university audit layer.

**5. Change the situation, not the warning.**
Credibility works as alief: fluent text feels credible even to experts, and disclosure misses the late-night reader. Warnings on phones did little and a cabinet worked, but that evidence comes from self-control research, not AI, so field-test first. Hide pedigree, polish and badges. Make funders, corrections and provenance quick to inspect, without penalizing people who use a model to cross a language barrier. In one hiring study, people paired with a moderately biased model copied it almost exactly, so fund replications. A human who copies the model is a relay, not a check. Paid checkers carry biases too. Strip identity cues before they judge, measure them with the models' battery and keep them independent. Lab studies show that explaining a causal mechanism reduces overconfidence. The effect does not extend to facts, and nobody knows whether it lasts. Field-test explain-first designs before any mandate, and never run them as box-ticking.

**6. Competence before reliance is an institution's job, not a personal virtue.**
In Krakauer's model, the same tool yields competence or dependence depending on which comes first. Recovery requires cutting access far below where competence was lost, and an opaque tool erodes even skilled users. Support so far is correlational. Before any mandate, fund the model's two tests across reading, reviewing and analysis: a within-subject study raising then lowering access, and one curriculum taught in both orders. Practise oversight without the tool, as pilots log hours. Practise it with the tool too, tracing each claim to a source that says it. Named actors each get a review date: officials and districts for schools and provosts for graduate training. Employers who replace untaught staff with software owe funded entry-level mentoring. The link between youth unemployment and collapsing mentorship rests on reporting, not a study.

**7. Plural origins, not just many voices.**
Repetition is not independent evidence. Coordinated campaigns, some state-backed like the Pravda network, seed corpora to fake cross-checking, and pay-per-use markets will pay them. Readers have a right to know if an operation shaped an answer. Expose operations, never individuals: anonymity is a right. More voices can mean fewer ideas. Agent runs grew lexically diverse but semantically collapsed, and null claims in abstracts dropped after ChatGPT. Independence checks ask who seeded the corpus and who staffs checking, by region, language, generation and school of thought. Commission a commons of negative knowledge and fund the tail independently of usage. Better signals won't win back those who distrust the corrector. People trust those they work with, so fund places where readers check together.

**8. If your AI reads the commons, you pay to keep the commons alive.**
Models obligately complement human knowledge, so paying for it is maintenance, not charity. Deals, levies and contracts buy identified crawlers, audit rights, two-way corrections, faithful links and event-level reporting for audit, not pricing. Training and grounding need separate controls, since robots.txt fails as consent. Grounding with linked sources stays open, and retraction flags travel with licensed content. Pay coarsely, never per retrieval, citation or work, and pay for editing, correction and review. Checkers and editors get a protected right to bargain collectively alongside small presses, SciELO and Redalyc. They bargain under an antitrust safe harbour requiring open membership, affordable exit and published terms. Use several funders, including a public dividend from a presumptive commons-rent levy. Firms can reduce the levy only with independently audited provenance. The fund is held publicly across jurisdictions, and a share decoupled from usage goes to checkers, nulls and the tail.

**9. Every rule names who polices it, who pays, who can overrule it, and when it is reviewed.**
Every role and every condition on money names a policer, a paymaster, a review date and a publicly accountable outside overruler. Assume any overruler, court or body created here can be captured. None is the only check, and designs face bad-faith stress tests before launch. The last resort is exit. That means open data, code and weights with training data, patent non-assertion, a running non-US mirror, pooled reserves and trained successors, since forks copy artifacts, not people. Makers and deployers stay liable and insured on top of audit. Registries identify crawlers, never readers. Southern and regional infrastructures co-design the audit layer, procurement tests and schemas. These include SciELO, Redalyc, AmeliCA, AJOL and networks their communities name. They hold self-chosen, budgeted voting seats, filled before any standard is adopted. No standard set here may condition visibility or funding. Regional exit, refusal and federated regional models are fundable.

## What we commit to do

- **Contract and build.** We ask first whether a tool belongs, never mandate AI and may decline without giving a reason. Alongside any vendor, we run open, community-governed tools on university servers, with open interfaces so providers stay swappable. Where no vendor passes our tests, the community tool is the default.
- **Reconvene in a year** with a named payer. Regional co-governors and early-career checkers vote as co-stewards, and sleuths are funded to attend. A dated dashboard, co-designed regionally so it never ranks, marks misses as warnings. It tracks the money, the checking, the overrulers and second-order effects. Overrulers name their own overruler and jurisdiction, and tools that could track readers name who may open them. Unanswered questions show as open.
- **Dated pledges, due before we reconvene:**
  - **OpenAttribution (Alex Springer):** free repository membership, log witnessing without a CDN, a verification design before pricing, an outside-funded audit and crawler-only registration.
  - **OpenAlex (Jason Priem):** a self-audit, a running non-US mirror, open retraction flags and corrections, and no rankings built on its data.
  - **System (Adam Bly, accountable):** written patent non-assertion, an outside extraction audit, a published clinical overreliance study and a board answer on its licence.
  - **Wikimedia:** crawler edge logs and a measure of whether AI-referred readers edit.
  - **Microsoft (Brent Hecht):** an outside audit of Copilot's citation fidelity, with binding cost-sharing.
  - **NPR (Katherine Maher):** no AI-made content, and published licences that buy audit rights, paid correctors and local-news money not tied to usage.
  - **O'Reilly:** auditable royalty logic.
  - **Nick Vincent:** a safe-harbour memo and an audit-layer specification.
  - **David Krakauer:** designs for both Principle 6 tests, stating what would refute hysteresis.
  - **Esther Dyson:** rules for liability insurance priced from the audit layer.
  - **James Evans:** a novelty test, a null-results pilot and a concentration index that never becomes a ranking.
- **Fund tests before mandates:** tool removal in both orders, explain-first designs, dropping the first-person "I", machine recognition of novelty and lateral reading. This manifesto has its own review date.

## Noted dissents

**Adam Becker:** "Voluntary payments from AI companies to checkers, journalism or infrastructure are billionaire philanthropy. Money for checkers and journalism should be public and should bypass the labs entirely. Second, the opening grants 'real gains' that the evidence doesn't support. The benefits to science are unproven. The harms are here now. These systems generate plausible text, not tracked truth."

**Peter Salib:** "Copyright is not a welfare program. I don't think levies, royalties or per-work payments on existing work should be how AI money reaches knowledge producers. Paying for future scarce work does better: commissioned contracts for checking, curation, replication and negative results. My outside overruler is a court, but only within jurisdictions whose courts can be trusted."

**Sherry Turkle:** "The principles still start from integration. Some places should have no AI at all: therapy, grief, and companionship for children, including robots in classrooms. Paid tools shouldn't speak as 'I', simulate emotion or flatter. Disclosure doesn't make inauthenticity acceptable. This manifesto should never call these systems 'partners' or a 'cure for loneliness'."

**Amy Brand:** Open for reading is not open for training. Commercial pre-training on in-copyright scholarship needs the author's opt-in, under short licences renegotiated at set points. Her other changes are in Principle 8. Selena Deckelmann, Katherine Maher and Jason Priem disagree, and the disagreement stays on record.

**Benjamin Bratton:** Don't meter content. Per-work licences and per-citation micropayments add friction and reward writing for retrieval. Treating AI as a mirror of culture invites elites curating a "correct" model, and some alarm is status defence.

**Blaise Agüera y Arcas and David Weinberger:** Both reject "just statistics" and banning verbs by fiat. Agüera y Arcas calls human-only oversight at scale theatre, since auditor independence is what matters. Weinberger calls these tools an epistemic gift as well as a risk.

**Geoffrey Bilder:** The word "AI" assumes the answers under debate. He is suspicious that nobody called any of his three proposals wrong.

**Ivan Oransky:** Only stopping output counts addresses the source. All else, his retraction checks included, is a sump pump. Metrics get gamed, so a replacement may not be needed. Reviewers asked to check more need pay and time.

**Leslie Chan:** Design from the South and invite the North in. Up-front conditions, telemetry schemas, procurement tests and attestation risk becoming "the next DOI." Take rankings out of funding and promotion altogether.

**Peter Pomerantsev:** Identity registries endanger activists who survive by anonymity. Govern around the reader's right to receive information, not "information sovereignty," which is cover for censorship.

**Adrian Johns:** Choke points get repurposed, and courts cannot always be trusted across jurisdictions, so the overseer question stays open. Print's credibility was policed from the start.

**Tamar Gendler:** "Tightly tying pay or credentials to learning or grounding events may shift motivation outside the person. That rests on an analogy to the Israeli daycare fine, not on AI data. It still counts against per-event payment, but it should be field-tested. Our brains also do the most likely thing, so the line belongs at accountability, not substrate."

**Ramón Alvarado:** Instruments often beat human judgment, so human roles in the loop need epistemic justification, not default.

**Tim O'Reilly (from a proposed edit):** Allow per-output royalties gated on verified fidelity to the source. They would be checked against the open retraction and correction record and sampled by independent auditors never paid by the count, beside a tail fund not tied to usage. This conflicts with Principle 8's ban on paying per retrieval, citation or work, which stands.
