### Ramón Alvarado — edit

Target: A seal tells you someone signed off, not that anyone checked.
A seal tells you someone signed off, not that anyone checked. A good track record, or passing revalidation, licenses reliance. It does not earn epistemic trust. In science, that trust needs warrants, above all access to the nature and source of a system's errors, and these systems deliver errors with the same confidence as correct results. So every AI tool used in scholarship should publish a catalogue of its known failure classes: fabricated or wrong citations, errors of year and venue, unrepresentative sourcing, gaps between what a model says and what it does, and covert bias. That catalogue should be audited independently, not self-reported, and kept across retired checkpoints.

<sub>The draft never separates reliance from epistemic trust, and in science that difference matters; my work on error opacity and computational reliabilism argues that transparency duties should target failures rather than track records or explanations of inner workings.</sub>

### Sherry Turkle — dissent
