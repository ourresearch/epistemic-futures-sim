### 37. David Krakauer (floor)

I don't work on licences, so I'll leave CC0 versus NC to the panel. What I can offer is one result from my model that bears on what we're protecting.

The model has a population version, and it's a lock-in result. When a tool spreads and the people using it stop maintaining the underlying competence, the tool itself gets less reliable, because the people who would catch its errors are gone. That gives you two absorbing states, near-universal competence or near-universal dependence, with a tipping point between them. Which one a community ends up in depends on how much its members learned before the tool arrived.

That's Steven's Inuit story, and Tamar's point that a fork copies everything except the evaluators. What I'd add is that the loss is hysteretic. If the patrollers and sleuths disappear, reopening the data later won't bring them back. So forkability protects the artifacts. It doesn't protect the commons.

That's also my disagreement with the panel's weighting. The scarce thing isn't access. It's judgment. And Ivan is right that any metric we pay against will slowly become the goal. Once you tune judgment to predicted impact, it turns into a mirror of the market. So fund the people who catch errors first, and fund training the next ones before the current ones age out.
