## The short version

The three runs agree on the principle set and disagree on packaging, on the lead idea, and on which three or four things get the most words. Nine themes appear in all three manifestos. The title and first principle differ every time. Filed dissents land on the same fault line in every run that filed any: whether "open to read" implies "open to train", and whether extractors owe a levy or a contract. Run 1 filed no dissents; runs 2 and 3 filed 3 and 5. The multi-agent runs also reproduce sim 1's principle set almost one for one, so the interaction changed the mechanisms and the attributions, not the beliefs.

Two process facts bear on how much to trust the differences:

- **The Session 5 harvest failed in every run.** The convener's harvest call for Session 5 (interventions, open questions, who isn't in the room) hit its 12K output cap at effort high in all three runs. Run 1 got a truncated harvest (interventions only, cut mid-sentence, no open questions, no "who isn't here", no minority pass). Runs 2 and 3 got an empty harvest. The drafter in runs 2 and 3 therefore never saw a Session 5 harvest. Session 5 content reached those manifestos only through the two Session 6 synthesis agents and the diaries. This is a confound between run 1 and runs 2–3 on top of the intended one (length enforcement).
- **"Signed as written" counts include unparseable reviews.** One review per round in runs 2–3 (run 2 round 2: Farrell; run 3 round 1: Bratton; run 3 round 2: Sloman) failed to parse and was counted as a signature. Real round-2 signatures: run 1 six, run 2 zero, run 3 two.

## What differs by seed

**The lead idea is a Session 6 artefact.** Each run's title and first principle is the line the synthesis agents (Cukier and Miller) chose to open with: Bilder's "stop counting papers" in run 1 (the one run whose drafter saw any of the Session 5 interventions), Cukier's "keep disagreement visible" line from unconference room 2 in run 2, and the Cukier + Miller "bottleneck is people" framing in run 3. The same material is present in the other runs as a middle principle. So the ensemble predicts the content well and the emphasis poorly.

**The institutional form of "who guards the guards" changes every time.** Run 1 writes a long charter for an endowment-paid checkers' body with a veto over schema categories. Run 2 makes every proposed body sign a nine-item Ulysses pact with a sunset clause. Run 3 names an independent review body that can trigger a living will. All three answer Johns's Session 3 provocation. None of the three forms is a stable prediction; the question is.

**Training consent flips.** Run 1's majority text calls for licences with easy opt-out and treats consent to train as an open split (Brand's edit adopted in part). Runs 2 and 3 make "open to read is not open to train" a principle, with author opt-in for in-copyright scholarship. That is the single largest swing between seeds, and it is where every filed dissent in runs 2 and 3 concentrates (Salib in all four rounds; Bratton, O'Reilly and Priem's agents in run 3). A prediction for the real manifesto: this is the most likely place for a noted dissent, and the majority direction is a coin flip.

**Dated, per-person commitments are a run 2 feature.** Run 2's commitments section names 23 agents, 8 of them with hard dates and keeps an "unowned" register (levy collection, validator governance, compute commons, paying for refusal, telemetry custody, DORA enforcement, classroom policy, employer mentorship). Run 3 has the "report any left unowned" instruction but fewer names. Run 1 has fewer names and more prose. Since runs 2 and 3 had no Session 5 harvest, this is not a Session 5 effect; it came from the round-1 edits (run 2's round 1 shows edits targeting the commitments list).

**What appears once.** The novelty test built from once-overlooked papers (run 3, from Evans and Farrell in Session 4). Energy, water and moderator costs (run 3 preamble). Agent liability in the agent's own name (run 1, dissent only). A statewide classroom policy lever and governors as named actors (run 2). Publishers must press-release retractions (run 1). These are the tails the charter's Monte Carlo section wanted; with three runs they are noise until the real document says otherwise.

## Likely bias of the ensemble

The expected failure direction recorded before the runs was: more consensual, more confident, less idiosyncratic than the real thing. The three runs support the first two. They agree with each other on twenty-two themes and each states them as settled policy with named mechanisms. A real drafting committee of three or four people writing in two weeks will most likely produce something shorter, vaguer on mechanism, and organised around one or two ideas rather than eleven. If the real manifesto is scored against the ensemble, expect high recall on themes and low precision on mechanisms, and expect the sim's lead idea to be wrong in at least two of three runs even if its middle is right.
