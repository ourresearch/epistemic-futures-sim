## Definitions: reliance and trust

Reliance means depending on a system in practice. Track records, grounding, per-release error rates and behavioural audits can justify reliance, and they are enough to show harm. Epistemic trust is the only kind of trust owed to an epistemic technology, and it requires warrant. Warrant includes access to the nature and source of a system's errors, not only how often it errs. An error-class field, a provenance label or a reference check supports reliance. None of them is warrant, and none may be presented as warrant. This matters most in science and in other areas where feedback is slow.

## Principles

**1. Competence before reliance, tested by removal.** Judge a tool by what its users can still do once it is taken away, not only by what they produce while they have it. Funders and institutions should start longitudinal cohort studies now, before the untrained cohort is gone. These studies should compare learners taught the skill first with learners given the tool first. They should track the gap between performance with the tool and without it as reliance grows. They should also measure the collective dependent floor: what remains once the tools are removed, for individual scholars, for checkers and for credentials. Most people never check, so defaults are designed for the user who does not verify.

**2. Unbundle deliberately, not by default.** Scholarly authority has bundled four functions: research, teaching and learning, socialization into a community of inquiry, and certification. AI puts the most strain on certification. Before any function is rebuilt on its own, ask which functions must stay together for authority to be earned rather than simply conferred. At a minimum, certification stays tied to first-order evaluation that someone can show without the tool. If certification is tied to counts instead of understanding, learning becomes something done for the meter. We will guess wrong about what is essential, so these couplings are hypotheses to test, not settled design.

**3. No trust signal is certified once and for all.** Every model, correction record or provenance badge used to vouch for knowledge is revalidated in public, repeatedly, against ground truth. Each test is scored on tail claims rather than typical cases, and failures are published. The tests are run by independently funded evaluators from different backgrounds and different model families. They are not run by the provider, or by a guild of last generation's senior reviewers. Each signal is also tested on people who check nothing, because a badge people trust on sight is faith without revalidation. The charter says openly whose law it operates under.

**4. Evaluators are tested, not presumed.** No model family grades its own work, and no single human cohort does either. Seniority is not a credential for judging novelty. Panels, human and machine, are drawn from deliberately different distributions. They are benchmarked on the tail as well as on agreement about typical work, for example on whether they recognized early work later shown to be transformative. Verification is funded as a science in its own right, with AI inside it. It is fed by a public commons of null results, failed replications and dead ends, and the checkers, not the funders, define the categories of that commons. Failed benchmarks are published. *(James Evans, who proposed this text, discloses a Google affiliation.)*

**5. Bias audits across model generations.** Any model used in scholarly screening, review, citation or summarization is audited by independent reviewers. They use a shared, versioned battery of indirect bias measures. No new generation may be more biased than the one before it. Developers keep retired models available rather than deprecating them. They also give auditors training checkpoints and disclose data sources, so that changes can be traced across generations. No system grades its own output. The auditors, human or model, are themselves audited blind and held to conflict-of-interest rules. Such audits measure behaviour; they do not by themselves explain where a bias comes from.

**6. Verifiers should not have to give up AI.** Fact-checkers, librarians, Wikipedians and Community Notes writers get funded, open verification tools. These tools return sourced material rather than answers, so that people can leave the model to check the model. Every AI research tool publishes its rate of fabricated or misattributed links with each release. Independent trials test whether AI-assisted tracing lowers the cost of refutation, compared with the checker alone and with the tool alone. The trials include people who normally check nothing. Where a tool fails that test, drop it.

**7. The open scholarly record stays open by default.** Metadata, identifiers and open-access full text are free to use under CC0 or CC BY. They are never gated behind opt-in licensing for machines, and they always carry attribution. The signals that integrity depends on stay unmetered: retraction status, corrections, single-record lookups and curation fixes. Providers may charge for bulk and heavy use, but never for the checking itself. Stewards publish their curation rules and correction logs. Mirrors held outside any single country preserve the right to fork. A ratifying body of diverse evaluators, with Global South seats, provides voice over the schema.

**8. The categories are held by the people they count.** This charter uses categories to count contributions: who counts as a checker, what counts as a contribution, and which knowledge is legible. These categories are held jointly and can be reviewed by the people they count. That includes Global South, Indigenous and non-English-language communities, who get binding say before any schema is fixed. Regional allocations of compute and funding are published openly. Declining to digitize, collect, train or deploy when governance is inadequate is a legitimate, fundable outcome. The next convening is co-convened from the South.
