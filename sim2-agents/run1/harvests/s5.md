# Harvest — Session 5: Interventions and Futures

## 1. Interventions

### The three the room weighted most

Geoff Bilder's three candidates, as Tui Shaub merged them, were explicitly endorsed as a set by Anil Dash, Brent Hecht, Blaise Agüera y Arcas, David Weinberger, David Krakauer and Katherine Maher. Most other speakers built their planks on one or more of the three. The first two drew the most support. The third drew fewer direct endorsements, but several separate planks converged on it.

**1. Stop counting papers.**
- **Problem:** Every layer designed this week sits downstream of publication as the proxy for productivity (Bilder).
- **Action:** Funders write it into grant conditions: no paper counts and no citation metrics in hiring, promotion or review. Reward data sharing and correcting the record instead (Oransky). Grant conditions also require audits, public failure records, the take-the-tool-away test, and data statements on provenance and consent (Shaub).
- **Who polices:** Funders, auditing what institutions actually do rather than what they sign. Oransky cited DORA as the warning.
- **Who pays:** Funders.
- **Exit:** Grantees can move to funders who don't count papers (Shaub).
- **Extensions:**
  - Dash and Tim O'Reilly asked that the manifesto state this first and plainly.
  - Tamar Gendler carried it into the university. Provosts, deans and promotion committees would rebuild assessment and hiring around demonstrated judgment: oral defences, reconstructing work without the tool, and a few judged pieces instead of lists. Accreditors and funders police it, and institutions pay by cutting the counting.
  - Leslie Chan warned that rankings will keep counting for us unless funders also stop using them (see list below).

**2. A funded commons for the checking, with open context and exit.** This is Shaub's merge of Bilder's second and third candidates.
- **Problem:** No meter will ever pay for the checkers, the null results or the scholarly infrastructure. Per-item "revenue from services" fails when the service is essential, as retraction data is (Bilder).
- **Action:** Diamond infrastructure. An endowment or pooled fund pays the checkers as a body, not per item. Checkers hold binding power over the categories (Shaub). Payers don't govern. Mirrors sit outside the US.
- **Insurance test:** POSI-style protections: open data, open source, patent non-assertion and a living will. The test for the affected community is whether it can see the thing, undo it and walk away (Bilder, crediting Selena Deckelmann and Katherine Maher).
- **Amendments from the floor:**
  - **Hecht:** Count the unpaid checking before funding it. He proposed a "volunteer-dependence" assessment pegged to measured labour as the basis for what labs pay in. He cited 466 hours a day of Reddit moderation, about $3.4M a year, or roughly 3% of Reddit's revenue. Oransky asked that sleuths be counted too.
  - **Maher:** Operators and payers stay out of the categories. The checkers hold them in public, contestable and undoable. Integrity signals are never charged for. The rule goes in the fund's charter.
  - **Adrian Johns:** Purpose limits and a fixed-term standing review go in the charter. The review is not staffed by whoever holds the money.
  - **Adam Becker:** Every dollar is itemized. Disclosure and lab non-governance are legal bylaws, not pledges.
  - **Mahzarin Banaji:** The guild of checkers gets blind audits and conflict-of-interest rules.
  - **Chan:** Transparent regional allocations that build SciELO, Redalyc and African diamond journals, not compliance with Northern schemas.
  - **Benjamin Bratton:** Compensation is a public dividend, pooled and weighted by data mix, not a meter on each work.
- **Who pays in:**
  - Labs, in most accounts.
  - Publishers (Oransky).
  - A presumptive commons-rent tax that firms can reduce by showing licensed or reciprocal provenance (Nick Vincent).
  - A levy on data-driven advertising, paid into a separate CPB-style fund (Peter Pomerantsev).
- **Stated conflicts:** Hecht (Microsoft) and Agüera y Arcas (Google) each said their companies would pay in and not govern.
- **Uses named for the fund:**
  - Sleuths (Oransky, O'Reilly)
  - Patrollers and newcomer mentoring (Deckelmann)
  - Paid first jobs and apprenticeships (Kara Miller)
  - Dialogue literacy (Weinberger)
  - Tool-withdrawal studies (Krakauer)
  - Readers of error logs (Ramón Alvarado)
  - Error-finders credited by name (Steven Sloman)
  - Open verification tools (Mike Caulfield)
  - Regional journals (Chan)

**3. Design against smoothness.**
- **Problem:** These systems hand out verdicts. Disagreement, null results and dead ends disappear.
- **Action:** Disagreement, null results and dead ends show up on the surface (Shaub).
- **Who polices:** Independent auditors.
- **Who pays:** The labs.
- **Related planks that converged here:**
  - Weinberger's "talk page" for contested AI answers.
  - Caulfield's "no answers from nowhere."
  - James Evans's negative-knowledge commons.
  - Adam Bly's null-result and study-dependency fields.
  - Agüera y Arcas's structured disagreement across model families.
- **Caveat:** Weinberger said he has not shown that seeing the argument makes people wiser. He asked that the plank be tested against the verdict alone.
