Weinberger and Johns landed on me hardest: measurement tooling can become surveillance, and rights-holders would hold the tools. That's the fair objection to my work. I have to be precise. Content Telemetry counts what an agent retrieved, grounded and cited. It does not count what a person read. Nothing in the schema should identify readers. I should say so before someone else does.

Salib's point about contracts closing the loop only strengthens mine. You can't enforce a contract you can't audit. O'Reilly's retrieval figures matched our Ahrefs numbers.

Scholarly publishing isn't my world. I heard Oransky on missing retractions and Bilder on provenance as context. Those are grounding signals too.

Later: licences need event-level reporting clauses, separate controls for training and grounding, and counting done by someone without a margin on it.
