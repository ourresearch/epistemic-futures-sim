# Harvest — Session 5: Interventions and Futures

## 1. Interventions

### The three the room weighted most

These are ranked by how many speakers proposed, backed or built on each one.

**1. Decouple career evaluation from publication counts, and pay the checkers from a pool.**
Proposed by Geoffrey Bilder. Ivan Oransky put it first and named a payer. Backed by Tui Shaub, Anil Dash, Brent Hecht, Tim O'Reilly, Peter Pomerantsev, Adam Bly, Amy Brand and Leslie Chan. Peter Salib, Amy Brand and Adam Bly backed Ivan's pooled bounty. Kenneth Cukier draws his revalidation checkers from the same pool.
- *Problem:* Detection just before publication is close to unwinnable, because anything trained to detect a pattern can be trained to avoid it (Geoffrey). Without decoupling, everything designed this week is whack-a-mole (Ivan). "Evaluate after publication" means nothing unless someone is paid to evaluate (Amy's point, as credited by Geoffrey).
- *Action:* Stop counting papers and citations in hiring, promotion and funding. Enforce DORA; signing it doesn't count (Ivan). Stop rankings counting in funding and policy decisions (Leslie). Pay reviewers, sleuths and correction work from a pool, not per act, and never score them on volume (Geoffrey). Ivan's version: publishers, who profit from volume, fund a pooled sleuth bounty. The pool is held by neutral infrastructure, as the retraction database is held by Crossref, and not by the publishers being audited. The pool-not-per-catch design follows Tamar's daycare example. Adam Bly's version: pay for peer review collectively from a small slice of global R&D spending.
- *Who acts:* Funders, universities, provosts and promotion committees. Publishers pay into the pool (Ivan).

**2. Competence before reliance: apprenticeship, judging before seeing the machine's answer, and audits of what people can do without the tool.**
Proposed by Kara Miller (apprenticeship), David Krakauer (sequencing plus audit), Tui Shaub (the leave-behind test, credited to David), Steven Sloman and Tamar Gendler (certification). Built on by David Weinberger (unaided work first), Blaise Agüera y Arcas (as a condition in every role), Mike Caulfield (as the test standard for curricula), Kenneth Cukier and Ramón Alvarado (judge-before-seeing), James Evans (for stewards) and Sherry Turkle (who said it depends on reading).
- *Problem:* Capability with the tool rises while competence without it falls. Nobody notices, and recovery is hysteretic (Krakauer). The automation paradox, as in Air France 447 (Sloman). Young-worker unemployment rose from about 6% in 2023 to over 9% last fall, and interns say they feel "marooned" (Kara). A fork keeps copies, not competence (Geoffrey, via Krakauer).
- *Action:*
  - Budget protected mentorship time the way compliance is budgeted (Kara).
  - Juniors do the work before the tool and judge before the machine's answer appears. During the learning period, allow only tools they could in principle do without (Krakauer).
  - Run sampled audits of capacity with the tool removed. Never score them by volume, and let the people being audited govern them (Krakauer).
  - Judge any tool, meter or infrastructure by what it leaves behind, using audits rather than bans that come first (Tui).
  - Build explain-back prompts into AI-assisted reading (Sloman).
  - Redesign assessment so a credential certifies a capacity that survives without the tool, and stop settling elite access at seventeen (Tamar).
- *Who acts:* Employers and HR heads, provosts, deans, accreditors, grant-makers and tool builders.
- *Money:* Unresolved. Krakauer draws it from the checker line Tim and Nick named. Kara is still asking who pays, by name.

**3. The labs pay for the commons they depend on, and the payment is made visible through disclosure.**
Proposed by Tim O'Reilly (disclosure standard), Nick Vincent (commons-rent tax), Anil Dash ("pay your bill"), Brent Hecht (firm-level charge), Selena Deckelmann (pay for load), Esther Dyson (water fee) and Benjamin Bratton (sovereign wealth fund). Backed or built on by Peter Pomerantsev, Peter Salib, Adam Becker, Jason Priem, James Evans, David Krakauer and Leslie Chan (who wants any levy fund governed by the people being summarized).
- *Problem:* AI's relationship with scholarly infrastructure is almost entirely extractive, and the checkers subsidize the firms (Tim).
- *Action:* Several variants were offered.
  - **Tim:** One disclosure regime in two places. First, financial filings carry commons line items: Crossref, OpenAlex, ORCID, Retraction Watch, a pooled checker fund, and apprentices. Second, citation-log telemetry records what was retrieved and what was cited, with correction status and "not checked" on every event. Auditors rotate and are never paid by the labs. Tim names the labs as payer for junior checkers and will draft the standard, governed with SciELO and Redalyc from the start.
  - **Nick:** A presumptive commons-rent tax on AI operating profits. Firms can reduce it toward zero with receipts audited by someone the labs don't pay. Apprentices, checkers and null results are each a named line.
  - **Brent:** A firm-level charge on dependence on volunteers and checkers. It lands on the firm, never on the volunteer. Labs disclose the pricing numbers they already compute (mixture weights, scaling laws), audited by someone they don't pay.
  - **Anil and Selena:** Labs pay for the load they create and for the commons they depend on.
  - **Esther:** A water fee for use of a public asset, with the money going to people (teachers, checkers, mentors) and not only to servers.
  - **Bratton:** The labs' share flows through an AI sovereign wealth fund.
- *Who acts:* Labs, securities regulators (including for private labs that avoid public reporting), legislators and tax authorities.
- *Caveats raised:* Tim would test the payment at firm level before scaling it. Adam Becker is grateful a payer was named but is not reassured (see Part 4).

### Other interventions proposed

Each entry gives the problem, then the action, then who acts.

- **Geoffrey Bilder, exit guarantees.** Problem: "Nonprofit" or "community" guarantees nothing, and .ORG was nearly lost. Action: Require all four of open code, open data, full dumps and patent non-assertion. Fund endowments and a shared insurance pool for small open infrastructure; Geoffrey called POSI's "revenue based on services, not data" naive. Make sure self-audits are actually read. Who acts: Infrastructure organizations, with funders as readers.
- **Geoffrey Bilder, redundancy outside the US.** Problem: Critical scholarly infrastructure is concentrated in the US, which Katherine's CPB point makes urgent. Action: Build redundant infrastructure outside the US. Who acts: Not named.
- **Tui Shaub, no single lever.** Problem: Governance works as an ecosystem. Action: Combine regulation, funding conditions, professional norms and insurers. Any funder condition publishes its money flows, carries a sunset term and sits next to public money. Who acts: Regulators, funders, professions, insurers.
- **Anil Dash, consent, attribution and small alternatives.** Action: Consent belongs to the people who made the thing. Attribution links through to the source, and a paywall dead-end doesn't count. Build many small, community-controlled alternatives so one vote can't wipe them out ("don't wait for Washington"). Who acts: Libraries, universities, NGOs, labs.
- **Brent Hecht, a bargained scholarly settlement.** Problem: The old bargain depended on producers not knowing their work's value and on AI not competing with them. Action: Societies, presses, reviewers and Wikipedians bargain jointly under an antitrust safe harbour, with sunset terms and accountability to members. Separate consent for training and for grounding. Make clear that fair use doesn't cover commercial training without consent. Grounded citations open to the passage and carry correction status. Who acts: Legislators and producer communities. Brent disclosed that he works at Microsoft on Copilot.
- **Ivan Oransky, sunlight.** Problem: Most of the record was never corrected, so a blank field doesn't mean clean. Action: Universities publish misconduct investigation reports by default. Retractions come within about two months. Retraction status travels into every tool, with "not checked" as its own value. Who acts: Universities, publishers, tool builders.
- **Ivan Oransky, credit for correction.** Problem: Retraction numbers are used as a weapon against science. Action: Correcting the record earns credit instead of ending careers. Who acts: Not separately named.
- **Peter Pomerantsev, the right to understand.** Problem: People can't see how their information environment is shaped, and hiding it is a form of censorship. Article 19 protects the right to receive information. Action: Create a right to understand how an AI answer was assembled. Give independent researchers access to test models. Make covert coordinated seeding of training corpora illegal, with the line drawn at deception and never at the speaker's identity or origin. Provenance flags coordination so royalties don't pay the flood. Design every body on the assumption that a hostile state will control it one day. Who acts: Legislators, the EU (following its case against X), rotating outside auditors.
- **Peter Salib, manufactured consensus.** Problem: Opinions now cost almost nothing, and ten "independent" sources can come from one operator. Action: A Pigouvian charge of a few cents per undisclosed non-human contribution, found by regulators' random sampling and paid by the platform. Thin identity for agents acting in knowledge markets, with no required identifier, no single root of trust, a decentralized design (credited to Alex) and sunset terms. Who acts: Legislators and platform regulators. Journals and funders can require it for review pipelines now.
- **Steven Sloman, making correction count.** Action: Build explain-back prompts into AI-assisted reading. Name correctors. Make trust signals count independent sources, not echoes. Who acts: Employers, provosts, journals, tool builders.
- **Alex Springer, a reporting obligation in every licence.** Problem: AI deals set payment terms with almost no telemetry, so publishers can't verify use. Action: Every deal names the events reported (retrieved, grounded, cited, engaged), fixes the schema and grants audit rights. It includes separate training and grounding controls and three-value correction status. Logs are used for payment and audit only, and no event count ranks a person. A separately funded auditor samples whether cited passages support their sentences before payout. Who acts: Deal signers (societies, presses, libraries, repositories), and funders paying for the auditor.
- **Sherry Turkle, reading.** Problem: The week focused on writing, grounding and citation. Summaries are built so nobody goes to the source, and checkers come from people who have actually read. Action: Put part of the money designed this week into libraries, teachers and protected slow-reading time. Build no click-through meter. Adopt a design standard of no first-person "I", no simulated feeling and no AI companions sold to children. Who acts: Funders, school boards, provosts, product teams.
- **Nick Vincent, evaluation guilds.** Problem: Expert evaluation work will go either to centralized gig labour (Mercor) or to guilds. Action: Societies, presses and reviewers form guilds that set standards, adjudicate quality, price correction work and bargain as a group. Wardens rotate, and nothing is scored by volume. Who acts: Funders seed the guilds, and legislators grant a safe harbour.
- **David Weinberger, dialogue as a literacy.** Problem: Schools grade essays students will rarely write, while the daily skill is arguing with a fluent machine. Action: Students hand in the AI conversation transcript alongside the work, and the teacher grades the process. Students then keep arguing in a room of people. Unaided work comes first. Who acts: Teachers, provosts, school boards, libraries.
- **Jason Priem, an open, agent-ready scholarly record.** Problem: Agents are becoming the readers, and whoever runs their layer sets the norms, as ISI showed. Action: Fund an open, CC0, agent-ready record as public infrastructure, with retraction status, "not checked", full dumps and the exit pool. Funders and labs pay, never one patron. SciELO, Redalyc and AmeliCA are co-owners. Paid, signed review sits beside it, following Amy's under-2-percent finding. Publish who downloads the snapshot. Who acts: Funders, labs.
- **Blaise Agüera y Arcas, role templates for mixed human–AI work.** Problem: When one model family fills every judging role, errors line up and independence is lost. Action: Templates for reviewer, auditor, adversary and archivist, each with four conditions: real stakes, independence in models, funders and ownership, the power to overrule, and audits of capacity without the tool. Add "not checked" and a verifiable check wherever one exists. Who acts: Journals and societies (starting with peer review), funders, public agencies.
- **Amy Brand, a press and society licensing consortium.** Problem: AI licensing is concentrating among the five biggest publishers, as open access did, and authors at smaller presses lose any say. Action: A shared consortium and deal template. Consent is opt-in, deals are renegotiated every two years, and retrieval is favoured over pre-training. Citations open to the passage with correction status. Independent sampling audits check support. RFPs score climate impact and public benefit. SciELO and Redalyc co-govern, with a sunset term and published money flows. The MIT Press will draft the terms. Who acts: Presses and societies, and legislators for the safe harbour.
- **Ramón Alvarado, error-class disclosure and a shared glossary.** Problem: Failure rates and severity can be measured but not the reasons, and a track record gets read as warrant. Action: Independent sampled audits record classes of failure: fabricated support, misrepresented source, citation to a retracted paper. Keep retired models and checkpoints as commons, and pair disclosure with judge-before-you-see workflows. The glossary separates retrieval, grounding and support, and separates "not checked", "no correction found" and "known failure class". Who acts: Auditors, journals, funders.
- **Mahzarin Banaji, a public bias registry.** Problem: Alignment hides biased associations that still drive decisions. The bias worsens across generations, and humans copy it. Action: A versioned registry built from independent behavioral audits using a shared battery of indirect measures. The standard is that no generation is more biased than the last. Auditors get checkpoints from each training stage, old models are kept, and data sources are disclosed. Humans in the loop are audited too. Auditors rotate, are paid for verifying, and have published funders. Who acts: Journals and funders, with regulators for teeth.
- **Adam Becker, funding disclosure beside provenance.** Problem: Mixing junk with good work launders credibility, and hidden money makes that possible (Inference and Thiel). Action: Every tool, dataset, audit, telemetry stream and checker pool shows who pays and how much, where readers can see it before they trust the output. Checkers are paid by several public-interest payers with published flows. Who acts: Funders, journals, legislators, securities regulators.
- **Adam Bly, finding-level provenance.** Problem: Trust attaches to documents and fluent answers rather than to findings. Action: An open standard, not owned by System. Each finding carries population, effect size, source and correction status (with "not checked"). Null results count as findings, and records show whether replications are independent. No identifier is required. Outside auditors, paid by someone other than the extractor, check support. Revive the Office of Technology Assessment. Who acts: Standards bodies and funders, with SciELO and Redalyc co-governing, and Congress for OTA.
- **Benjamin Bratton, a compute commons.** Problem: The chokepoints are chips, compute and training capacity, held by a few labs in two countries. Action: Public and lab money funds a compute commons with transparent regional allocation. SciELO, presses, societies and Wikimedia can train, fine-tune and audit open-weights models on their own corpora through federated learning. The labs' share flows through an AI sovereign wealth fund. Terms are "green lines" that widen access, not tolls. Who acts: Funders, labs, legislators.
- **Mike Caulfield, lowering the cost of refutation.** Problem: The people who keep the record honest are few and unpaid, and cheap generation widened Brandolini's gap. Action: Fund open verification tools and curricula that work as context engines rather than verdict machines. Mike has drafted a rebuild of SIFT for AI but lacks resources to test it. Test by what students can check without the tool. Who acts: Foundations, library associations and universities, with teachers co-designing. Mike disclosed that Google funded last summer's Super Searchers update.
- **Leslie Chan, funding federated governance.** Problem: The proposals pay only knowledge that can be measured, so for much of the world the choice is comply or be invisible. Action: Public funders, through the Global Research Council, commit long-term money to governance of community-run, federated infrastructure (SciELO, AmeliCA, Redalyc, Mukurtu), not only servers. Grants require portability and open interfaces. Any levy fund is governed by the people being summarized, with rotating terms. Set up a compute commons with published regional allocations. Fund refusal as a legitimate outcome. Keep rankings out of funding and policy. Who acts: Public funders and the GRC.
- **Kenneth Cukier, revalidation.** Problem: Revalidation, which Kenneth argued for in 2018, means something only if someone independent, trained and paid can still do it. Action: High-stakes uses publish a revalidation schedule against ground truth. Checkers judge before seeing the machine's answer and are paid from the pool. Results go to public authorities and auditors; whether rivals see them is marked contested. Who acts: Journals, funders, regulators.
- **Selena Deckelmann, reciprocity with teeth.** Problem: Crawlers have saturated Wikipedia's links, and pageviews, new editors, donors and patrollers are falling. Action: Human traffic comes first, and crawlers identify themselves, use APIs and pay for load. Attribution links to a page that opens, as recruitment. Affected communities govern the AI used on their work, with demographic performance data, human review before anything is final, "not checked", and a community off-switch. The dumps stay free. Who acts: Labs and product teams, and funders for the exit pool.
- **Esther Dyson, sunset and insurance.** Problem: Trusted institutions outlive their accountability, as with .ORG. Action: Every registry, fund, guild, auditor and consortium has a fixed term, publishes money flows and gets no automatic renewal. Companies offering public-facing knowledge agents must buy liability insurance and disclose premiums. Esther will push both inside the .agent bid she advises. Who acts: Legislators, securities regulators, insurers.
- **James Evans, a negative-knowledge commons.** Problem: Across 39 million papers, abstracts with no null claims rose sharply after ChatGPT, and models state nulls less often than humans. Losing nulls leads to correlated error and, in his simulations, collapse. Action: Fund elicitation of registered reports, replication archives, dead-end notebooks and expert null intuitions as public record and training data. Pay from a pool with credit attached, never per notebook. Stewards rotate. Fund verification as its own science and measure field and corpus concentration. Who acts: Funders and journals, with a named line in Tim's and Nick's funds. James disclosed that he works with Google.
- **Henry Farrell, organize before metering.** Problem: The people being summarized bargain one at a time and lose. Action: Societies, presses, libraries, Wikimedia, reviewers and evaluators bargain jointly under a safe harbour, with rotating wardens and several payers across several jurisdictions. The first thing any deal funds is the repair rate (checkers and apprentices, pooled). Separately, run and publish a blind trial of models against senior reviewers on spotting novelty. Who acts: Legislators, funders.
- **Adrian Johns, guarding the guards.** Problem: Most of the roadmap is policing, nobody guards the guards, and enforcement systems get repurposed (tobacco tracking, Microsoft's anti-piracy unit in Russia). Action: Every policing mechanism carries rotating wardens, a named payer, and a review of what else it could be used for, written before launch and repeated at each sunset. Fund the empirical study of scholarly reading. Who acts: Charters, funders, legislators.
- **Katherine Maher, a public broadcaster consortium.** Problem: Local journalism has contracted, no station can bargain alone, and a single payer can vanish in one vote. Action: US and international public broadcasters negotiate licensing and grounding jointly. Labs pay for reliable, attributed service, and nobody pays a toll to read. Citations open to the passage with correction status. No deal makes the chatbot the only place a story can be read, and anything shown to an audience stays human-made. Sunset terms and several payers apply. Who acts: Station networks, international broadcasters, philanthropy.

- **Peter Salib, thin identity for agents.** Salib is the only speaker proposing identity for agents in knowledge markets. He acknowledged that Leslie's objection lands and built in no required identifier and no single root of trust.
- **Sherry Turkle, bans before audits.** Sherry proposes a design standard of no first-person "I", no simulated feeling and no AI companions sold to children. This cuts against Tui's preference for audits over bans that come first. Sherry told Tui to test the "I" if she wants, but by what it leaves in the person, not whether they liked it. Sherry also holds that the week neglected reading for writing and noted that libraries did not make yesterday's harvest.
- **Benjamin Bratton, compensation through generative AI, not against it.** Bratton proposes federated learning so no one has to choose between withdrawing their work and handing it over, and "green lines" instead of tolls. This sits against the opt-in consent and refusal framing of Anil, Amy, Selena (the community off-switch) and Leslie (funding refusal).
- **Ramón Alvarado and Tamar Gendler, labels are not warrant.** Ramón holds that a track record, whether from telemetry, citation logs or benchmarks, is not warrant. Tamar holds that disclosure alone won't switch off trust ("knowing isn't half the battle"). Adam Bly adopted the point. It cuts against treating revalidation and "not checked" labels as sufficient on their own. Ramón pairs disclosure with judge-before-you-see, and Kenneth took up the question as unresolved in his own case.
- **Anil Dash, "don't wait for Washington."** Anil calls for building small community-controlled alternatives rather than waiting for legislation. Many other proposals depend on legislators, including safe harbours, the Pigouvian charge, the seeding ban and mandatory insurance.
- **On the manifesto's wording.** Two instructions sit in tension. Anil asked for something as concrete and repeatable as "free buses". Geoffrey asked that nothing in the manifesto be called free, because somebody maintains it.
