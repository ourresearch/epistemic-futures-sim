# Harvest — Session 6: Closing Synthesis

1. **Frame.** Kenneth Cukier's proposal to write the manifesto around roles, stakes and the power to overrule, rather than around what these systems are, was endorsed by Peter Salib, David Weinberger, Blaise Agüera y Arcas and, with a wince at "AI," Geoffrey Bilder. Agüera y Arcas recorded that he still does not sign "statistical model, not mind," and Weinberger asked that his minority view be recorded rather than dropped: machine learning shows that tangles of particulars can predict where generalizations fail.

2. **Actions.** Cukier proposed five actions:
   * stop counting papers and citations in hiring, promotion and funding
   * pay checkers from a pool, never per act or by volume
   * put competence before reliance
   * treat "not checked" as its own value, separate from "no correction found"
   * give every new body a fixed term, rotating inspectors and published money flows

   Kara Miller added protected mentorship time, budgeted like compliance. Floor additions were:
   * no payout on self-reported counts until a separately funded auditor has sampled them (Alex Springer)
   * named credit for correctors, and correction records that read like maintenance logs (Steven Sloman)
   * reading, and a firm line against AI companions sold to children (Sherry Turkle)
   * small pilots with published costs before any mandate (Amy Brand)
   * a funded within-subject study of competence loss, because his thresholds come from a model and are not measurements (David Krakauer)
   * community-read self-audits by a deadline, with a sunset if they aren't read (Bilder)
   * a charge on undisclosed bot contributions (Salib)
   * open infrastructure for the agents that now read the record (Jason Priem)
   * "free to read, paid to maintain" as manifesto wording (Tui Shaub)

3. **Durability (open).** Miller asked what survives when a legislature changes its mind. The answers offered were:
   * practised norms, several payers and people who can still check (Cukier)
   * organized producers with collective bargaining, and evaluation guilds (Nick Vincent)
   * mirrors and governance outside the US (Bilder)
   * deals renegotiated about every two years (Brand)
   * an open schema and logs held by publishers (Springer)
   * a CC0 dump, which keeps copies but not people (Priem)
   * libraries, teachers and conversation (Turkle)
   * a mutually reinforcing ecosystem of regulation, funding conditions, professional norms and insurers (Shaub)
   * what people learned before the tool arrived, with the caveat that a lost generation is not reversible (Krakauer)

   No single answer was agreed.

4. **Money and other contested points.** Apprentice funding has a named payer (the labs, per Tim) but no guarantor, and Priem, Agüera y Arcas and Krakauer said it still has no owner. Miller, Sloman, Agüera y Arcas and Shaub took up Adam's warning that whoever pays may come to own what they fund, and Krakauer called for several payers. Other points stay marked contested, per Cukier:
   * consent first versus regulating use (Anil versus Cukier)
   * who reads the logs (Tim and Springer versus Cukier, with Springer still unsettled)
   * bans versus audits (Turkle versus Shaub)
   * Ramón's question of where ground truth comes from in fields with slow feedback

5. **Standing dissents and missing voices.** Cukier asked that two dissents stand as dissents: Leslie's, that the proposals add up to "comply or be invisible," written in Northern cities, and Adrian's, on who guards the guards and what else these structures will be used for. Miller, Vincent, Priem, Brand and Shaub said that naming SciELO and Redalyc is not representation. Priem and Brand committed to bring them into governance before drafting, and Shaub asked for seats for the funders and for artists, and noted that error and serendipity were missing from the synthesis.

## Minority positions

**Kenneth Cukier**
- He still holds that the term "artificial intelligence" assumes what the room is disputing. The harvest records only Bilder's wince at "AI."
- He said the post-Gutenberg intermediary will not be a profession this time. It will be a set of structures that keep human framing in play: causality, counterfactuals and constraints.
- In his called response he said markets should carry some of the load and that regulation comes as much from culture as from government. Both points sit uneasily with Vincent's view that anything short of collective bargaining becomes patronage.
- His summary of the bans-versus-audits dispute was narrower than the harvest's. He described it as Turkle against Shaub specifically on the first-person "I."

**Kara Miller**
- She argued that waiting is not neutral, citing phones in classrooms, where other countries acted while America debated. This urgency cuts against Brand's call for small pilots before any mandate, and it was not captured.
- She warned that the tempting move is to replace untrained young workers with software. She cited young-worker unemployment rising from about 6 percent to over 9 percent in two years.
- She said funders and promotion committees were not in the room, and someone present has to go to them.

**Peter Salib**
- He said independence is also threatened by manufactured voices. You cannot count real sources unless you can price fake ones. The harvest records his bot charge but not this reasoning.
- He corrected his own record on two points. Saying the model layer is contestable is not the same as showing that buyers of human knowledge work won't concentrate, and Henry's worry remains open. He also said Nick's commons tax is closer to his position than he had admitted.

**Steven Sloman**
- He reworded Cukier's claim: expertise shows in a willingness to be corrected. He added Pomerantsev's warning that a published failure rate on its own reads as proof that nobody knows anything, which qualifies how figures like Ivan's are presented.
- He raised the knowledge illusion: fluent answers feel like understanding the reader already has. He asked that explaining back be built in wherever AI helps people read. This was not captured.

**Alex Springer**
- He said the measurement currency for payouts should not be set by anyone who takes a margin on the count, including himself.
- He committed to a three-value correction field, a review of other uses before launch, and letters to SciELO and Redalyc. The harvest credits only Priem and Brand with commitments on SciELO and Redalyc.

**Sherry Turkle**
- She argued that a summary is designed so that nobody goes to the source, and that the room spent two days on writing and citation while neglecting reading.

**Nick Vincent**
- He argued that without collective bargaining, the pool, the tax and the telemetry all become patronage that can be cut or redirected. This challenges the payment-pool approach in the action list, not just the durability question.
- He said he has not designed how money gets routed or who counts as a community.

**David Weinberger**
- He objected that the synthesis counts only damage.
- He asked that his dialogue-literacy proposal go in as a hypothesis to test, not a plank. He accepted Turkle's order of reading first, then unaided work, then dialogue.

**Jason Priem**
- He warned that the scholarly record is increasingly read by agents. He compared this to handing decision-making infrastructure to ISI, which produced the impact factor.
- He committed to publish who downloads the OpenAlex snapshot.

**Blaise Agüera y Arcas**
- He said the synthesis lists institutions without defining their parts. He proposed that the reviewer, auditor, adversary and archivist roles each meet five conditions: real stakes; independence in models, funders and ownership; the power to overrule; audits of unaided competence; and rotating wardens plus a review of other uses.
- He said no single concentration of intelligence, human or machine, should regulate itself.

**Geoffrey Bilder**
- He said "cost-free" usually means a volunteer is carrying the cost, often while burning out. As a former Crossref staffer, he noted that Crossref went a thousand days without updating its own self-assessment.

**Amy Brand**
- She said open access shows how a good goal pushed too fast ends in consolidation. She sees the same pattern in AI deals, which are clustering among the largest publishers while university presses and societies are left behind.
- She reported that more than 850 authors said they are not against AI but want consent, attribution and compensation. This bears on the consent-versus-use dispute and was not recorded there.

**David Krakauer**
- He asked that the people being audited govern the competence-loss study.
- He described apprenticeship as the hysteresis in the system.

**Tui Shaub**
- She said treating technology and chance as opposites is itself a design choice. The harvest records only that she found error and serendipity missing.
